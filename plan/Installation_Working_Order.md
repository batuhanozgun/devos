# Installation working order (rules of the working session)

**What this file is.** The rules of the working session (the executor) that runs the DevOS installation from C00 to C12. The executor is the session whose first message is the installation `/goal` of Appendix F (`plan/Ek_F_Baslangic_Mesaji.md`); any other session follows the task in its first message and does not run the work loop (section 4). Source: Batu's decision D-010 (`plan/decisions/D-010.md`; the approved summary `briefs/conversation/KARAR_OZETI_2026-10-05_TR.md`, English rendering `briefs/conversation/DECISION_SUMMARY_2026-10-05_EN.md`; "summary n" below means its item n) and the plan change PC-06. The plan (`plan/DevOS_Kurulum_Plani.md` and its appendices) is binding; this file says how the installation work runs, not what it builds. Rules for every role: `CLAUDE.md`. Role rules: `.claude/agents/`.

Summary section B, items 3–9, verbatim (all of them Batu's), each with its English rendering. The working session and every subagent follow them.

## 2. Structure

- **One working session** runs the installation and gives the work to subagents and workflows (summary 10). It reads the plan itself, takes the next step in plan order, splits the work, writes the subagents' tasks, is the only one that commits, opens pull requests and merges into `main`, opens and closes side branches, and brings Batu only his own decisions (16). Where it left off is carried by the record (`main`, the plan, the ledger), not by the session.
- **Helpers** (`.claude/agents/`; 17–19). `producer` (Üretici) writes. `researcher` (Araştırmacı) reads and does not decide. `prober` (Sınayıcı) watches the platform and runs tests written in advance. `checker` (Denetçi) is fresh eyes: it evaluates and does not fix; it also asks "have we broken away from the plan?" before a side branch opens, in its closing check (section 5), and at the end of a stage. `counter-designer` (Karşı tasarımcı) designs without seeing the plan, for C00 step 5 only; its only tool is Write, so the task text the executor writes carries everything it may see (SOUL's goal, Batu's decisions and principles, the criteria and the platform facts; plan C00 step 5). `test-designer` (Test tasarımcısı) writes a work item's acceptance condition and its tests, each with its break, before any result of that work is seen, and never the item's product or a verdict; it was written at C02 (W-C02-02), and from then on it writes the conditions and tests (section 4). The executor sets the timing. The roles run through the `Agent` tool, one call per role with the role as its type; from W-C02-24's M1a the guard denies the Workflow tool as a whole (rule TW, section 10), so a workflow is no longer a helper of the working session, and the first bullet's "subagents and workflows" (summary 10), which stays as written, is read so: workflows were a means (W-C02-24 disposition 2). This is not in force before M1a is merged and synced. These are the builder's helpers, not Appendix A's DevOS roles: at C05, when DevOS's role set is set up, they are removed or handed over, and `CLAUDE.md` gives way to DevOS's common rules (plan Appendix D).
- **The team, and the roles' own files** (PC-53). The executor does not decide technical matters alone: by D-039 (Batu's direction of 2026-10-19 at 18:18Z and 18:24Z), knowledge comes first (the research library, then the web), then the roles' evaluation in their fields, then the work; the executor integrates their answers and records a disposition where it decides across fields (section 4 step 1a). So "the builder" in this file means the team the working session leads: plan 0.3 item 11 gives technical decisions to the team, and plan 6.12 item 5 gives it the technical corrections seen on stepping outside the frame (6.12 carries the section label `[Proposal]`, `plan/DevOS_Kurulum_Plani.md:789`). Plan 0.7's term note reads "the builder" as the single working session; that tension is settled by Appendix D's D8 item 3, whose order puts Batu's decisions above the plan's notes. Summaries 16 and 20 (rendered above) and principle 5 (section 1) are read by D-039 at 18:24Z, his later direction on how his earlier text is applied: the plan does not change, and the executor no longer decides alone; they stay as written. Each role takes up, returns, sends back and hands over work by its own file in `.claude/agents/` (its take-up step, send-back and hand-over), and every role carries Appendix D section 2's common floor from `CLAUDE.md`.
- **Limit** (11). The single session holds fully up to C03. Separate sessions or environments stay where the plan requires them: the session and routines observed in C01, the audit environment from C03, C04's hidden search question set, and the exams (C05, C07, C10). A session in Accept edits cannot start sessions, so when one is needed it goes to Batu as one narrow question (the first time in C01).
- **Two layers** (20). (a) Running the plan, mechanically: sections 3 to 9. (b) Reasoning inside a step: splitting the work, choosing the role, interpreting the result, making the technical decision, noticing a gap in the plan, questioning the frame when stuck. For (b): Batu's principles (section 1), the thinking disciplines and the session opening and closing rules of plan Appendix D section 2, and Appendix G's G4, G8 and G10 (23). The plan's own principles (plan 0.3, [Batu kararı]) hold here too, among them: quality is not lowered for convenience or cost, and an unsolved problem is not presented as solved because "it will be tested later" (items 1 and 6); frame blindness is the greatest danger, so when a design hits a limit and starts producing new mechanisms as a fix, the frame is questioned first (item 11, plan 6.12). Appendices D and G were written for DevOS and are borrowed here, adapted (their records go to the ledger files until DevOS's database exists); they do not take the place of C05's setup. Appendix D's "do not trust a long session's summarization; move on by hand-over" is replaced by the single session with the re-read hook (section 3). **The thinking disciplines in practice** (D-016, Batu's direction of 2026-10-06): every agent (the executor, and every helper except the counter-designer, which may not see the plan) evaluates all nine trigger questions of Appendix D section 2 item 3 at the start of each work item or task, after each material change of plan or evidence, and before it enters a wait or ends work whose next step waits on a schedule, a person or an outside event (PC-23); a question that does not apply is answered "no", and "yes" or "uncertain" means reading that discipline's full text (Appendix D section 3) and applying it. D9 is answered against the knowledge maps in the role files (PC-52): when the work makes a design, architecture, implementation or testing decision, or touches an area of a role's map, D9 is "yes" or "uncertain", and the answer names what was read and whether it changed, limited or justified the work, or bore on none of it and why (Appendix D's D9 items 1 and 5). A bare "D9: no" (D-031) says the work does neither; a helper's reason for it is its task's library line (section 4 step 3). When the library could not be read, the answer is "D9: uncertain: could not consult the library: <why>", never "no" (section 3). Answering is mandatory, and its presence is checked by tools: `tools/records.py render --check` for the executor's log entries (from L-159), `tools/subagent_audit.py disciplines` for a helper's task and report. The content is the agent's own judgment, a hint and not evidence (Appendix D section 2), which checkers sample. Every task the executor writes carries the questions in the words of section 9, "Discipline block".

## 3. Opening, and re-reading

**Opening check** (once, when the session starts; summary 12):
1. `get_session` without `session_id`: the model is `claude-opus-5-5` and the permission mode is `acceptEdits` or `default` (the app's "Accept edits" choice registers as `default` for a session Batu opens, observed on 2026-10-05, L-118 CR-1; in both the guard decides every call). Effort, ultracode and the goal condition (`external_metadata.goal`) are recorded as shown; a setting that is not shown is recorded as not visible, not as absent (the repository pins effort at `xhigh`). The evidence of the goal is the session's own first message.
2. `python3 tools/guard_report.py`: this session's decision log exists and counts allowed calls, so the guard is live; note the effort the harness reports. Also count the hooks the repository does not provide that the session can see, the entries of the hooks files of the plugins under the user-level plugins folder (`~/.claude/plugins/`): counted by event, not named, or "could not read", never zero for a folder not read (PC-35).
3. If the model, the mode or the guard is not as required, or the session's own first message is not the installation `/goal` of Appendix F, do no plan work: tell Batu in one short Turkish message what to change in the session, and record it.
4. Write what was found in a log entry, then continue.

**The library** (at the opening check, and again after every compaction; PC-52). `python3 tools/leak_fingerprints.py status` shows whether the library's clone is at `/home/user/agentic-os-search` and its HEAD, and whether the leak store was built from that HEAD so that public writes are checked (section 10). The opening check's log entry records what it shows. A missing or stale store while the clone exists makes the guard deny every public write: rebuild it (section 10) before the next one. If the clone is absent or cannot be read, the state is "could not consult the library", recorded as its own state, never as a D9 "no": the tasks' library lines and the D9 answers then say so (section 2; section 4 step 3). A step that would make a decision in an area of a role's map (Appendix D's D9 item 1) does not start until the library can be read (Appendix D's D8 item 7), and the log names each step held; work that touches no map area goes on. Attaching the library is Batu's account action: in Accept edits the platform refused `add_repo` (2026-10-12, L-285) or held it for an approval that did not reach him (2026-10-19, L-416; N-310 (2)). So the executor records its reason, a checker judges the single-action window that D-034 (b) names (re-attaching the library), and the request goes to Batu as one line (section 8); the wait is section 7's (a).

**Where it left off** (at opening, and after every compaction): read this file, `CLAUDE.md`, the executor's package under `.claude/roles/` (from W-C02-23; read once it is merged and synced into this tree; until then there is none), plan Appendix D section 2 and Appendix G's G4, G8 and G10 (the reasoning layer, section 2), `plan/ledger.md` sections 1 and 2, `DURUM.md`, the last entries of the current stage's log (`plan/ledger/<stage>-log.md`) and Batu's new answers (section 8); then read in full the plan sections that the next item names. At opening, also read the whole plan and all its appendices (plan 0.1). After a compaction the hook `.claude/hooks/reread_after_compact.py` prints a reminder to re-read this list; re-read before continuing, and do not trust the summary; then repeat the library's check above.

## 4. The work loop

1. **Next step in plan order.** Take an item from the startable frontier in `plan/ledger.md` section 2 (generated by `python3 tools/records.py render`), critical path first, with one logged sentence of reason.
1a. **Team triage** (PC-53; D-039). Before an item's acceptance condition and approach merge, the executor asks the roles the item's work touches, chosen by the triggers in each role file's `description:` line as `main` holds it; an asked role may name another, and the executor drops a named role only with a disposition. Two rounds by default. In the first, each asked role gives its take-up of the item and its note from its own field. The executor integrates the notes into the item's approach block (section 9). In the second, every asked role, and at least every field owner that a cross-field disposition overrules, answers that block `PROCEED` or with a typed objection (a defect, missing evidence, a preference or a scope conflict), and those answers are filed in the EV record that the block's `asked:` line cites. The approach is agreed only when every asked role's last answer there is `PROCEED` or its objection has a disposition in the block. Inside its field the owner decides: the method, the producer; the criterion, the test designer; the depth and reading mode of a check, the checker (a triage checker's depth is a minimum: the binding checker may go deeper, and says why); platform behaviour, the prober, by a discriminating observation; what a source says, the researcher, by re-reading it (the researcher settles what a source states, not the decision). Across fields the executor decides and records a disposition: the direction, the alternatives, what was given up, what still holds of the objection, and what reopens it. A majority never decides. "No team round needed, because …", with one role's reason, is a valid result, and so is a role's reasoned `NOT_NEEDED`.
2. **Acceptance before results.** An item's acceptance condition is in its file and merged into `main` before work on it starts, and it is never loosened afterwards; if it must be, the earlier result is void and the test is repeated (ledger rule 3; plan section 14).
2a. **One pull request, then the task** (PC-53). The acceptance block, the approach block and the EV record of the triage notes go in one pull request; after its merge, `tools/sync_worktree.sh`; then the producer's task (section 9, "Task template"). A role's blocking return after the approach is agreed is recorded as a blocking note on the item, and the executor closes it only by a disposition in the approach.
3. **Split and delegate.** Give the parts to role subagents (`Agent` with the role as its type), with no isolation option; long work runs in the background, and a finished background subagent starts the next turn by itself. From W-C02-24's M1a (not in force before it is merged and synced) the guard admits only the five role types and denies the Workflow tool as a whole (rules T4 and TW, section 10), and rule T5 reads every producer and test-designer task: its `Writer or reader:` and `Item:` lines (section 9) and, in a producer's writer task, the item's approach block, which the executor pastes by tool from `git show HEAD:<item file>`, byte for byte. Each task names its purpose and item, the expected output and where it goes, sources and tools, limits and budget, and whether the agent writes or only reads. It also carries a library line (PC-52): the library paths, taken from the knowledge map in the role's file, that bear on the task and are read first; or "none", with the reason naming the map areas the task does not touch; or, while the library cannot be read, that state (section 3). The paths are where the helper starts, not the edge of its search, and naming them is not their use: its D9 answer says what it read and what that changed (section 2; Appendix D's D9 item 2). The counter-designer's task has no library line, since it may not see the library. One file has one writer at a time. A writing helper stays the writer of its files until the platform's end status for it has arrived (PC-24). Every task is written in the task template of section 9 (PC-53), with no method field. A task that a role returns at its take-up is never sent again unchanged: the executor first records a disposition (what it changed, or why the task stands).
4. **Check.** A `checker` that did not produce the result judges it, at its full head SHA, against the acceptance condition and the plan text it serves. The executor writes its verdict verbatim to `evidence/<stage>/checks/CHK-<stage>-<nnn>.md` (section 9), taken with `tools/subagent_audit.py last`, and checks with `tools/subagent_audit.py disciplines` that the task carried the questions and the verdict carries the nine answers; a helper's report is taken the same way (D-016). `last`, `disciplines` and `audit` run only after the helper's end status has arrived, and say `NOT FINISHED` before it (PC-24; N-149). After `DISCIPLINES MISSING`, the helper's output is not taken as a result. If the task lacked the block, the task is given again, with the block, to a fresh helper or to the same helper resumed by message (`SendMessage`, under guard rule T6, section 10; a producer, test designer or checker is resumed only by the executor). If the report or verdict lacks the nine answers, it is not filed or used, and the task is given again, to a fresh helper or by message to the same one. The earlier output may be read as a hint, never as evidence or a verdict. The log records the result and the re-run (N-110). On PASS, or PASS-WITH-CONDITIONS with every condition met, the item is accepted (`acceptance: accepted`, `accepted_by:` the verdict file). On FAIL: fix, and a fresh checker judges again, within the loop limit of section 6 (a second FAIL on the same item opens a frame review before a third attempt). **Typed conditions and closure modes** (PC-53; for verdicts given after its merge). The checker types each condition by its consequence: a defect (a missing step is one) or missing evidence blocks; a preference goes to the field's owner, and a scope conflict to a frame review, and to Batu only if it touches his purpose or scope; neither of these two blocks the merge or the acceptance, so for such a verdict "every condition met" above means every blocking condition, the others being routed. Each condition names what it blocks (the producer task, the merge or the acceptance) and its closure mode: `diff-confirmed` only for a condition whose closing diff is class normal and mechanically checkable, recorded as the executor's own diff check, not independent, listed in the log entry and named in the next verdict's task; `scoped re-review` for a blocking condition whose fix touches class-high paths, a verdict file at the new head from a fresh checker, or from the same checker resumed by its ID by the executor's message under T6 (section 10; W-C02-27, in force from the first call after its sync), declaring D4 item 10's anchoring risk in D4 item 9's limited language; `full fresh check` only when the condition changes the claim. A dispute over a condition's type goes to a scoped re-review by a fresh checker given the producer's evidence; the executor never retypes a condition. The checker's own triage, its floor and its two output forms are in `checker.md`. A binding check's task gives the design inputs (the approach, the triage notes, the hand-overs) by path, asks the product-only questions first and names the plan texts to open only after those findings are written; the verdict reports the two readings under separate headings, with no single score (Appendix G G4 item 2). From PC-53's merge the loop limit counts rounds that leave a blocking condition open (section 6 item 2).
5. **Merge.** Edit, render and commit in a scratch clone (`git -C <literal path>`), never in the live working tree, which stays at `main` (guard B4); after each merge bring it forward with `tools/sync_worktree.sh`. Commit on a `claude/` branch with the machine account as the commit's git author and committer, unsigned: before a scratch clone's first commit, set in it `git -C <clone> config user.name batuhanozgun-devos`, `git -C <clone> config user.email 335658439+batuhanozgun-devos@users.noreply.github.com` and `git -C <clone> config commit.gpgsign false`, so that GitHub shows every commit made there, merge commits included, under the machine account (N-156; W-C01-18; the platform's own identity and signature would show it under the platform's account); push, open a pull request on `batuhanozgun/devos`, and merge with `merge_method: merge` and the full 40-character head SHA as `expectedHeadSha` (guard M6). The guard then runs `python3 tools/merge_gate.py --pr N --head SHA` (M7), which reads each item's state and approach block `writes:` line at the merge base: a head whose diff from `main` touches a class-high path (listed in `tools/merge_gate.py`), deletes a `plan/work/*.md` file, changes or removes an existing acceptance or approach block in one, or touches a path an open item's `writes:` lists and is not records-only, needs a verdict file that the pull request itself adds or changes, whose `reviewed_head` is that head or a commit X of this pull request (not one already on `main`) after which only class-normal paths changed; a verdict already on `main` covers no later pull request. The verdict's keys are read in their grammar: for a declared path its `item:` names that path's item and its `producer_run` is not `none`, which the binding checker confirms with `launch --item` and `calls` (a producer, or a test designer for test and oracle paths only; attempted writes, path level); a head that touches a floor path needs a floor class and `own_breaks`; a records-only head (every path class normal and under `plan/` or `evidence/`, or `DURUM.md`) needs no verdict; a revert of a merge commit on `main` may take the revert route (`item: revert <sha>`). Each refusal names its reason ID; the rules in full are in section 10, "The merge gate's verdict forms". So: commit the change, have it checked at that SHA, add the verdict file and the records, then merge.
6. **Record.** A log entry, the item's status, and `summary_tr` (Turkish, for Batu) in `plan/ledger.md` section 1; then `python3 tools/records.py render`, which regenerates the ledger's generated blocks and `DURUM.md`. Merge at every checkpoint (each finished item) and before every stop (PC-03).
7. **Go on** to the next step. A stage boundary is not a stop.

**Where a result ran** (PC-52; N-309 (3)). The evidence record that holds an executed result (its `EV-` record, or the raw file under `evidence/<stage>/raw/` that the record cites) names, for that result, the interpreter and its version, the database engine and its version, and where it ran: the session's container, the job's runner and its image, or the project. The log entry cites that record and does not repeat it; no other record is added. A task that has a helper run or write a job program names the counted environment, the one whose run counts for acceptance (in C02, the `migrate` job's runner and the project it targets). A check run offline on another version than the counted environment is labelled exploratory for each claim that version can change; the counted run decides that claim.

## 5. Side branches (summary 21)

Reasoning may propose a side branch: work that the current plan step does not name. Before it opens, the executor writes in the log the plan step it serves, its return point and its limit, and a `checker` asks "have we broken away from the plan?". The branch closes before the next plan step starts, and a branch that reaches its limit closes; the work then returns to the main line.

**When a branch closes** (PC-40). A branch closes when all three are done: (1) its result, or its cancellation with a reason, is recorded on the branch; (2) its closing check passes: a `checker` judges that head and asks the same question (the check that accepts the branch's result can be its closing check, if its task asks the question), and gives PASS, or PASS-WITH-CONDITIONS whose conditions are met before the merge; after the judged head the branch takes only what those conditions require, the records of the close and `main` merged into it, and anything more needs a fresh closing check; (3) the branch is merged into `main`. Neither a result recorded on the branch nor a passing verdict closes it by itself; a failed closing check leaves it open. The log records the close: the closing verdict and the merge.

**What waits for the close** (PC-40). While a branch is open, no plan step starts that had not started when it opened, and so none while it waits only on its closing check and merge: the next plan step starts after the merge. For this rule a plan step is an item of a stage's work list, or a change that a note requires to a file other than the records (the log, the items' and notes' state lines, `DURUM.md`, the ledger's generated views, a stage file's "Usage" rows, and an evidence record, or an addendum to one, that only states what was observed); a change to what a file instructs or decides is a plan step wherever it is written (for example N-143's change to `evidence/C01/routine/CONTROL.md`, L-199). Work a note requires that only observes and records (for example a listing it asks for at a set time) is not a plan step, and may start and go on while a branch is open when it relies neither on the branch's result nor on its rules, which the log states. A plan step starts with the first task given or the first commit made for it. Work started before the branch opened goes on. Until the merge, no work outside the branch relies on its result or on the rules it changes (`CLAUDE.md`: what is not merged into `main` does not exist). A plan step started while a branch is open is a departure, and the log records it as one. This fits sections 4 and 7: the closing check runs in the background like any check (section 4 step 3), and its end starts the next turn by itself, so waiting for it is none of section 7's states; and for section 7's "finish and merge all work that does not depend on it", a plan step not yet started depends on every open branch. Meanwhile the executor makes the close (the conditions, the records, the merge) and goes on with the work started before the branch opened, with records, and with a note's observations that rely neither on the branch's result nor on its rules.
