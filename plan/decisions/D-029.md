# D-029

**What is asked (one sentence, Appendix E section 3).** Will you turn off, on claude.ai's skill settings, the 24 skills your account syncs into every DevOS session, 12 of which ask for things DevOS's rules forbid, knowing that this also removes them from your claude.ai chats: (a) turn them off, except any you use in your other work (recommended), (b) keep them, listed in DevOS's sessions with their loading refused, or (c) turn off code execution and file creation in your settings, which also turns those features off in your chats?

**Why it is yours.** Turning skills off changes your account's settings, and by the documentation's account the change holds for the whole account: the skills leave your claude.ai chats and your other Claude Code sessions as well as DevOS's. The builder cannot make that change and does not make it for you. No setting that DevOS's repository can carry is documented to keep these skills out of DevOS's sessions alone.

**What you need to know.**
- Every DevOS session (the installation's working session, the routine's sessions, and later the sessions of the working, audit and exam environments, which live in your one account) receives the skills enabled on your claude.ai account. The C01 routine's run P4 (11 October 2026) counted 24 of them, and one more skill whose source is not shown (`evidence/C01/EV-C01-026_W-C01-46_row13_P4.md`). They are not a plugin, so DevOS's plugin setting does not reach them.
- 12 of the 24 texts ask for something DevOS's rules do not allow: 8 of them, without a condition, for a connector or another account service, which `CLAUDE.md` forbids; 4 only under a condition. The one other skill asks for a write into the guard's folder and the repository's settings file, which the guard denies.
- DevOS's guard already refuses to load any of them (rule T4). What stays is their names and short descriptions in each session's list: text the model reads, not an action it can take, because the guard also refuses the connector tools and the writes they ask for. That text is the residual that (b) keeps.
- Some of Anthropic's skills always sync, whatever the toggle says; they stay listed under every option, and the guard refuses to load them too. Skills you enable later reach DevOS's sessions the same way.
- When a running session takes up the change is not stated; the next session start shows it.
- These points come from the platform's documentation, read on 11 October 2026 (`evidence/C01/EV-C01-027_PC-35_synced_skills_documentation.md`), and from the routine's run; that the toggle holds for the whole account is an inference, since no per-surface switch is stated, and none of it has been tested.

**Options.**
- **(a) Turn them off on claude.ai, except any you use in your other work (recommended).**
  - Purpose: remove the conflicting texts from DevOS's sessions at their source.
  - Benefit: the names and descriptions of the skills you turn off leave the list of every DevOS session, including the environments created later, so the text the guard cannot remove is gone, and C03 has a smaller residual to state. No money.
  - Cost: by inference, the skills you turn off also leave your claude.ai chats and your other Claude Code sessions; your chats and files do not change, and you can turn any skill back on, which brings it back into DevOS's sessions too. A few minutes of your time. Skills that always sync, the one skill whose source is not shown, and skills you enable later stay listed, with their loading refused.
  - Alternative: keep on the ones you use in your other work and turn off the rest; those you keep stay listed, as under (b).
- **(b) Keep them.**
  - Purpose: change nothing on your account.
  - Benefit: no action from you; your chats keep every skill.
  - Cost: in every DevOS session their names and descriptions stay in the list, 12 of them asking for things DevOS's rules forbid; the guard refuses to load them and refuses the connector tools and writes they ask for, so the risk is instruction text in the model's context, not an allowed action. It is re-assessed with D-003 at C03.
  - Alternative: (a) at any later time.
- **(c) Turn Skills off entirely.**
  - The documentation names no single switch that turns all skills off for an individual account. The nearest: on individual plans, skills depend on Settings > Capabilities > "Code execution and file creation"; by inference, turning it off disables skills.
  - Purpose: no skill you can switch off reaches any session, including skills you would enable later.
  - Benefit: (a)'s benefit for every skill, without choosing one by one. Whether it also stops the sync into DevOS's cloud sessions, and whether it removes the skills that always sync, is not stated.
  - Cost: it also turns off code execution and file creation in all your claude.ai chats (for example analysing an uploaded file or creating a document), not only skills, and by inference in your other Claude Code sessions. It is not documented as a skills switch, so its effect on DevOS's sessions would have to be observed.
  - Alternative: (a).

**Recommendation: (a)**, keeping on only the skills you use in your other work. It is the only step that removes the conflicting texts from DevOS's sessions, at no money cost, and it rests on the documented toggle; (c) changes much more in your chats and is not documented as a skills switch; (b) leaves conflicting instruction text in every DevOS session.

**If you do not answer.** (b) holds: nothing on your account changes, the guard keeps refusing to load the skills, and their listing stays the residual that C03 states and re-assesses with D-003. No work waits on your answer.

**How to answer.** Write `D-029: a`, `D-029: b` or `D-029: c` on issue #6 or in the working session. For (a), after the steps, write only how many skills you turned off and how many you kept on, never their names: the repository and the issue are public.

**What is checked after your answer.** Under (a) or (c), at the working session's next start, or in C02's first sessions if none comes before C01 closes: the skills synced into the session are counted, not named, and a skill you turned off that is still offered is reported to you.
