# FR-05

**What it is.** The frame review Batu directed before any further C01 work on platform separation (L-178), made by the executor as coordinator under plan 6.12 item 2. Its question is his: is each of three parts of the security scope required by DevOS, or more than its need? It is kept at the level of need, by D-017's standing constraint.

**The squeeze.** Three working sessions in a row were interrupted by the host's automatic content review while reading or writing this material, and the response was a series of added mechanisms (the interim leak check's extensions, PC-18 and N-109, the probe-only guard rule, FR-04's run orchestration, D-017). That is the signal of plan 6.12 item 2: question the frame before adding another mechanism.

**The test.** For each part: which criterion or Batu decision requires it; whether shrinking it lowers a requirement or only removes more than the need (plan 0.3 items 2, 5 and 10; Appendix E section 5: quality is not lowered, and the simpler solution at the same quality is preferred); and plan 6.12 item 4.

**Decision.**

A. **Separation between environments (C01).** The property is required (criteria 8 and 30; PC-05). Showing it by active trials against the platform is more than DevOS's need: it tests the platform, not DevOS, and the platform documents session and environment isolation (counter-assessment, sources read 2026-10-10). So:
1. C01 establishes the separation-related platform facts from the platform's dated official documentation (plan 0.3 item 12; D-017 item 2(a), 2(c)), and DevOS's own side by reading its own configuration (which environment, repositories and settings each routine has). DevOS's own enforcement (its database's role rules) is shown by its own negative tests in C02 and C03, which test DevOS's code, not the platform.
2. No active trial across environments is made. The probe environments with tokens (P1), the probe schema (P3) and the probe-only guard rule are not built.
3. Ordinary platform facts that need a session started by a routine (session duration and hand-over, the tools such a session has, subagents in it, the routine limit, the skill inventory) are observed with one ordinary routine in one environment, without tokens or connectors, recording only what that session observes about itself.
4. Where the documentation does not state a fact, it is recorded as not stated, and the design takes the path that does not depend on it (for example, exam answers behind the exam role in the database, plan 7.3's own alternative).
5. The claim is limited and recorded as: "guaranteed by platform (documented, with its source and reading date); not independently tested". The requirement is not lowered; the level of its evidence is stated (plan 8 item 7).
This changes plan text, so it is made through plan Section 14 as PC-21, with its own checker, as D-017 item 3 requires; FR-05 decides the frame, PC-21 the text.

B. **The installation's guard.** Its required core stays: D-008 (Batu's) and D-003's barrier until the audit environment. It is kept as it is for the installation and grows no new rule families; the probe-only rule falls with A. Its phase-B form (K-9 item 4, a [Proposal]) is sized by need at the stage that builds it: platform configuration first, to remove what must never be used; custom code only where D-008's written reasons need it. A check that runs inside the session is not counted as an authority boundary; authority lives in the tokens, the database and GitHub's rules, as K-9 item 4 already says. No change to D-008 or D-003. A note carries this to C03 and C05.

C. **The leak and credential checks.** Their required core stays: K6's code-based pre-check before public writes (criteria 8 and 31), the interim check until C04 and the phase-B check from C04. GitHub's secret scanning and push protection carry known secret types (criterion 27). The one-off C00 credential tools are spent installation scaffolding: not extended and not used further. Whether the semantic layer is kept is decided at C04 with its measurement; because Batu was told that both layers exist (plan 0.5, PC-07 note; U-6), dropping it would go to him then. The key inventory stays: it is ordinary operations, needed for revocation and restore. A note carries this to C03 and C04.

**What is Batu's.** Nothing now. A follows his own direction (L-178) and plan 0.3 item 12; B and C change none of his decisions. He is told the outcome in one line (Appendix E section 3; D-013).

**Comparison of the two inputs, every difference closed.**
1. Both judge separation required and active platform trials more than the need. The premise inventory adds that the evidence level drops and that premises of D-003, D-014 and PC-05 then rest on documentation. Closed: the claim is limited (A.5), and a fact the documentation does not state takes the path that does not depend on it (A.4).
2. The counter-assessment judges a custom check of every tool call over-scope; the premise inventory finds the decider is Batu's requirement (D-008). Closed: the installation keeps it (B); its phase-B form is sized by need.
3. The counter-assessment judges a semantic gate over-built; the plan already routes semantic matches to review, not to a block. Closed: decided at C04 with its measurement (C).
4. The counter-assessment offers keeping each environment token where the session cannot read it, as a platform option. Closed: a design option for the token's form, carried to C02 by a note; it changes nothing in C01's frame.
5. The premise inventory names B3 (a)'s shared GitHub reach as a shared premise, whose strongest alternative is closing the channel at account level, a Batu decision. Closed: FR-05 adds no mechanism for it; it stays in reopen_if.

**Second-order effects (12 to 24 months).** Fewer custom parts mean less upkeep, fewer false blocks of unattended runs and less exposure to the host's review. More of the control state lives in web settings outside git, so a periodic read-only record of the configuration is needed (carried to C06's maintenance work). Documentation-based claims go stale when the platform changes, so they are re-read then (plan 0.3 item 12; 6.12 item 4).
