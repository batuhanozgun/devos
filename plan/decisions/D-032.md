# D-032

**What is asked (one sentence, Appendix E section 3).** After the installation ledger moves to the database at the end of C02, should the database be the authoritative home of the installation's progress records (where each piece of work stands, the log, the notes, the decisions' status), with `main` receiving a copy of them after every completed piece of work and before every stop, or should your rule in D-010, "The only source of truth is `main`", stay word for word: (a) the database is the authority and `main` holds a copy updated each time (recommended), or (b) `main` stays the only source of truth and the database holds a copy filled from it?

**Soru (Türkçe, #6 için).** C02'nin sonunda kurulum defteri veritabanına taşındıktan sonra, kurulumun ilerleme kayıtlarının (hangi işin ne durumda olduğu, günlük, notlar, kararların durumu) asıl yeri veritabanı olsun ve `main` bunların kopyasını her tamamlanan işten sonra ve her duruştan önce alsın mı, yoksa D-010'daki "Tek doğru kaynak `main`'dir" kuralın sözü sözüne kalsın mı?
- (a) Asıl yer veritabanı; `main` her seferinde güncellenen kopyayı tutar (önerim).
- (b) Tek doğru kaynak `main` kalır; kayıtlar bugünkü gibi `main`'e yazılır, veritabanı onlardan doldurulan kopyayı tutar.

Cevap: `D-032: a` ya da `D-032: b`.

**Why it is yours.** Your rule "The only source of truth is `main`" is part of your decision D-010 (plan Section 9, the builder's working order, item 2), and a text labelled as your decision changes only by your decision. The ledger transfer the plan asks for at the end of C02 has been designed so that, from then on, the records are written to the database first and reach `main` as a copy at each checkpoint. That keeps the purpose your rule served, as the builder reads it, but not its words: between two checkpoints a record exists in the database before `main` shows it. Whether your rule was meant to hold after the ledger moves is yours to say. How either option is built is the builder's work, checked by fresh checkers; you are not asked a technical question.

**What you need to know.**
- The plan asks for the move. Plan Section 9's "Installation ledger" (written before D-010 and kept in it) says the ledger is transferred to the database at the end of C02 and that afterwards `plan/ledger.md` "is not an authoritative write surface"; C02 is accepted only when that transfer's four conditions are met. The `/goal` you pasted, rewritten with D-010 on 5 October, names C12's acceptance record as "plan/work/C12.md until the ledger moves to the database at the end of C02, its database record after that". DevOS's own draft rules say "Live state: Supabase". Only D-010's item 2 says `main` is the only source of truth, with no time limit.
- D-010's reason for item 2 is continuity: where the work left off is carried by the record, not by the session, and the work is merged before every stop. Under (a) the session re-reads the database and the copy on `main`; under (b) it re-reads `main` as today.
- From C03, the binding verdicts on the installation's work are written by DevOS's audit environment, in the database (plan Section 9 item 5; Appendix B 3.11).
- The copy on `main` under (a) holds every log entry word for word and the state views generated from the database (`DURUM.md`, the ledger's views); the records written before the move stay in git as they are. W-C02-14 builds the views and shows that each kind of record has one authoritative home.
- Under either option `DURUM.md` stays your page, issue #6 your channel, and the decision records your questions link stay in git.
- These points come from the plan, its appendices and the design record (`evidence/C02/EV-C02-005_ledger_transfer.md`, sections 3.4, 4 and 9); nothing of it has been built or tested yet.

**Options.**
- **(a) The database is the authority; `main` holds a copy updated at each checkpoint (recommended).**
  - Purpose: do what the plan asks at the end of C02, so that the installation's records live where DevOS's own rules and, from C03, its audit environment govern them.
  - Benefit: every record the installation writes from then on passes DevOS's rule gate when it is written (role classes, format gates, no duplicates); from C03 the audit environment's binding verdicts are in the same place as the work they judge; DevOS's later stages read the real records; `main` keeps a reviewable copy, and the git history before the move stays as it is. C02's acceptance condition on the hand-over can be met as written. No money; no action from you.
  - Cost:
    - Time: none added; this is the design already made, built in W-C02-14.
    - Quality and risk: `main` lags the database between checkpoints: a record binds once it is in the database, before `main` shows it, and anyone reading GitHub then sees the copy of the last checkpoint.
    - A database outage, or a broken path to it, cuts access to the authoritative records until the service returns or the database is restored: the work that needs them stops ("could not check"), and merges that touch the views are blocked. The restore path is tested only in C09; until then, a loss of the database would leave the copy on `main` and the git history as of the last checkpoint, and no rebuild from them is designed or tested.
    - Between checkpoints the records' integrity rests on the database's rules (direct writes closed, events append-only), not on git's history.
    - More machinery: the merge gate reads the database, and a write that lands between a render and a merge makes the merge fail until the views are rendered again.
    - The texts that state your rule (plan line 879 item 2, `plan/ledger.md` rule 6, `CLAUDE.md`) are reworded to say where the records live and where their copy is.
  - Alternative: the same, with the copy merged after every record write rather than at each checkpoint: less lag, many more merges, and the same question of authority.
- **(b) `main` stays the only source of truth; the database holds a copy filled from it.**
  - Purpose: keep your rule word for word.
  - Benefit: no lag; the git history stays the authority; a database outage does not cut access to the records; the records tools stay as they are; `CLAUDE.md` and `plan/ledger.md` rule 6 stay true as written.
  - Cost:
    - Time: W-C02-06's design is redone for what moves and what stays, and checked again; W-C02-14's acceptance condition is changed through the merge gate; a path that fills the copy after each merge, and updates records that change, is designed and built (the transfer as designed refuses a changed record). About a day of the working session's time, on the critical path only if it is still open when W-C02-14 is otherwise ready (inference).
    - Quality: DevOS's rule gate checks the installation's records only after they are already true on `main`, so a record the gate refuses still stands. From C03 the audit environment's binding verdicts count on `main` only once a copy is merged, written by the working session whose work they judge, or by a new mechanism (inference). DevOS's later stages read a copy.
    - Risk: the records stay reachable when the database is not; the risk moves to the copy, which can fall behind `main` or differ from it, and then misleads whatever reads it unless a comparison catches it (the same kind of check (a) needs, run the other way).
    - Plan: C02's acceptance condition "after the transfer, `ledger.md` is not an authoritative write surface" and Appendix C N18's matching case cannot be met as written under (b) as defined (records written in git as today); they change through plan Section 14 on your answer.
  - Alternative: (b) until C03, then (a): the question comes back at C03, and C02's hand-over condition is still not met at C02's end.

**Recommendation: (a).** The plan as a whole asks for the move, including the `/goal` that D-010 itself rewrote ("its database record after that"); (a) is the reading under which both those texts and D-010's reason hold: the record, not the session, carries where the work left off, and the work is still merged into `main` after every completed piece of work and before every stop. (b) keeps your rule's words, which is real value (no lag, no dependence on the database being reachable), but it leaves C02's acceptance condition unmet, adds a copy mechanism, and leaves DevOS's own rules checking only a copy of the installation's records. The cost of (a) is bounded but real: the lag lasts until the next checkpoint; an outage stops the affected work rather than letting it go on from stale records; and a restore is untested until C09. The decision is yours.
