### 1. Section 5, its paragraph (line 57). Labelled: no (summary 21, (K)).
Old: "Reasoning may propose a side branch: work that the current plan step does not name. New (three paragraphs):
"Reasoning may propose a side branch: work that the current plan step does not name. Before it opens, the executor writes in the log the plan step it serves, its return point and its limit, and a `checker` asks "have we broken away from the plan?". The branch closes before the next plan step starts, and a branch that reaches its limit closes; the work then returns to the main line.

**When a branch closes** (PC-40). A branch closes when all three are done: (1) its result, or its cancellation with a reason, is recorded on the branch; (2) its closing check passes: a `checker` judges that head and asks the same question (the check that accepts the branch's result can be its closing check, if its task asks the question), and gives PASS, or PASS-WITH-CONDITIONS whose conditions are met before the merge; after the judged head the branch takes only what those conditions require, the records of the close and `main` merged into it, and anything more needs a fresh closing check; (3) the branch is merged into `main`. Neither a result recorded on the branch nor a passing verdict closes it by itself; a failed closing check leaves it open. The log records the close: the closing verdict and the merge.

**What waits for the close** (PC-40). While a branch is open, no plan step starts that had not started when it opened, and so none while it waits only on its closing check and merge: the next plan step starts after the merge. For this rule a plan step is an item of a stage's work list, or a change that a note requires to a file other than the records (the log, the items' and notes' state lines, `DURUM.md`, the ledger's generated views, a stage file's "Usage" rows, and an evidence record, or an addendum to one, that only states what was observed); a change to what a file instructs or decides is a plan step wherever it is written (for example N-143's change to `evidence/C01/routine/CONTROL.md`, L-199). Work a note requires that only observes and records (for example a listing it asks for at a set time) is not a plan step, and may start and go on while a branch is open when it relies neither on the branch's result nor on its rules, which the log states. A plan step starts with the first task given or the first commit made for it. Work started before the branch opened goes on. Until the merge, no work outside the branch relies on its result or on the rules it changes (`CLAUDE.md`: what is not merged into `main` does not exist). A plan step started while a branch is open is a departure, and the log records it as one. This fits sections 4 and 7: the closing check runs in the background like any check (section 4 step 3), and its end starts the next turn by itself, so waiting for it is none of section 7's states; and for section 7's "finish and merge all work that does not depend on it", a plan step not yet started depends on every open branch. Meanwhile the executor makes the close (the conditions, the records, the merge) and goes on with the work started before the branch opened, with records, and with a note's observations that rely neither on the branch's result nor on its rules."
Reason: the decision below. Stages: C02 onward.

The wait (B) imposes ends without Batu, a schedule or an outside event: the background check's end starts the next turn (section 4 step 3).

**Risk.** The failure class is the next step depending on a side branch not yet merged: it uses a file or a decision from the branch; it relies on a rule the branch changes (a guard or working-order change); work beyond the closing check's conditions is added to the branch while the next step runs; the overlap goes unlogged. Under (A) each is forbidden only by a condition the executor applies to its own work at every overlap (whether the next step relies on the branch), which no tool sees until the stage-close sample, and a closing check that fails after the next step started leaves two lines of work open at once. Under (B) the first two cannot arise for a new step, and "until the merge, no work outside the branch relies on its result or on the rules it changes" covers work already running; the third is closed by "after the judged head the branch takes only what those conditions require ... and anything more needs a fresh closing check", which holds under either choice; the fourth is named: a step started while a branch is open is a departure, logged as one. A side branch serves the current plan step and returns to it, so the next step usually relies on its result: three of C01's four did, and for them (A)'s third condition would have held the step anyway.

**PC-23's rule against needless waits.** D3 item 2 (Appendix D, as PC-23 place 3 changed it) asks for "a simpler, more reliable or sooner way at the same quality" and to "state the time it adds and weigh it like any other method choice". (A) is sooner by at most the interval above, and was by zero on C01's critical path, but not at the same quality: it adds the dependency judgment and the two-open-lines case. So the wait (B) keeps is not a needless one in PC-23's sense. Section 7's states (a) to (c) do not include a background check, so (B) adds no Wait line and no question to Batu; section 7's "finish and merge all work that does not depend on it" still holds for everything but a new plan step, which by section 5 depends on the open branch (place 3).
