### 2.1 Appendix B section 8

**DM-01 Concurrency control. Provisional.**
(a) `claim` locks its work item's row (`SELECT … FOR UPDATE`), re-checks readiness and the active claim (with the lease computed, DM-06), marks a lapsed claim `expired`, and inserts the new claim with the next generation; the unique index of one active claim per work item (B:153) stays as the backstop. Every function that changes a work item's readiness inputs (`claim`, `release`, `revoke_claim`, `revise_work`, `cancel_work`, `mark_stale`, and `relate` or `retract_relation` on a hard dependency) locks that row for update; an effect's re-check (F08) locks it, the claim and the global authority epoch's row for share inside the effect's short transaction. A write of a hard dependency first takes one transaction-level advisory lock, then checks for a cycle, then writes. Isolation is PostgreSQL's default, read committed. Locks are taken in one order: the hard-dependency lock, the work item, the claim, the global authority epoch. **The global authority epoch** is one row in `devos_private`, readable by no application role and distinct from the claim's own `authority_epoch` field (B 3.5), which records the epoch the claim was made in: every effect's re-check reads the row for share, and `revoke_env_token` takes it for update and increments it, last in the order above. It is built with the identity chain's first part (EV3; EV-C02-004 X-15), and EV3 §1.4 cites this decision for it (X-02).
(b) Two claims of one work item queue on its row; the second sees the first's committed claim and is refused, and one always succeeds (C:110) with no retry. The cycle check must see every hard dependency committed before it: the one advisory lock puts hard-dependency writes in a queue, and under read committed each statement after the lock sees what was committed before it began. Locking only the two ends of a new edge is not enough: four edges that close a cycle of four, added at once, can pass their checks in two pairs that share no end (A→B with C→D, then B→C with D→A), each check running before the other's commit (Inf). An effect that holds its work item for share cannot be overtaken between its re-check and its write by a new dependency or a revocation (F08). One order of locks keeps these paths from deadlocking one another. One row for the epoch makes a revocation and every effect meet on one lock: an effect that read the old epoch for share finishes before the increment, and one that starts after it sees the new epoch, so no effect of the old epoch lands after the revocation (Inf). It is the simpler of the forms that meet C:108 (K-10): waiting instead of failing, so no caller needs retry code.
(c) Doc (PG transaction-iso, 13.2.1): "Read Committed is the default isolation level in PostgreSQL", and a query there "sees only data committed before the query began"; at the stricter levels "applications using this level must be prepared to retry transactions due to serialization failures". Doc (PG explicit-locking, 13.3): `FOR UPDATE` "prevents them from being locked, modified or deleted by other transactions until the current transaction ends"; explicit locking "can increase the likelihood of deadlocks" when transactions take locks in different orders. Doc (PG functions-admin): `pg_advisory_xact_lock` "Obtains an exclusive transaction-level advisory lock, waiting if necessary". Inf: the four-edge case; DevOS's write rate is low (a few sessions, 6.4), so one queue for hard-dependency writes costs nothing measurable. Doc: B 3.5 (the claim's `authority_epoch`), B 3.27; EV-C02-004 X-02, X-03, X-15 (2026-10-12). No observation exists yet: B:442's basis is "concurrency tests and failure frequency", which come from a later test.
(d) **Initial proposal** ("row lock and uniqueness constraint on claiming; stricter isolation on critical transitions"): kept for the claim; its "stricter isolation" is replaced by explicit locks, because serializable isolation turns a conflict into a failure that every agent, job and test must retry, while C:110's positive asks that one claim always succeeds. It stays the fallback below. **Locks on the edge's two ends only**: misses cycles longer than two (above). **`SKIP LOCKED` queue claiming**: not needed, since a claim names its work item. **The epoch kept only per claim or per token, with no global row** (A1.2 before this revision assumed a per-token epoch): a revocation would have to update every claim's or token's row, and an effect could read a value between those updates (Inf); binding tokens to the epoch is also rejected on its own ground (EV-C02-004 X-03). **CD's candidate** (CD §4.3 and §9 row 1; EV-C00-012 R03, D08, P7): lease fields inline on the work item, the lease bound to a session id from the hook input; its authority part was rejected in EV-C00-012 D08 (keep plan), and it states no concurrency form to weigh.
**Confirming test.** C's Concurrency test as W-C02-07 writes it. For this record to count the choice confirmed, that test includes: many connections claiming one work item at once, repeated; two opposing hard dependencies added at once, and a cycle of four closed by four edges added at once; a late effect by a claimant racing `revoke_claim` and a new hard dependency on its work item; and, over its repeated runs, the count of errors other than the expected refusals (deadlocks detected, serialization failures, lock timeouts), which is the "failure frequency" B:442 names. **Read by** W-C02-13 (the full run with breaks) and the stage close (W-C02-18). **If it fails:** a cycle that forms moves hard-dependency writes to serializable isolation with a bounded retry inside the function; a deadlock or a lost refusal changes the lock order; either goes through a Section 14 change of B section 8's row and a migration, before C02 closes.

**DM-02 Revision storage form. Decided.**
(a) Each family whose fields carry `revision` (Mission, Work, Finding, Artifact, Decision; later Source, the task definition and others) has one row per object for its identity and its state, and an append-only table that holds every revision, the current one included. A reference to a revision (principle 3) is a foreign key to that table. The current form of each family is a view that joins the two. State is not content: WorkStanding's axes, a claim's status and a decision's status are fields of the identity row, and each change produces its event (principle 2) without minting a revision. The revision table refuses update and delete to every role.
(b) Principle 3 asks that references carry the revision they refer to. With every revision in one table, each such reference is one foreign key the database checks, whichever revision it names. Content is stored once. Because revisions never change, the text Batu answered (DM-11) and a verdict's basis (N25) cannot change under them. "Query simplicity" (B:443) is met by one view per family.
(c) Doc: B principle 3, 3.3 (the axes), 3.11 (staleness), 3.17. Inf: size does not decide at the installation's scale (R1 section 4 counts, at `796c427`: 71 work items, 64 decision records, 187 verdicts, about 260 log entries and 194 notes); C04 task 6's capacity measurement reads it again.
(d) **Initial proposal** (a current table plus an appended history table): a reference to a past revision points into the history table and one to the current revision into the current table, so no single foreign key can check it, and every update copies the old row; this choice keeps the proposal's idea (state apart, history appended) and puts every revision, the current one too, in the appended table. **One table of revisions with no identity row**: every state change would either mint a revision or update a revision row in place, against principle 3. **Event sourcing** (state folded from events; close to the CD's journal, CD §9 row 2): a fold per read gives up the query simplicity B:443 names. **A temporal-tables extension**: not core PostgreSQL, its availability on Supabase was not read, and it is not needed. **Reopen if** C04's capacity measurement shows the revision tables near the plan's limit.

### 2.2 Choices assigned to C02 elsewhere

**DM-06 Claim expiry: computed, not applied by a job. Provisional.**
(a) `claim`, `ready_works()` and every effect's re-check compare `lease_expires_at` with the database's clock. A lapsed claim counts as no claim for readiness, is refused at every effect (F08 (c)), and is marked `expired`, with its event (`origin = rule`, DM-31), by the `claim` that next takes the work item. No expired-claims job is built.
(b) The effect's re-check must compare the lease with the clock anyway: a job that runs every few minutes leaves a window in which a lapsed claim is still `active`, and an effect in that window must still be refused (B:155; F08 (c)). Once the comparison is made in the three places, a job would only refresh a stored status that no C02 test or C03-start need reads. Without the job there is no scheduler dependency, no section 5 job in C02, and no T-77 outside check needed in C02 (N-092: "the expired-claims job in C02, if C02 applies expiry through it"); a lapsed work item is claimable at once, not at the next tick.
