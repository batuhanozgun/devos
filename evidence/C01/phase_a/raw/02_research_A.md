## Findings

**F1. The unit is a binding, not the agent.**
- EXP4/continuations/DQ01-…md L47-63 (§4): a missed result has rival explanations (information never acquired, a wrong view of it, information unused, wrong method or criterion, real domain inadequacy, missing access or permission, a faulty evaluation path). So competence is better attached to a specific combination of actor, method, information, environment and intended use. No schema is chosen.
- EXP4/MS01-SOUL L15 (P3) and L41: parts that exist separately do not make a ready configuration. A role is an instruction plus a real carrier, context, a tool and effect scope, and a qualification basis. A role name trains nothing.
- EXP4/artifacts/SY01-…md L38, L59: calling an actor an expert creates no competence, and a math checker is not a quality oracle for pedagogy or literature.
- Maturity: opinion (working hypothesis; self-reviewed, MS01 L163). Sub-question 1.
- Effect: limits the "agent alone" option in decision (i).

**F2. The accepted Foundation backs the binding.**
- ACT/A1 L83-135 (§4.1-4.3): an actor's competence, its environment's conditions and what it can actually do vary independently. An AI participant is not the same as its model, process, credential, tools or host, yet those decide what it can do.
- ACT/INDEX.md L39-44; ACT/FINDINGS-MAP.md L52: competence belongs to the Actors ground, evidence standing to Information, and review or certification to Work.
- Maturity: study (accepted 2026-09-05; reviewed adversarially by the same model only, not replicated independently, ACT/INDEX L55). Sub-questions 1 and 5.
- Effect: justifies the binding unit. It also implies that a qualification record touches three grounds.

**F3. The Foundation already has a grammar for qualification (not in the discovery record).**
- CS/KEY.md L18-20, L39-66, L68-89, "Composite Standing / Qualification" (a leaf, accepted, with bounded scope).
  - Core: the qualified focus with its bindings; a frame (criterion, intended use, scope, version); a basis; a result; and the dependencies its validity rests on.
  - Two optional layers are kept apart from the core and from each other: an assessment layer (evidence, method, evaluator, uncertainty and coverage) and a decision layer (authorised actor, accept or reject).
  - None of these may be collapsed into another: the standing holds; an assessment supports it; a decision recognises it; a record says it. Likewise verified, validated and accepted are different, and so are capable, actionable, authorised and technically reachable.
- CS/KEY L33-37: a removal test decides whether a standing really spans several grounds.
- CS/S3-EVALUATION-UNCERTAINTY-INVALIDATION.md L10-58: three layers. L121-148: stale, not applicable, not evaluated, unknown and false are distinct. L150-168: "unknown" describes our knowledge, not the object.
- EXP4/MS01-RESEARCH L138 ties the exploration's Qualification Case (QC) to this grammar, but says not every QC is cross-ground.
- Maturity: study (same-model adversarial closure, no replication, KEY L120-122; still accepted per lib:soul-foundations/STATE L36). Its NASA, JCGM and NIST holdouts date from 2026-09-05 and I did not re-check them. Sub-questions 5 and 1.
- Effect: justifies the content any qualification record must carry under decision (ii). It does not choose the physical shape.

**F4. Thresholds are tied to acts.**
- DQ01 L73-81 (§6), three levels:
  - worth trying: reasonable grounds justify a bounded trial, not use;
  - support for a named use: evidence of what the arrangement does under the relevant conditions. A label, training text, the authority of a source or self-declaration is not enough, and producing, discovering and evaluating are different competences;
  - acceptance of this result or effect: this output, this version, an authorised effect.
  - Before moving to a stronger level, the case must say which change reopens the verdict and which authority accepts it.
- CS/S3 L271-298: under NIST AI RMF and NASA TRL pressure, measured performance, validity for this use and a deploy decision are three things, and demonstration conditions (lab, relevant, operational) set the maturity.
- EXP4/continuations/DQ04-…md L88-95: for each verdict, the basis it needs and the shortcut that is not enough. A loaded skill or an expert label does not mean "usable here". One success, or a score that rose under a changed criterion, does not show benefit. Hand-picked cases do not show selection competence.
- Maturity: opinion (DQ01, DQ04) and study (S3). Sub-question 1.
- Effect: justifies graded thresholds under (i) and (iii).

**F5. What an unqualified agent may do.**
- MS01:
  - L33: the starting authority may allow drafts and bounded review, but not publication, new accounts or credentials, or changes to shared policy. Missing capacity is recorded as unavailable; no imaginary actor is planned.
  - L99: weak qualification opens a small qualification work item: real application plus a separate evaluation that fits the claim. One small success is not broad expertise, and a dependent effect waits.
  - L119-121: exploration is not blocked for lack of a final QC, but an effect needs its decision and QC.
- EXP4/continuations/T10-…md L39-51 (§5): creating an agent, its competence, its access and its acceptance for each effect are separate standings, and there is no global trusted flag. A new agent drafts in a bounded environment without live publishing power, and a scope is switched on only when shown.
- DQ04 L99: narrower or advisory-only use may fit, but a pilot label does not make an effect safe.
- Counter-evidence in the same library: DQ02 L48. Exploring is not harmless by default: sending private text out, asking real people, spending budget or changing production state each needs permission.
- Maturity: opinion. Sub-questions 4 and 1.
- Effect: limits "qualified before any act". It justifies gating effects and the admission of results instead.

**F6. Evidence paths when there is no answer key.**
- DQ01 L85-95 (§7):
  - Discovery and evaluation built from the same incomplete frame share its blindness.
  - The remedy is to change the evidence path materially: inspect real use, bring in another source or field, use known-good and known-bad cases, evaluate apart from the producer's account.
  - A different agent or model label alone does not remove a shared assumption, and a model's opinion cannot settle a strong claim about real reader or learner effect.
- DQ01 L97-101: no endless chain of evaluators. Use outside grounding, a valid source, a bounded check whose error sensitivity is shown, or an explicit authorised acceptance. Where none exists, the confidence claim shrinks; it is not hidden behind a new role.
- DQ02 L58-64: three review paths, each with a remaining blind spot: comparing the plan with the text, reading only the text in order, and a human editor or reader. The human path cannot be assumed, and without it model results must not be presented as human experience.
- DQ02 L70-80: common blindness also comes from the same question list, summary or success criterion. Ask why path B would catch what path A misses. A second same-model look still has value. Independence is a bounded argument, not a certificate.
- Maturity: opinion. Sub-question 2.
- Effect: limits model-graded evidence and justifies narrowing claims under (i).

**F7. LLM judges in practice (gstack).**
- Source: GS/testing-eval L110-120, L643-722 (§§19-21), L914-922 (V15-V16), L994-996.
- A judge score means only that the artefact met a rubric under that judge model and its calibration history. Repository comments report that a second model graded the same rubric more harshly, and the repository kept its judge model for that reason.
- A parser decides what a parser can; the judge handles only substance.
- Planted-bug evaluations with known ground truth and model-mediated matching are stronger than taste scoring but weaker than proof by execution.
- A judge of the same family as the generator shares its priors. It adds diversity, not independent verification, especially when nothing outside the rubric fixes the right answer.
- Maturity: synthesis in a completed study (pinned gstack 0d1bd56, v1.79, revalidated v1.80). The A/B result is an unverified first-party claim. Sub-questions 2 and 4.
- Effect: limits the validity of model-graded evaluation. It justifies recording the judge model inside the qualification.

**F9. Evaluator mechanics (Superpowers).**
- SP/S01:
  - L21: a no-guidance control and an old-versus-new comparison are different experiments. Adjusting the method on cases it already knows yields evidence about development, not about generalisation.
  - L37-41: separating the evaluator's role and inputs while it runs on the same model is not independence. A schema-valid row can answer the wrong obligation.
  - L47-58: qualification has several dimensions. Pass, fail and indeterminate are kept apart, and an instrument failure can coexist with a delivered answer.
  - L60-68: a better score can come from repairing the instrument, not from a better subject.
- SP/CORE.md L78: the limits of uncalibrated graders.

**F10. Transfer of the hidden-exam yardstick.**
- DQ02 L92-98:
  - A fair hidden test may hide where the defect is, or its answer, but not the legitimate basis for acceptance. That basis must be derivable from the purpose, the sources and the field.
  - A set made only of flawed examples rewards a system that flags everything. Add sound but unusual solutions, intended ambiguity and cases where the information is insufficient.
  - Rejecting a known broken case shows only limited sensitivity, and a case seen while changing the method cannot later count as validation.
- DQ01 L93 and EXP4/MS01-COVERAGE L34: cases already described cannot serve as hidden tests.
- DQ04 L36-50:
  - If the evaluator knows more than the real participant would, the verdict must keep that difference.
  - A transfer argument names which source conditions carried the result and which of them hold, differ or are unknown in the target.
  - An unknown material difference is not closed by success at the source. The evidence needed depends on the claim, the risk and the authority; there is no fixed count of successes.
- Maturity: opinion. Sub-question 3.
- Effect: limits transfer of plan 7.3. It frames the replacement for an answer key as an acceptance basis derived from purpose and sources, plus balanced known cases.
