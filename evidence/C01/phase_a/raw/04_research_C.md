2. **HD1, the unit.**
   - **Library:** agentic-os-search@941f027d:
     - lib:EXP-004/DQ01 L63: the candidate unit is the combination of actor, method, information, environment and use.
     - lib:EXP-004/MS01-SOUL L15 (P3): parts that each exist do not make a work ready. L41: a new role is instruction, carrier, context, tool and effect scope, and a basis for its competence.
     - lib:actors-ground/INDEX L40: competence is distinct from what the environment allows.
     - lib:superpowers/S01 L49: a subject's identity is distinct from how it actually behaves at run time.
   - **Outside:**
     - Feng, McDonald, Zhang, https://arxiv.org/html/2506.12469v2 (v2, 2025-07-28), section 4: a proposed autonomy certificate binds model, prompts, tools and operating environment. A change to any of them invalidates it.
     - NIST AI RMF MEASURE 2.5, https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/playbook/measure/ (no page date; read 2026-10-06): validity must be shown, and the limits of generalizing beyond the tested conditions documented.
     - Mitchell et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993 (2019-01-14): evaluation results travel with their intended context of use.
   - **DevOS already has part of this:** its Competence record (Ek_B L269) binds a role to task class, model and settings, and tools and context method. It lacks the information-sources, operating-environment and named-use dimensions.
   - **Effect: justifies** the configuration as the unit. Nothing I read supports "the agent alone"; that absence is limited to these sources. [S/I]

3. **HD2, model-graded evidence.**
   - **Outside:**
     - Zheng et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685 (v4, 2023-12-24): strong judges agree with human preferences above 80% on open chat, with position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases.
     - Krumdick et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05061 (submitted 2025-03-07): judges match experts mainly on questions they can answer themselves; expert-written reference answers largely fix this.
     - Panickssery, Bowman, Feng, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076 (2024-04-15): self-preference grows with self-recognition, and the link is causal.
   - **Library agrees:** S01 L37 and L41 (a report that fits the schema can still be a wrong judgement); DQ01 L89 (a different agent or model label does not remove a shared wrong assumption).
   - **Effect: changes.** Where neither the user nor the model can answer, a judge without references is at its weakest. Model grading without human-grounded references there supports at most "worth trying" (DQ01 L75), not "fit for a named use". [S/I]

4. **HD2, a second model family.**
   - **Outside:**
     - Goel et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04313 (revised 2025-06-12): judges favour models similar to themselves, and mistakes grow more alike as capability rises.
     - Kim et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962 (2025-06-09): on one leaderboard, when two models are both wrong they agree about 60% of the time; large models correlate even across providers.
     - **Counter:** Verga et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796 (2024-05-01): a panel drawn from separate model families beats one large judge, shows less intra-model bias, and is cheaper.
   - **Library:** lib:multi-agent-patterns/CONVERSATION-SYNTHESIS L349-356 (degrees of separation; a different model can share a wrong specification); lib:anthropic-playbook/04-FALSIFIERS L170-193 (F6).
   - **Effect: limits** plan U-3 (L1127). A second family reduces shared blind spots but does not remove them. Its result should be recorded at a stated independence level, not as independent verification. [S/I]

5. **HD2 and HD7, cost and privacy of a second model in SOUL.**
   - **Outside:** Gemini API terms, https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms (effective 2026-03-23, updated 2026-04-28, read 2026-10-06):
     - content sent to the unpaid tier is used to improve the provider's products and may be read by human reviewers;
     - users are told not to send sensitive, confidential or personal information to it;
     - clients offered to users in the EEA, Switzerland or the UK must use the paid tier.
   - The rate-limits page (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits, updated 2026-09-02) says limits are not guaranteed and are shown per account.
   - **Effect: changes.** For DevOS, B2 already limits the free tier to fake and public content (plan L579, L1176). For SOUL, a user's work is usually private, so a free-tier evaluation conflicts with criterion 3 (L192) and is closed in some regions. The path becomes paid, which is the user's cost decision in SOUL and Batu's in DevOS.
   - **Uncertain:** whether a self-hosted SOUL running on a user's own key counts as "offering clients" under that clause. [S/I]

6. **HD2, non-experts judging with help, and real experts.**
   - **Outside:**
     - Bowman et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03540 (2022-11-11): non-experts helped by a model beat both the model alone and themselves alone.
     - Khan et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06782 (final 2024-07-25): with debate, non-expert human judges reached 88% accuracy against 60% for the naive baseline.
     - Kenton et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04622 (2024-07-12): debate beats direct question-answering only when the judge lacks information the debaters have; results elsewhere are mixed.
     - NIST MEASURE 1.3 and 2.5: independent assessors and experts in the context of use.
   - **Library:** multi-agent-patterns CONVERSATION-SYNTHESIS L460-474: a human's authority and a human's error-finding are different, and approval can become a rubber stamp.
   - **Plan:** U-3 (L1127) has no independent human expert outside Batu's fields; K-11 item 3 (L455) lists his expert fields.
   - **Effect: changes and limits.**
     - A user's approval in a field they cannot judge is an act of authority, not qualification evidence.
     - Structured adversarial help can raise a non-expert's judgement for some task types. This fits SOUL's "inform enough to decide" (L141), but only conditionally.
     - A real expert grounds the judgement best (Krumdick), but costs. In DevOS that means Batu's time in his own fields, which is his decision (K-11 item 7); outside them no expert exists. [S/I]

7. **HD2, outcome checks and reused test sets.**
   - **Outside:**
     - τ-bench, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045 (2024-06-17): grading compares the end state with an annotated goal state.
     - Anthropic, 2026-01-09, "Demystifying evals for AI agents" (www.anthropic.com, Engineering): grade outcomes rather than fixed paths; production monitoring is real-world ground truth within layered checks; model graders need frequent calibration against expert humans; test suites saturate and need upkeep.
     - Dwork et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02629 (2015-09-25): reusing a holdout set adaptively overfits to it.
   - **Library:** lib:autoresearch/META L95-97 (a planned study, signals only); lib:anthropic-playbook/04-FALSIFIERS L376-399 (F13); plan L815.
   - **Effect:** **justifies** outcome checks wherever a checkable end state exists. **Limits** a small runtime case set: once it has been reused against a configuration that is being tuned, it no longer counts as held out. [S/I]

8. **HD3, thresholds and the effect gate.**
   - **Library:**
     - DQ01 L73-81: three thresholds (worth trying; fit for a named use; this result accepted), and what a stronger verdict must answer.
     - MS01 draft L99 (K4): weak competence opens a small qualification task, and dependent effects wait.
     - MS01 draft L119-121 (K6): discovery is not blocked by a missing final check, but any effect needs the decision or check it requires.
     - lib:anthropic-playbook/03-SOUL L400-411 and L512-516: autonomy depends on the actor, the action, the environment, the evidence and the gate, not on one level.
     - multi-agent-patterns L478-485: a human stops irreversible, financial and external actions.
   - **Outside:**
     - Feng et al.: five user roles, with autonomy treated as a design choice separate from capability.
     - Greenblatt et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942 (revised 2024-07-23): a trusted weaker monitor plus scarce trusted human effort can keep an untrusted model safe without proving it trustworthy. Its threat is deliberate subversion, so it carries over only partly to SOUL's competence problem. [I]
     - Anthropic 2026 and τ-bench: when consistency matters, the bar is pass^k (all k trials succeed). In τ-bench's retail domain the best 2024 agent reached pass^8 below 25%.
   - **Effect: changes** the binary premise into permission tiered by effect. It also adds that "fit for a named use" should be measured as consistency across trials, not best-of-k. [S/I]

9. **HD4, invalidation.**
   - **Library:** lib:anthropic-playbook/04-FALSIFIERS L484-498 (F17: a model change triggers evals; the authority path behaved unexpectedly after an upgrade); the playbook's META L50; lib:gstack/CORE L467-475 (F5: an old artifact taken for current truth); DQ01 L81 (what reopens a verdict).
   - **DevOS:** Ek_B 3.15 L273 sets retest_required when the model, role text, tool set or context method changes.
   - **Outside:**
     - Chen, Zaharia, Zou, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09009 (revised 2023-10-31): the same named service changed materially within three months.
     - Feng et al., section 4: a change to the specification or the environment invalidates the certificate.
   - **Effect: justifies** change-triggered invalidation, and **limits** it. Drift under an unchanged model name escapes a rule that fires only on declared changes, so pinned versions or periodic re-probes are needed. A qualification on one provider does not carry to another (criterion 4, L193). [S/I]

12. **Transfer test of plan 7.3 and 7.4.**
    - **Holds anywhere:** known-good and known-bad examples (L814), renewal (L815), self-assessment as a hint only (L816), traps for thinking abilities (L817).
    - **Needs conditions only DevOS has:** an exam environment whose credential alone reaches the answer keys, and exam tasks that do not look like exams (L812; Ek_B L273).     - **Inference:** the hidden, keyed part moves to DevOS's validation of SOUL's method before release (criterion 33's comparison). Runtime evidence in the user's account is recorded at its real, lower independence level. [N]

**Counter-evidence and alternatives:**
- Juries (finding 4), debate and sandwiching (finding 6) support model-assisted judging under conditions.
- AI control (finding 8) is an alternative frame: contain and monitor rather than qualify first.
- SOUL may itself find expert actors for the user (purpose 1.1; DQ01 L71: a human's contribution and its cost are kept visible).

**For the decision:**
- The unit should be the configuration plus its use.
- Model grading without references cannot qualify an agent for a named use in a field nobody present can judge.
- The user's approval is not evidence.
- Permission should be tiered by effect, with a consistency bar for effects.
- Invalidation should fire on declared changes and on silent drift.
- A second-model path in SOUL is a paid, privacy-bound user decision.
- The record-shape choice stays open.

## Gaps and wrong assumptions
1. **The discovery's frame merges three things.** Criterion 33's thinking floor and its method-comparison clause are read as a per-agent runtime hidden exam (plan L218, L1356; finding 1). This is material.
2. **The assumption "model-graded evaluation is valid where the user cannot judge" is wrong** without references (Krumdick 2025; Goel 2025; Kim 2025).
3. **The assumption "every agent must be qualified before any act" is contradicted** (DQ01 L73-81; MS01 L119; playbook 03 L400-411; Feng 2025).
4. **The assumption "a qualification survives a model or provider change" is wrong** (Chen 2023; playbook F17; Ek_B L273). A further gap: drift under an unchanged model name escapes a rule that fires only on declared changes.
5. **The prerequisite "a second-model gateway"** is not wrong for DevOS's high-impact decisions on public or fake content. For SOUL user work it collides with criterion 3 and the regional terms (Gemini terms 2026-04-28).
6. **The "real expert" path has no owner or cost.** In DevOS it means only Batu, only in his fields (U-3 L1127; K-11 items 3 and 7).
7. **One citation is mapped loosely.** The discovery cites MS01-COVERAGE L40 (P6) for when qualification may be small or deferred; the closer passages are MS01 draft L99 and L119. Minor.
8. **None of the library's thresholds has measured values** (DQ01 L95). MS01 is the author's own review, not an independent one (COVERAGE L3, L117).
