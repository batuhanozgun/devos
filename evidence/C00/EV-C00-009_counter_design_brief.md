# Brief: independent counter-design of DevOS (W-C00-09, plan C00 step 5)

You are the Counter-designer. Another design of the same thing exists; you do not see it, and it is not shown to you by any route. When you are done, the two are compared, and every difference is closed with a reason. Design from the goal, the decisions of the owner (Batu), the criteria and the platform facts below, and nothing else. Do not try to guess the other design, and do not try to please anyone: the owner's goal is a SOUL that works, not agreement. The comparison will especially ask whether the scope of the data model's record kinds and of the roles follows "start simple".

## 1. Goal

**SOUL** (Batu's decision, verbatim in English):

> SOUL is a structure that, in work where the user's expertise falls short, closes this gap; that discovers the work, the knowledge, the actors and the working conditions, and combines and manages them as a working system; that brings the user into the process only where the user must make a decision, informing them as much as they need to be able to decide; that, when needed, adapts its own working capacity in a controlled way.

"Informing" is not lecturing. When setting a goal, a preference or a budget, the user may have incomplete knowledge of what the work requires. SOUL does not treat these constraints as fixed input: for a constraint that conflicts with what the work requires, it presents the high-quality option, its purpose, its benefit, its cost and its alternative; the user makes the decision. SOUL will be open source, and others will be able to set it up with their own accounts.

**DevOS** is the working system that develops SOUL. It discovers by itself which work SOUL needs, and researches, designs, implements and tests it. Neither Batu nor any plan chooses SOUL's first real work; the team finds it. DevOS is also the first instance of SOUL, a SOUL whose subject is known in advance: its layer for context, memory, finding information, tracking work and testing is not scaffolding to be thrown away but the first form of the SOUL core, and its success or failure in developing SOUL is the first evidence for the SOUL method.

**DevOS is successful (Batu's decision) when it shows these capabilities in real work**, not when a database runs, role files exist or tasks are passed on: (1) discovering the right work; (2) researching well; (3) making reasoned decisions; (4) testing its mistakes and taking them all the way to their general rule; (5) carrying long and composite work forward without losing its integrity; (6) actually using the accumulated research; (7) doing all this without needing Batu to carry messages or do technical maintenance.

## 2. Batu's principles (his decision; they bind your design)

1. Quality is not lowered for convenience or cheapness. Default effort and depth are high; less is done with a rationale.
2. What is expensive or complex is not counted as better. The simpler solution that meets the same requirement at the same quality is preferred.
3. If a fee, a different tool or a change to a constraint is needed, it is presented to Batu with its gain, rationale, alternative and cost. An expense is neither accepted on his behalf nor is the need silently cut back.
4. Listing requirements is not enough; for every requirement, the mechanism that meets it, how the parts work together, the conditions, the failure case and the way of testing it are written down.
5. Start from the need, not the technology. Material alternatives are compared before the choice.
6. What has been designed but not verified is kept apart from what has no solution. An unsolved design problem is not shown as solved by saying "it will be tested later".
7. What matters is not the number of tests but what they prove. Every test must catch the wrong solution, allow the right one and truly represent the claimed capability.
8. Work proceeds in stages, but the goal is not shrunk. Critical uncertainties are dealt with before the large pieces of work that depend on them.
9. Finding the gaps is the designer's and the builder's responsibility; only the decisions that belong to Batu go to him, with the information needed.
10. The real measure is whether DevOS can actually develop SOUL. More records and checks do not mean SOUL is advancing.
11. Frame blindness is the greatest danger for DevOS and for SOUL. When a design gets stuck at a limit and starts producing new mechanisms as the solution, the frame itself is questioned first. Technical decisions, even large ones, are made by the team with their rationale; only Batu's own decisions go to him.
12. Every platform behaviour relied on is read from the current official documentation, with its date; a secondary source does not count as verified.
13. Every channel through which an agent can produce an effect in the world (database, GitHub, connectors, network, second model, scheduled jobs) is kept in one list; each gets a limit and a negative test.

## 3. Criteria (the contract; numbers as in the source)

SOUL: (1) the definition above; (2) open source: others set it up with their own accounts, SOUL does not depend on Batu's infrastructure; (3) user data stays in the user's own space, no private information leaks into shared learning; (4) provider independence: SOUL can also work with a model other than Claude, shown by a real test before it is claimed.

DevOS: (5) findability: every piece of information has a catalogue record and three kinds of search: keyword, semantic, relation; (6) awareness: every agent starts work with the state brief of that moment; (7) faithfulness to sources: every claim sourced, contradictions marked, review done with a different view of the information; (8) machine rules: the system, not the agent, enforces the rules; (9) flow without Batu: roles connect to one another without Batu, contributions are actually used; (10) interruption: unfinished work resumes from the record, old authority does not come back; (11) scale: works across thousands of files without reading everything; (12) dead ends: the knowledge in a failed attempt is kept, the reason recorded; (13) flexible roles: a new role can be set up, its competence tested, dropped when not needed; (14) user model: what Batu knows and what he must decide are tracked; (15) effort: safeguards complete in every piece of work; effort depth high by default; reducing it needs a rationale and another role's approval; the user's expertise can shorten research but never removes verification of changeable information, the scan of alternatives and, for high-impact decisions, research in external sources; (16) constraints can be questioned: for a constraint that conflicts with the work, the high-quality option, purpose, benefit, cost and alternative are presented; (17) current research: model knowledge not trusted blindly, changeable information verified; (18) staying tied to the purpose: the main goal audited regularly, every new piece of process shows that it serves SOUL's progress; (19) learning: lessons recorded, suitable ones selected, method changes tested without breaking old good behaviour; (20) test data: personal and business data never used in tests, only fake data; DevOS's own research library may be used in measurements; (28) an error is distinguished from a capability gap: every error classified as symptom, failure class or capability gap, repair and regression test at the most general level found; (29) accumulated knowledge from outside sources is mandatory: for high-impact decisions, known solutions and counterexamples in other fields are researched; (30) measurement is independent: competence measured with exams the role under test cannot see, no role can approve its own change; (31) private sources are protected: content of the private library is not carried into public repositories; (32) common thinking standard and role preparation: every role carries the same common thinking floor; a new role is prepared not just with a name and a task sentence but with a knowledge map, methods, tools, known failure classes, examples and a hidden exam, rebuilt at every session opening, every contribution request and on return from an interruption; the smallness of the work is no reason to skip the expert assessment, though the assessment may result in little work; (33) agent quality in SOUL is a floor, not a ceiling: SOUL's own agents and those it creates carry a thinking standard at least as high as DevOS's roles; how SOUL prepares agents is found through DevOS's research, design and testing, not by copying DevOS's method; (34) mechanism against frame blindness: DevOS writes down the premises it relies on, questions the frame first when a design gets stuck, and for major design decisions compares with an independent counter-design that does not see the existing design; also a requirement for SOUL.

Environment and Batu's role (his decision): (21) his role is purpose, decision, acceptance; no carrying messages, no maintenance; (22) it works while his computer is off; (23) phone: the Claude app and a backup channel certain to get through; decisions in a single list, in plain Turkish, with short options; (24) its own maintenance: backup, monitoring and audit run regularly; what cannot be solved drops into the decision list.

Infrastructure (his decisions): (25) Claude Max $200 plan, extra usage off, no Anthropic API key in the environment; (26) live state in Batu's personal Supabase account, agents with restricted permissions; the Supabase MCP is not used by the running system; (27) GitHub repositories as below; branch protection covering administrators, automatic checks on every pull request, secret scanning on; triggers do not respond to events from outside accounts.

## 4. Batu's other decisions and constraints

- **Repositories and their writers** (single-writer principle: each repository has one writer; others only read): `devos` (public; DevOS's own repository; may hold DevOS's own synthesis, source identifiers, design documents and Batu's decisions, never research-library text or conversation transcripts; the leak risk of a public repository is accepted with code-based checks before the first public write); `soul-system` (public; SOUL's released product, written only by DevOS's release flow); `devos-evals` (private; exams; never attached to the sessions of the roles under test); `devos-backup` (private; backups and library transfer jobs); `agentic-os-search` (private research library, about 2,300 files, about 18.5 MB of text, mostly Turkish, maintained by another assistant with Batu's approval; DevOS only reads it, never writes); thirteen old experiment repositories (read only; history and counterexamples; their "current/next" statements are not instructions).
- **Machine account:** the system's GitHub identity is a separate machine account with write access to `devos`, `soul-system`, `devos-evals` and (technically) the library; nothing is ever written to the library.
- **Language:** everything inside DevOS (files, records, agent-to-agent communication) is English; everything to Batu is Turkish; his own words are kept verbatim in Turkish with an English interpretation.
- **Money:** Supabase free plan until a measurement shows the limit is near; then the plan level goes to Batu before any scope is narrowed. A second model family for provider-independence tests and second opinions: Gemini API free tier, only public content, through a single gateway. No paid feature is enabled without his decision.
- **Approval:** technical correctness and the technical approval of high-impact changes come from independent review, not from Batu. Batu decides only purpose, scope, money, his accounts and other work, and acceptance of results; a change touching one of these goes to him as a decision with options, recommendation, what he needs to know, and what happens if he does not answer. His silence is never approval.
- **Batu himself:** expert in finance, FP&A, reporting, SAP, Microsoft Fabric, Power BI; not an agent engineer; uses his phone; wants short, plain Turkish, one topic at a time; does not want to be asked "may I read this, may I write that".

## 5. Platform facts (as of early October 2026; status given)

- **Claude Code on the web (cloud sessions):** each session runs in a sandboxed container that can be paused between turns and replaced; uncommitted work can be lost (documented). A session's repositories are chosen when it starts (documented). Sessions can run in-process subagents (same identity and same hooks as the session; observed) and scripted parallel subagent workflows (observed in one session; uses more of the usage allowance). Repository hooks from `.claude/settings.json` run in single-repository sessions (observed); whether they load in multi-repository sessions is doubtful (documented ambiguity). Account connectors (mail, calendar, files) reach cloud sessions unless a hook blocks them (observed). In "Accept edits" mode, tools that start sessions, schedule wake-ups or attach repositories need a person's interactive approval, which nobody can give in an unattended session (observed several times); in "auto" mode a classifier judges each step and may refuse with a label only (observed). A cloud session does not resume by itself after a usage limit resets; a user message resumes it (documented and observed).
- **Usage:** Max $200 plan with 5-hour and weekly windows, shared with Batu's own Claude use (documented). Parallel subagents cost several times the tokens of one agent (secondary sources).
- **Routines:** start a cloud session on a schedule or by API trigger (documented, research preview); Max allows 15 routine runs per day (official announcement); all account connectors are attached to a routine by default and can be removed (documented); a routine disables itself if its GitHub connection is broken for more than 72 hours (documented); one-off scheduled runs reportedly do not count against the limit but cannot be created from inside a cloud session (documented, unverified).
- **Claude Code Projects** (beta, September 2026): a coordinator conversation plus parallel worker sessions in one environment, about 200 new sessions a day; may not be enabled on Batu's account (unverified).
- **Supabase free plan:** PostgreSQL with row-level security, database functions, pgvector, Edge Functions, scheduled jobs, outbound HTTP; 500 MB database; a project pauses after a week without use; no automatic backups (documented). The secret (service) key bypasses row-level security (documented). The only built-in embedding model is English (documented). Two projects exist: a live one and a test one.
- **GitHub Free:** branch protection is not available for private repositories; 2,000 Actions minutes a month for private repositories; Actions logs of public repositories are public (documented). Pull-request authors cannot approve their own pull requests (documented).
- **Gemini API free tier:** content sent may be used by Google for product improvement; quotas can be low (a few dozen requests a day on some models) (documented, secondary).

## 6. What to design

Design **DevOS's way of working** and **the scope of its data model**, from first principles, for the period when DevOS runs and develops SOUL (not the installation that builds DevOS). Start simple: add a part only when a stated need, criterion or decision above requires it, and say which.

Answer at least:
1. **Work:** how DevOS discovers, admits, orders, carries out, reviews and accepts work; how "ready to start" is decided; how long and composite work keeps its integrity; how a session ends and the next one continues from the record (criteria 6, 9, 10, 12, 18).
2. **Actors:** which roles (or other units of responsibility) DevOS needs at the start, each traced to a demonstrated need; how they are prepared (criterion 32); how roles are added and retired (13); which work runs inside one session and which needs a separate session, environment or identity, and why; what "independent" means for review and for measurement (7, 30) on this platform.
3. **Rules and authority:** how "the system, not the agent, enforces the rules" (8) is achieved on this platform; how authority is checked and how old authority is kept from coming back after an interruption or a restore (10); the effect channels and their limits (principle 13); private content and the public repository (31).
4. **Knowledge:** how the library and DevOS's own findings are made findable and actually used (5, 7, 11, 17, 29), with the language situation (mostly Turkish library, English agents).
5. **Batu:** what reaches him, through which channel, in what form, and how DevOS knows an answer really came from him (14, 16, 21, 23).
6. **Continuity and capacity:** how work continues while his computer is off and within the usage limits and the 15 routine runs a day (22, 24, 25); what happens at a usage limit.
7. **Learning and frame checks:** how lessons, method changes, capability gaps and frame reviews work (19, 28, 34), and how DevOS keeps itself from growing process for its own sake (18, principle 10).
8. **Data model scope:** the minimal set of record kinds (what each holds, who writes it, which criterion needs it) that DevOS needs from the start, and what can wait until a need appears; say for each what goes wrong without it.
9. **Tests:** for each major mechanism, one test written before results that would fail if the mechanism were absent.

For each major choice, write its **premises**: the premise; where it comes from (Batu's decision, a platform fact, this brief, or your own assumption); whether it still holds; and whether you would choose it again from scratch. Mark what is designed but unverified, and what has no solution yet.
