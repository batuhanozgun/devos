# Environment — Vertical Synthesis

**Status:** bounded independent foundation research result; ready for user evaluation  
**Vertical revision:** `environment-foundation-2026-09-01-r1`  
**Date:** 2026-09-01  
**Architecture status:** research finding only; not an accepted SOUL ontology, module or data model

## 1. Research question

The independent pass asked:

> Does a coherent first-order problem region remain around boundary-relative external/world state, operating conditions, resources, affordances, tools/infrastructure, external systems/interfaces and observable/changeable conditions that cannot be represented without material loss as Records, Work, Actors or relations/qualifiers among them?

The pass deliberately allowed the answer to confirm, split, rename or dissolve `Environment`.

## 2. Bounded answer

**Yes, an independent foundation problem region remains — but the initial fourth-leg framing requires a major qualification.**

The evidence does **not** validate `Environment` as:
- a mutually exclusive fourth object class beside Records, Work and Actors;
- a bucket containing all tools/resources/context;
- a required fourth architecture module;
- a fixed boundary-independent collection of external entities.

The strongest current result is narrower:

> **A controlled working system operates relative to a selected system-of-interest and boundary within a world whose current states, conditions and dynamics may change independently of the system's present Records, Work and Actor facts; those conditions can affect actionability and outcomes, may be only partially observable, and are coupled to the system through sensing, action, interfaces and resources.**

`Environment` remains a workable research label for this region. `Operating world` or `operating situation` may be less misleading candidate framings, but neither is accepted terminology.

## 3. First-order distinctions preserved by the research

### 3.1 System-of-interest and boundary are load-bearing

Something is not intrinsically an Environment entity. A service, tool, person, infrastructure component or subsystem can be internal or external depending on:
- what system is being analyzed/controlled;
- the purpose and level of analysis;
- coupling and lifecycle context.

Environment membership is therefore boundary-relative.

### 3.2 Live world state is not the same thing as its representation

When material, keep distinct:
- actual/current world or process state;
- observations/measurements of that state;
- Actor/system beliefs or inferred state;
- persistent Records/assertions about state;
- models/assumptions about environment dynamics.

Partial observability, noise, latency and unobserved change make these differences operationally real.

### 3.3 Environment includes dynamics, not only static context

External disturbances, other systems/Actors, markets, regulations, resource availability and physical conditions can change during Work. The environment problem therefore includes processes/transition dynamics and not just a context snapshot.

### 3.4 Actionability is relational

`Actor can do X` is not a complete situated claim.

Actual actionability can depend jointly on:
- Actor capability/competence;
- authority/permission where relevant;
- current external condition;
- available interface/resource/tool;
- Work purpose/requirements;
- time and situation.

Affordance/actionability is therefore better preserved as a relation or derived condition than as an intrinsic property of either Actor or Environment object.

### 3.5 Work state is not world state

Work can be blocked, ready, failed, complete or recovering because of an external condition, but these statuses are projections/consequences. One external condition can affect multiple Work units and can exist independently of their lifecycles.

Work can also produce externally visible partial effects, so the world after execution/failure need not equal Work's internal status or the pre-Work world.

### 3.6 Tools/resources/infrastructure are roles under a boundary

A concrete entity can function as:
- internal component;
- tool/mediator;
- resource;
- infrastructure;
- external system/interface;
- enabling/support system;
- constituent of a wider coupled Actor/system.

The research found no basis for making `tool`, `resource` or `infrastructure` an intrinsic Environment class.

### 3.7 Other Actors can contribute to the environment without ceasing to be Actors

Multi-agent/shared-world cases reject mutually exclusive classification. A counterparty/agent retains identity, authority and attribution semantics as an Actor while its actions may form part of another system's external dynamics.

## 4. Strongest non-collapse rules

Later reasoning should not silently collapse:

- world/environment state → Context;
- world state → Observation;
- observation → Belief;
- belief/assertion → Record;
- Actor capability → current actionability;
- Work readiness/failure → underlying external condition;
- tool/resource/infrastructure → intrinsic Environment entity;
- external system → non-Actor;
- system boundary → fixed natural fact;
- environment → static metadata.

## 5. The null hypothesis result

The most important test was whether Environment can disappear into Records + Work + Actors + relations.

### 5.1 Strong reduction fails

Hold Actor, Work and current Record facts fixed; change only an external condition such as:
- network/API availability;
- physical accessibility;
- temperature/radiation;
- shared resource capacity;
- regulatory or market state;
- another system's state/action.

Feasible actions, risks and outcomes can change.

Therefore a model containing only the three verticals' local facts loses a load-bearing variable.

### 5.2 Relation-only representation remains viable

If a later relation fabric can represent relations to external states/conditions/resources/interfaces, it may not need a dedicated Environment module or fourth object bucket.

But this does not eliminate the foundation problem. It represents it through boundary-relative world-state relata and interaction relations.

Thus:

- **fourth foundation problem region:** currently supported at bounded research level;
- **fourth mutually exclusive ontology leg:** not supported;
- **fourth architecture module/component:** not established and not decided.

## 6. Runtime/technical environment versus physical/social/domain environment

The research tested whether execution/runtime substrate should split into a different first-order family.

Current bounded judgment: **no split is required at foundation level.**

Technical runtime, physical surroundings, social/institutional conditions and external business/system conditions have radically different domain vocabularies, but they share the higher-level problem:

- states/processes relative to a system boundary;
- external/supporting conditions that can vary;
- interfaces/resources that mediate feasible interaction;
- disturbances/constraints/opportunities affecting outcomes;
- imperfect observation and model mismatch.

This is a convergence of problem structure, not a proposal to store them in one generic table/schema.

## 7. Context's place after the research

`Context` should not be used as a synonym for Environment.

A useful current distinction is:

- **world/environment:** states/processes/conditions relative to a system boundary;
- **context:** which facts/relations become relevant to an Actor/Work/interaction in a situation and how that relevance is established;
- **Records:** persistent representations needed to preserve/recover claims/observations/context/history.

This is an analytical distinction, not final architecture ownership.

## 8. Recurrence across levels

The problem appears structurally reusable wherever a bounded working system interacts with something beyond its current controlled boundary:

- the present SOUL research arrangement has ChatGPT/GitHub/tool/service operating conditions;
- Development OS will operate within repositories, runtimes, tooling, stakeholders and external systems;
- SOUL itself will have deployment/runtime and external interaction conditions;
- task-specific systems will have domain-specific physical, digital, social or institutional operating worlds.

This supports the **plausibility** of recurrence, but does not independently verify the stronger cross-level `same grammar` hypothesis. The concrete boundary and environmental facts can differ radically at each level.

## 9. What the research falsified or weakened

### Rejected / weakened

- `Environment = everything outside` without an explicit system-of-interest.
- `Environment = context`.
- `Environment = tool/resource bucket`.
- `Actor capability includes all situated feasibility`.
- `world state = what is recorded/known`.
- `runtime environment is necessarily a separate first-order family`.
- `Records + Work + Actors alone make external state unnecessary`.
- `Records + Work + Actors + Environment are naturally four mutually exclusive object classes`.

### Survives

A distinct problem of boundary-relative operating-world state/dynamics and interaction conditions survives reduction attempts.

## 10. Candidate minimum analytical envelope

A later system analysis may need to answer, only when material:

1. **System of interest:** what is currently inside the controlled/analyzed system?
2. **Boundary:** what is outside, and why is the boundary drawn there?
3. **World state/conditions:** which external/current facts matter to protected use?
4. **Dynamics:** what can change independently or through interaction?
5. **Interfaces/resources:** through what surfaces/resources can the system sense, act or depend?
6. **Observation:** what can actually be known/measured, with what delay/uncertainty?
7. **Actionability:** which Actor/Work actions are possible under current conditions?
8. **Effects:** how do actions and exogenous dynamics change the world?
9. **Contextual relevance:** which of these facts are relevant to the current situation and why?

This is not a mandatory schema or field list.

## 11. Evidence base and robustness

The pass drew from materially different traditions:
- systems engineering and Concepts of Operations;
- HCI/context theory;
- ecological psychology/affordances;
- decision theory/RL/POMDP/POSG;
- control systems;
- access control;
- container/runtime specifications;
- Web of Things interfaces;
- infrastructure/distributed-system alternatives;
- environmental ontology;
- OECD external-environment measurement.

Source mapping is in `../evidence/SOURCE-LEDGER.md`.

A late omission/countercheck probed physical hazards, social/institutional conditions, hidden state, non-stationarity, multi-agent cases, scarce resources, tools/infrastructure and the current research operating arrangement. These mapped back to the established distinctions rather than opening a new first-order family.

The completeness check is **same-model adversarial self-review, not independent verification**.

## 12. Bounded-completion judgment

Environment is **sufficiently researched for the current independent foundation purpose and ready for user evaluation**.

Why stop:
- serious rival framings were tested;
- the preferred initial four-leg framing was materially revised rather than confirmed;
- a direct dissolution/null test was run;
- late omission probes increasingly mapped to existing distinctions;
- load-bearing claims have source-recovery paths;
- remaining uncertainty is mostly refinement or cross-vertical composition/ownership.

Reopen only on explicit direction, a material later falsifier, or a later authorised synthesis exposing a decision-critical Environment distinction the current corpus cannot support.

## 13. Architecture boundary

This synthesis does **not** decide:

- number of architecture components/modules;
- whether Environment becomes a named SOUL concept;
- relation/control fabric design;
- data model/storage technology;

The immediate next authorised state after this bounded research result is **user evaluation**. Broad cross-vertical composition/relations or architecture work remains unauthorised until explicitly directed.
