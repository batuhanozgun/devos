# Environment — Initial Research Orientation

**Status:** active working orientation; not evidence; not synthesis  
**Date:** 2026-09-01

## 1. Established context

The following is established by current project state and explicit user authorisation, not by Environment research evidence:

- `Environment` is the current authorised independent foundation research line.
- `Environment` is only a working research label and is not an accepted fourth leg, primitive, module or ontology class.
- Records, Work and Actors are sufficiently complete and user-evaluated for the current foundation purpose, but their substantive findings are constraints/context rather than Environment axioms.
- The current research must test whether any independent first-order distinctions remain after Records, Work and Actors are accounted for.
- Rival framings and falsifiers must be actively tested; the task is not to confirm a preferred four-leg model.
- Cross-vertical composition/relations research, SOUL architecture, a final data model and implementation are not authorised by this pass.
- After bounded Environment research, the next required gate is user evaluation.

## 2. Falsifiable research question

> Does a coherent first-order problem region remain around boundary-relative external/world state, operating conditions, resources, affordances, tools/infrastructure, external systems/interfaces and observable/changeable conditions that cannot be represented without material loss as Records, Work, Actors or relations/qualifiers among them?

The fourth-leg hypothesis is weakened or falsified if the material distinctions can be preserved without loss by existing verticals plus typed relations/context qualifiers, or if `Environment` repeatedly decomposes into several unrelated concerns with no useful common foundation.

## 3. Weak working hypotheses

These hypotheses create discriminating research directions. They are not conclusions.

H1. Some facts relevant to successful Work are neither Actor-intrinsic capabilities nor Work state nor persistent Records; they may concern the current state and conditions of a world external to the selected system boundary.

H2. Environment may be boundary-relative rather than an intrinsic type. The same entity may be internal system component at one analytical level and external environment at another.

H3. Resources and affordances may be relational: what is usable or possible can depend jointly on actor capability, work purpose, current world state, access/interface conditions and time.

H4. Execution/runtime substrate may expose first-order feasibility and behavior conditions—resource limits, isolation, network/storage availability, platform interfaces, configuration and external services—that cannot be reduced safely to a scalar Actor capability.

H5. Observation and externally visible effects may require a distinction between changing/observing world state and persisting a Record about that state.

H6. A useful Environment region may still dissolve into a more precise framing such as `system context`, `world/domain state`, `execution substrate`, or typed relations among existing dimensions rather than surviving as one foundation leg.

## 4. Strong alternative frames to test

### A. External-world / system-boundary frame

Environment is everything outside the chosen system-of-interest boundary that can affect, constrain, supply, receive from or otherwise interact with the system.

Risk to test: this may become an unbounded residual bucket whose content changes whenever the system boundary changes.

### B. Context / situation frame

The key problem is not an external container but the subset of situational facts relevant to a particular interaction, decision or Work episode.

Risk to test: a purely representational context model may falsely treat context as stable, enumerable external attributes and miss interactional/emergent context.

### C. Affordance / resource relation frame

The load-bearing primitive is the relation between a bearer and world conditions: what the situation affords, permits, enables or makes reachable for particular Work.

Risk to test: this may collapse Environment into relation/control semantics and leave no independent fourth region.

### D. Execution substrate / infrastructure frame

Environment is the technical and material substrate in which Work executes: runtime, compute, storage, network, tools, services, physical infrastructure, configuration and resource limits.

Risk to test: this may be too software-centric and may confuse tools/resources with environment as a whole.

### E. Agent–environment interaction frame

Environment is operationally whatever lies on the other side of an agent/system interaction boundary and produces observations/state transitions in response to actions.

Risk to test: this gives a clean interaction model but may be ontologically indiscriminate—everything not classified as the agent becomes `environment`.

### F. No-fourth-leg / relation-fabric frame

There is no independent Environment foundation region. World state, resources, tools, interfaces and constraints are entities/records or relation-qualified facts connecting Actors and Work.

Risk to test: reduction may erase current external state, feasibility conditions, unavailable possibilities and effects that are neither persistent information nor Actor/Work-local facts.

### G. Boundary-shift / distributed-system frame

Tools, infrastructure and external representations may sometimes become constitutive parts of a larger socio-technical or cognitive system rather than remaining environment.

Risk to test: if system boundaries expand without discipline, `Environment` becomes analysis-relative to the point of losing operational value.

## 5. Decision-critical assumptions to resolve

A1. **Boundary assumption:** whether `inside` versus `environment` can be treated as stable without explicit system-of-interest and analytical-level binding.

A2. **Externality assumption:** whether first-order relevance requires something to be outside the system, or whether internal operating conditions belong to the same problem region.

A3. **Context-as-data assumption:** whether relevant context can be represented as a stable set of facts independent of interaction and interpretation.

A4. **Capability-location assumption:** whether `can do` belongs to an Actor, an environment, or an Actor–Work–environment relation.

A5. **Tool-category assumption:** whether a tool is naturally an Actor, resource, infrastructure component, environmental object, or only role-relative.

A6. **World-state assumption:** whether externally visible effects and observations require a first-order current world/domain state distinct from persistent Records.

A7. **Resource assumption:** whether capacity/resource availability is merely Work planning data or a dynamically changing condition with independent semantics.

A8. **Unity assumption:** whether physical/social/domain context and technical runtime/infrastructure are one coherent foundation region rather than two or more distinct regions.

A9. **Recurrence assumption:** whether the same distinctions actually recur across the research operating arrangement, Development OS, SOUL and task-specific working systems.

## 6. First evidence priority

The first evidence pass should maximise discrimination among the rival frames rather than accumulate `environment` terminology. Priority is evidence that can separate:

1. intrinsic entity type from boundary-relative system context;
2. external/world state from persistent information about that state;
3. Actor capability from situational affordance/resource availability;
4. Work requirements from execution/runtime feasibility conditions;
5. stable context attributes from interactional/emergent context;
6. tool-as-Actor from tool-as-resource/infrastructure/mediator;
7. environment-as-independent region from environment-as-relation fabric or residual bucket;
8. one unified Environment region from a split between world/domain context and execution substrate.

Research direction should change if early evidence exposes a more fundamental distinction than these.
