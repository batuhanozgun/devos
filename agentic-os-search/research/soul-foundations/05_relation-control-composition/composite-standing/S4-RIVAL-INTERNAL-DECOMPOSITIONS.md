# Composite Standing / Qualification — S4 Rival Internal Decompositions

**Status:** COMPLETE  
**Date:** 2026-09-05

## 1. Purpose

Attack the leading bounded-leaf hypothesis against serious alternative internal decompositions and label/boundary rivals.

Leading hypothesis entering S4:

```text
Composite Standing / Qualification
    ← one shared cross-ground qualification grammar
    ← overlapping profiles
    ← no immediate children
```

## 2. Rival A — family-first children

Candidate split:

```text
Composite Standing
├─ Readiness / Actionability
├─ Assurance / Verification / Acceptance
├─ Epistemic / Evidence Sufficiency
├─ Eligibility / Authorization
└─ Goal / Outcome Attainment
```

### Strength

Each family has recognizable vocabulary and recurring operational failure modes.

### Failure

The families overlap heavily:
- `ready for deployment` may require validation, risk tolerance and authorization;
- `evidence sufficient to accept` crosses epistemic sufficiency + assurance + acceptance;
- `authorized and actionable` crosses eligibility + actionability + later Control;
- pure goal attainment may belong to Environment Criterion-Relative Standing rather than this child.

The split also encourages duplication of accepted ground semantics.

**Verdict:** reject as immediate hierarchy; retain profiles.

---

## 3. Rival B — semantic-layer children

Candidate split:

```text
Composite Standing
├─ Constitutive / Object Standing
├─ Epistemic Assessment
└─ Decision / Recognition Standing
```

### Strength

S3 strongly established that these meanings must not collapse.

### Failure

They are not stable sibling populations:
- some physical/technical standings exist independently of assessment;
- some institutional standings are constituted by authorized decisions;
- one evaluation episode may simultaneously assess a standing and create a recognized/accepted institutional standing;
- `epistemic assessment` is often Information + Work-mediated access rather than a separate standing type;
- `decision/recognition` quickly crosses into Control and Actors.

The split would turn semantic layers into entity classes.

**Verdict:** retain as mandatory layers/dimensions, not children.

---

## 4. Rival C — descriptive/epistemic versus normative/institutional

Candidate split:

```text
Composite Qualification
├─ Descriptive / Epistemic
└─ Normative / Institutional
```

### Strength

Separates statements about what is the case from what is permitted/recognized/acceptable under a regime.

### Failure

Many high-value standings are hybrid:
- verification/conformance combines descriptive evidence with normative criterion;
- validation depends on intended use and stakeholder expectations;
- readiness may combine technical demonstration and program decision thresholds;
- evidence sufficiency may depend on institutional burden/standard;
- risk acceptability combines empirical estimates and Actor tolerances.

The binary split leaves `hybrid` as a residual bucket or duplicates standings across both branches.

**Verdict:** reject as universal child split.

---

## 5. Rival D — feasibility / assurance / legitimacy / suitability

Candidate split:

```text
Composite Standing
├─ Feasibility / Readiness
├─ Assurance / Conformance
├─ Legitimacy / Eligibility
└─ Suitability / Fitness / Attainment
```

### Strength

Closer to decision questions than family vocabulary such as `verified` or `actionable`.

### Failure

Still porous:
- `deployable` can require all four;
- `acceptable` can mean conformance, risk tolerance, formal recognition or fitness;
- `suitable` often requires evidence/validation and authorization;
- `legitimate but infeasible` and `feasible but illegitimate` are useful orthogonal dimensions, not a partition.

**Verdict:** useful analytical axes, not children.

---

## 6. Rival E — criterion-source children

Candidate split by what makes the qualification relative:

```text
technical criterion
normative/regulatory criterion
use/purpose criterion
authority/mandate criterion
risk/value/tolerance criterion
```

### Failure

The same standing may involve several criterion sources simultaneously. Criterion provenance affects semantics/evidence/authority but does not change the core cross-ground qualification question enough to warrant sibling research regions.

This mirrors the rejected criterion-family split in Environment Criterion-Relative Standing.

**Verdict:** dimension/profile only.

---

## 7. Rival F — result-domain children

Candidate split:

```text
Boolean
Categorical/Ordinal
Graded/Scalar
Probabilistic/Uncertain
Partial/Coverage
```

### Strength

Result domain materially affects evaluation and downstream decisions.

### Failure

The same standing family can use different result domains depending on domain/decision rule. Result shape is a semantic dimension, not a protected problem question.

**Verdict:** reject as children; retain result semantics in signature.

---

## 8. Rival G — collapse Composite Standing into Relational Binding

Argument:

Any qualification can be represented as an n-ary relation or predicate among anchors.

Example:

```text
Verified(Target, Criterion, Evidence, Verifier, Time)
```

### Test

Representation as a predicate does not eliminate the separate research question:
- what criterion/evaluation semantics make the predicate true?
- what result domain/uncertainty applies?
- how does true/false differ from unknown/not-evaluated/not-applicable?
- which evidence/decision rule establishes the conclusion?
- what invalidates it?

Binding tells us which anchors/roles are connected; Standing tells us what evaluated qualification follows.

**Verdict:** collapse rejected.

---

## 9. Rival H — collapse into Environment Criterion-Relative Standing

Argument:

Every qualification can be reinterpreted as a condition of a larger `system` or `situation` in Environment.

### Test

This would erase accepted ground distinctions.

Example actionability:

```text
Actor competence
+ Work action/path
+ Environment access
→ actionable
```

Treating the whole tuple as one Environment bearer merely hides Actors and Work semantics inside an enlarged Environment object.

Likewise `evidence sufficient for decision D` cannot be reduced to a realized physical/domain condition without losing Information/Work use semantics.

### Boundary correction retained

Pure Domain Condition criterion-standing remains Environment-local; genuinely cross-ground qualification does not.

**Verdict:** collapse rejected.

---

## 10. Rival I — collapse into Control

Argument:

The only useful standings are those used to allow/deny/gate action; therefore they are control predicates.

### Test

Many standings are meaningful independently of control:
- verified against requirement;
- validated for intended use;
- evidence sufficient/insufficient;
- technology at maturity level N;
- target attained/not attained;
- technically actionable but institutionally forbidden.

Control may consume a standing, but does not own the truth/evaluation question.

**Verdict:** collapse rejected.

---

## 11. Rival J — collapse into Transition

Argument:

A standing is just a state in a state machine; all semantics belong to transitions.

### Test

The current qualification question is meaningful without knowing how the standing arose.

```text
what standing holds now?
!= how/why/when it changed?
```

Validity dependencies matter to currentness, but propagation/history belongs to Transition.

**Verdict:** collapse rejected.

---

## 12. Rival K — one universal `Evaluation` parent instead of Standing

Candidate rename/reframe:

```text
Cross-Ground Evaluation
```

### Strength

Many cases involve explicit assessment procedures and uncertainty.

### Failure

Some standings obtain without a focal evaluation activity:
- current actionability can change because Environment access changes;
- an institutional authorization standing may already obtain under a regime;
- goal attainment can hold before anyone assesses it;
- a source may be authoritative for a role even before a particular review.

`Evaluation` would over-center access procedure and repeat the Work/Information collapse S1 rejected.

**Verdict:** do not replace the node with Evaluation.

---

## 13. Rival L — label refinement to `Cross-Ground Qualification / Standing`

### Motivation

`Composite Standing / Qualification` can be misread as the universal home of every standing, especially given the accepted Environment `Criterion-Relative Standing` node.

### Candidate label

> **Cross-Ground Qualification / Composite Standing**

or more compactly:

> **Cross-Ground Qualification**

### Strength

Makes the scope boundary explicit: the node exists only when several accepted-ground semantics jointly determine the qualification.

### Weakness

`Qualification` can itself be read as an evaluation process or metadata label, and changing the accepted parent child label now may create unnecessary nomenclature churn.

### S4 judgment

Retain the accepted label for now but sharpen its canonical gloss:

> **Composite Standing / Qualification = cross-ground qualification whose semantic basis cannot be localized to one accepted ground.**

Revisit the label only if S5–S7 show recurring confusion or boundary failure.

---

## 14. Does any child survive promotion pressure?

No candidate decomposition currently clears the promotion test.

### Family splits
Fail due to overlap and ground duplication.

### Layer splits
Fail due to non-disjointness and Control/Information overlap.

### Descriptive/normative split
Fails on hybrid standings.

### Result-domain split
Is a dimension, not a problem region.

### Criterion-source split
Is a dimension/profile, not a problem region.

## 15. Current internal structure

Strongest current bounded model:

```text
Composite Standing / Qualification
    ← cross-ground only
    ← likely bounded leaf

Shared signature
├─ standing meaning / qualified focus
├─ qualification frame
├─ constitutive multi-ground basis
├─ result semantics
├─ evaluation/access basis [conditional]
└─ validity dependencies

Mandatory semantic layers
├─ object/constitutive standing
├─ epistemic assessment
└─ decision/recognition [when present]

Overlapping profiles
├─ readiness/actionability
├─ assurance/verification/validation/acceptance
├─ epistemic/evidential sufficiency
├─ eligibility/authorization/applicability
├─ outcome/attainment
└─ risk/priority/fitness [conditional]
```

No element above is a promoted child.

## 16. S4 verdict

The **bounded-leaf hypothesis survives all current internal rivals**.

S5 must now use authoritative external traditions and candidate-study holdouts specifically to attack:
- the three-layer distinction;
- non-boolean/uncertain result semantics;
- cross-ground boundary;
- standing-vs-control separation;
- absence of child split.
