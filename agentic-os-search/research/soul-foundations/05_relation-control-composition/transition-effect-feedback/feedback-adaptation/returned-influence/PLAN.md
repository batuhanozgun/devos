## Protected question

## Initial falsifiers

1. An alert, report or postmortem is present but the proposed feedback consumer ignores it.
2. Relevant observations keep an action unchanged because an applied control rule correctly holds/inhibits; apparent behavioral stasis must not prove absence of feedback.
3. Information changes an unrelated downstream action with no identified return into a system/pursuit; not every causal information flow is feedback.
4. A decision genuinely uses feedback, but execution fails or a competing path dominates the final outcome.
5. The same-model explanation says 'I used the signal' while traces or interventions do not distinguish it from a plausible retrospective story.
6. A false, stale, spoofed or duplicated signal changes behavior; influence must not imply accuracy, authority, independence or safety.
7. Multiple individually weak/redundant signals jointly influence a decision; single-signal deletion is not a universal causal test.
8. A fixed response policy provides operative feedback without persistent adaptation.
9. A loop's local metric improves while the wider protected outcome worsens or disappears from observation.
10. Ordinary educational/organizational usage calls a message 'feedback' before recipient uptake. A Foundation operational distinction must not be misrepresented as a universal lexical definition.
