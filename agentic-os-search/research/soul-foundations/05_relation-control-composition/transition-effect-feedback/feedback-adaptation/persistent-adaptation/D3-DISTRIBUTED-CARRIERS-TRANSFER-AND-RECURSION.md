## 1. The bearer is not necessarily a model

A response disposition may depend on model parameters, external representations, a retrieval path, a governing procedure, an available tool, actor skill, coordination or a combination. These are possible realization loci, not a prescribed component inventory.

A narrow model-only frame can report no adaptation while a broader model-plus-memory system has changed. The opposite error is expanding the boundary until any unrelated change somewhere counts as system learning. Name the continuing bearer and the exact response claim, and explain why the included carrier is constitutive of that claim.

The historical external-memory mechanism in S1 challenges the weights-only boundary. It does not justify assuming that a record is consumed in a particular current agent runtime.

## 2. Materialization across future paths

A committed change may not be loaded by running workers, selected in later context, deployed to every route, or retained after an actual lifecycle transition. Transition/Propagation already supplies the exact-realization semantics; this child asks whether the resulting configuration carries experience into future response.

The toy two-worker case changes one worker and leaves the other unchanged. It refutes universal coverage from local realization, not any particular deployment product. The toy serialization case reconstructs objects inside one Python process. It is not an actual operating-system restart, live ChatGPT session, database recovery or agent-memory test.

Where continuity matters, distinguish:

```
carrier written
!= candidate path installed
!= relevant path traverses carrier
!= response disposition changed at that locus
!= all applicable paths changed
!= retained after the claimed lifecycle transition
```

These are claim boundaries, not a necessary linear pipeline in every system. Redundant or interacting paths may make the relationship non-linear.

## 3. Human/team and membership continuity

Training completion, a postmortem, a revised procedure or an individual's stated understanding does not by identity establish changed team behavior. The exact response may depend on role assignment, coordination and access to what others know. Replacing an individual need not erase a system-level disposition if its operative carrier and relations survive; retaining every individual need not preserve it if coordination or access is lost.

These are conceptual counterexamples. The organization-level abstract S5 and the practitioner chapter S6 supply bounded pressure, not a fresh empirical study of SOUL teams or proof of a universal organizational-memory mechanism. Neither training records nor institutional acceptance substitutes for discriminating behavioral evidence.

## 4. Transfer is an additional question

Retention in one bearer under its ordinary support differs from successful transfer to another bearer, context or task. Exact replication of a record is not proof of equivalent access or response; comparable-looking inputs are not proof that all relevant causal mechanisms are invariant.

S7 supplies targeted primary support for this source-to-target boundary. It does not prescribe a universally sufficient transfer test. A new operator, model version, tool interface or coordination pattern can change applicability even when the lesson text is unchanged.

The fresh S7 reading narrows one gap relevant to this pass; it does not revalidate the entire accepted Contrastive Causal Effect package, solve interference in coupled adaptive systems or establish generalization of this taxonomy.

## 5. Three causal targets

Keep separate:

1. Relevant history caused an acquired/retained response change.
2. The changed regime caused a better or worse target outcome than a defined comparator.
3. A source finding supports a causal claim in a different target context.

The first is part of adaptation occurrence. The second needs the accepted contrastive-effect and Standing distinctions. The third additionally needs a justified transport/generalization argument. A before/after improvement, completion certificate or favorable aggregate alone proves none of them.

A system that learns to reject every request can exhibit real changed behavior while harming the broader objective. A system can also retain a useful local response and shift costs onto unobserved actors. Occurrence-neutral accounting makes these failures visible rather than defining them out of adaptation.

## 6. Recursive change

An adapted system may alter not only its ordinary response but also what feedback it selects, how it updates, or which updates persist. This can remain latent until later experience arrives. D1 therefore allows comparison of continuation sequences, not only current actions.

The ordinary/meta distinction is a level of the target response relation at current depth. It is not yet an independent immediate child. A recursive system can still be evaluated at a bounded depth and horizon; no infinite regress is required.

A system's ability to alter its learning rule does not authorize the alteration. Control/Actors remain responsible for governing basis and authority. Self-generated agreement does not create independent evidence; adaptation of the verifier does not automatically validate the verifier's new criteria.

## 7. Evolution is not a synonym for all adaptation

The original parent considered Evolution a potential repeated/recursive profile. That shorthand must not be universalized. Population composition can change without any individual learning; a carrier can vary without the declared phenotype/response changing; and biological processes can remain ground-local rather than constitutively cross-ground.

Optional biological reconnaissance is recorded at its actual limited access level. The decisive argument here is semantic: population bearer, selection/update relation, retained response and ground boundary must be explicit. A change in frequencies or bytes is not automatically the protected response-change claim.

For this bounded cross-ground inquiry, population/selection cases that satisfy the grammar do not yet require a third child. This is not a claim that evolutionary biology reduces to this Foundation leaf, nor that all genomic change is beneficial adaptation. A recurring independent cross-ground Evolution question remains a genuine reopen trigger.
