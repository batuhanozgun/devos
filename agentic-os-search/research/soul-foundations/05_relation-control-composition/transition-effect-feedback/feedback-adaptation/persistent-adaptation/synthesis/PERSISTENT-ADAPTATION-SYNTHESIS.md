## Accepted result

Persistent Adaptation asks whether relevant experience produced a retained change in a bounded system's response disposition over specified future situations or continuation sequences; where/how it is realized and retained; and what exact evidence supports acquisition, retention and expression.

Fixed code or model weights do not prove a fixed effective regime: external carriers and their operative access can matter. Changed parameters or records likewise do not prove a changed response. A latent disposition can be evidenced before natural recurrence, but storage and self-report alone are insufficient. A changed distribution or response to a future sequence may not change the present output.

Persistence is relative to a justified horizon, opportunity class, lifecycle boundary and support conditions. Historical acquisition, current retention, access/expression and adequacy remain different. Removing a constitutive carrier changes the tested question; a probe can itself become a training event. Local acquisition or one path's success does not establish whole-system retention.

Occurrence is distinct from causal benefit, legitimate authority, safety and transfer. Recursive update-rule change can be considered under the same question at a different target depth; no universal reduction of all Evolution is claimed.
