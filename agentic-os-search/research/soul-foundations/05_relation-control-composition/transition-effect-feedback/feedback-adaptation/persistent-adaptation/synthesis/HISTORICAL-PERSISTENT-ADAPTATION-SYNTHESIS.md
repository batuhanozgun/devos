# Persistent Adaptation / Future-Response Change — Integrated Synthesis

**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**Status:** PROPOSED BOUNDED LEAF / USER EVALUATION PENDING  
**Research:** D0–D7 complete; no immediate child promoted.

## 1. Result

Persistent Adaptation remains a coherent cross-ground diagnostic leaf at the present Foundation depth. Its object is not a stored lesson, modified parameter or favorable performance trend. It is a **history-linked retained change in a bounded system's response disposition**, with explicit realization, horizon and evidence scope.

The protected question is:

> Did relevant experience produce a retained change in how this system is disposed to respond to specified later situations or continuation sequences; where and how is that change realized, and what is actually established about its retention and expression?

The accepted Feedback/Adaptation parent retains two distinct, non-coextensive questions. Returned Influence is already accepted. This child's result and its narrow parent wording refinements remain proposals awaiting user evaluation.

## 2. The most consequential correction: effective regime, not code identity

A system can use the same model and interpreter while a history-derived external carrier changes later responses. Conversely, different parameters or representation bytes can implement the same response. The important boundary is therefore not internal versus external storage, or code changed versus unchanged. It is whether the effective, properly bounded response disposition changed because of the relevant history.

D1 derives this distinction; S1 supplies a historical external-memory mechanism case; the toy examples separately show possible same-code change and parameter-only non-change. No current SOUL model or runtime was tested.

This refines rather than discards the parent's negative case: repeatedly applying an unchanged effective response regime is not adaptation by identity. Evolving controller state or context is not automatically a new regime, and fixed update code does not automatically exclude one. A meaningful episode/lifecycle boundary and history-responsive relation are required.

## 3. From stored record to latent disposition

A lesson may exist but never enter the relevant response path. It can be inaccessible, overridden, unselected, stale or behaviorally inert. Storage alone is insufficient.

The reverse inference is also unsafe: absence of natural recurrence does not establish absence of a retained disposition. A rare-case capability can be supported by discriminating tests or a sufficiently established mechanism before that case occurs in ordinary use. What matters is whether the evidence distinguishes an operative changed path from an inert record or coincidence.

```
record retained != response change established
no recurrence yet != no response change
same present output != same future response disposition
```

A difference may appear only in a subset of opportunities, a response distribution, latency or restraint, or after a shared future experience sequence. An updated learning rule can be latent until another update opportunity occurs. The comparison therefore sometimes needs trajectories, not a single immediate input.

## 4. Persistence has a declared scope

A useful persistence claim specifies the bearer, opportunity class, time or event horizon, intervening conditions, lifecycle transition and continuing support. It does not mean forever, nor can a transient effect be called persistent merely by choosing a convenient post-hoc interval.

External support is not disqualifying. A memory-backed system can retain its disposition through retrieval/reload, while the model alone cannot. But that dependence must remain visible. A test that removes the carrier asks about carrier dependence or unsupported retention, not normal supported continuity.

Repeated rehearsal, refresh, repair or re-acquisition may sustain behavior. These are different histories from unaided preservation. Their success should be described accurately rather than hidden inside the word 'retained'. No universal numerical persistence threshold is selected here.

## 5. Acquisition, current retention and expression

Historical acquisition does not establish current retention. Current lack of expression does not establish erased acquisition. The disposition may be masked, inaccessible, overridden or simply untested at a relevant opportunity; it may also be truly lost. Evidence must discriminate these alternatives rather than privileging a favorable narrative.

Reversion to old output is not necessarily restoration of old internal state. A new representation can conversely restore the same function over the declared domain. A retained behavior can also become unsuitable after the environment changes without being forgotten.

D2 preserves these distinctions under primary pressure from sequential-learning, motor-adaptation and retention research. The sources are not proof of a universal hidden-memory architecture. Where the object is opaque, a retention judgment can remain unknown.

## 6. The test can become part of the learning history

A probe may feed back a score, write a memory, train a participant or trigger an update. Later success may then be caused partly by the probe rather than demonstrating a disposition that existed before it.

Separate the pre-probe and post-probe target, freeze or isolate updates where feasible, and include unavoidable probe exposure in the causal history. This is an evidential constraint, not a mandated testing platform. The constructed probe case demonstrates the logical problem, not its prevalence in any production system.

## 7. Distributed carriers and system continuity

The relevant carrier may be distributed across people, representations, policies, tools and coordination. Local realization is not whole-system realization. A new session or worker may fail to recover what another used; a replacement actor may preserve system behavior if the operative carrier and relations survive.

```
change committed != relevant future path changed
one path changed != all applicable paths changed
carrier copied != carrier accessed and used
individual change != team/system change
```

These are not required runtime stages. Redundant and interacting paths may make them non-linear. The child's task is to keep scope and realization visible, while the accepted Transition, Binding, Information, Actors and Control findings retain their own semantics.

## 8. Occurrence, benefit, authority and transfer

At least three causal questions must remain separate: whether history caused the retained change, whether the changed regime improves the intended outcome relative to an alternative, and whether a finding transports to a different target context.

An adaptation may be harmful, unauthorized or based on false information. A proxy can improve while the broader purpose worsens. A favorable before/after trend may instead arise from easier tasks. These are reasons to retain occurrence-neutral accounting, not to define failed learning out of existence.

The fresh targeted transportability reading supports caution about source-to-target inference. It is not a complete revalidation of the causal sibling's evidence boundaries. A successful local test cannot certify another model, team, time horizon or environment by resemblance alone.

## 9. Recursion and Evolution

Changing feedback selection or the update rule can be another instance of the same retained-response inquiry at a different target depth. It may require a continuation-sequence comparison. It does not grant the system authority to change its own governing rules or make its self-assessment independent evidence.

Evolution is not treated as a universal synonym. Population change can occur without individual learning; carrier variation need not change the declared response; biological dynamics can remain ground-local. The present result is only that applicable cross-ground selection/recursive cases have not yet demonstrated a separate protected question requiring a third child. Broad evolutionary theory is neither absorbed nor declared resolved.

## 10. Why one leaf rather than three

Acquisition, retention and expression identify genuine failures and evidence differences. D4 treats this as the strongest rival, not a straw man. At current depth, their adaptation-specific meaning is carried by the common history/bearer/comparator/opportunity/horizon relation. Generic forms already intersect existing Information, Transition, Control, causal and Standing research.

No immediate split yet preserves more meaning without duplicating that core or importing mechanisms. The cost of the leaf decision is explicit: it requires a richer semantic grammar and a precise findings map. A future recurring independent inquiry that this representation obscures would reopen the decision.

## 11. Minimum reusable grammar

```
Persistent-adaptation inquiry
├─ bounded bearer/system/continuing pursuit
├─ relevant experience/history and acquisition/update relation
├─ baseline/comparator regime and response locus/dimension
├─ later opportunity class or continuation sequence
├─ realized carrier/configuration and operative access path
├─ horizon/lifecycle boundary, intervening events and support
├─ acquisition / current retention / access-expression evidence
├─ exact scope, coverage, alternatives and uncertainty
└─ separate benefit / legitimacy / transfer claims where made
```

This is a semantic checklist, not a schema, service boundary, storage architecture or controller design.
