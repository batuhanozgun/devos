# Persistent Adaptation — D2 Persistence, Latency and Evidence

**Status:** COMPLETE / original synthesis with targeted primary pressure  
**Date:** 2026-09-11  
**External evidence:** S2–S4; see SOURCE-LEDGER for actual reading extent.

## 1. Persistence is a qualified relation, not eternity

'Permanent' is usually too strong; 'something changed for a moment' is too weak. A useful persistence claim specifies which later opportunities, elapsed times, intervening events and lifecycle transitions it covers. The horizon may concern the next comparable case, a later session, a seasonal cycle, member replacement or another justified boundary. It need not be a universal number of days or repetitions.

Distinguish retention under ordinary ongoing support from retention without rehearsal, refresh, retrieval or repair. Dependence on an external carrier does not disqualify adaptation; it changes the system boundary and the conditions of the claim. Neither continuous retraining nor a fresh re-acquisition should be silently presented as unsupported retention.

The toy horizon test chooses an explicit decay and threshold only to show why different horizons yield different answers. It supplies no normative retention threshold for SOUL or humans.

## 2. Comparison without removing the phenomenon

A materially comparable situation holds relevant background conditions sufficiently stable while allowing the putative changed carrier to do its work. Conditioning on every mediator, deleting the history record, or replacing the entire adapted system with a fresh baseline may erase the phenomenon being tested.

Carrier removal can be a valuable causal intervention. Its failure result means something different from failure during normal supported operation. State which question is being asked:

- Did this history contribute to the changed disposition?
- Is the carrier/path operative now?
- Does the disposition survive a specified transition?
- Does the adapted regime improve an outcome?
- Does the result transfer to another population/context?

One comparison does not answer all five.

## 3. Acquisition, retention and expression

A history-linked change may have been acquired, retained and yet not expressed in the current observation. Reasons include no relevant opportunity, lack of access, masking by another state, override, output saturation or measurement insensitivity. Alternatively, it may truly have been lost or never acquired.

The historical motor-adaptation source [S3] supplies a bounded warning against reading latent state directly from current output. Our `latent_cancellation` construction is not a replication of that experiment or proof of a particular neural architecture.

The safe distinctions are:

```
acquired in the past != retained now
no observed expression != erasure established
same current output != same retained disposition
retained disposition != currently appropriate disposition
```

When evidence cannot distinguish the alternatives, report the narrower observed result and leave retention unknown. Do not invent an invisible learned state simply because the preferred story needs it.

## 4. Forgetting, reversion, supersession and restoration

New experience can replace, interfere with or counteract earlier behavior; selective forgetting is one possible explanation, not a label for every regression. Sequential-learning research [S2] supplies component pressure that new acquisition and preservation of earlier responses are separate concerns.

Reversion to old output need not restore the old internal configuration. Conversely, a different representation may restore the same response function over the declared scope. The toy functional-restoration case protects this distinction. Supersession can be intentional and appropriate; it must not erase the historical claim that adaptation once occurred.

A historical claim, a current retention claim and a current adequacy claim can therefore have different truth values. A once-useful behavior may remain strongly retained after the environment changes, while becoming harmful. That is not forgetting by identity.

## 5. Evidence for latent response change

Possible evidence routes, each with limited scope, include:

| Route | Can help establish | Does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Storage/configuration inspection | A candidate carrier changed | Operative use or behavior difference |
| Trace of selection and response path | Coupling at the traced locus | All paths, future reliability or benefit |
| Matched pre/post probe | A conditional response difference | History attribution without alternatives controlled |
| Carrier intervention/ablation | Contribution under that intervention | Universal necessity, especially with redundancy |
| Delayed/lifecycle probe | Retention under specified conditions | Unlimited duration or unsupported transfer |
| Repeated/stochastic comparison | A distributional estimate | Causal identification or sufficient precision automatically |
| Mechanism-based analysis | A bounded conditional capability | Unmodeled conditions and opaque consumer behavior |

These are evidence families, not a mandated evaluation architecture or ordinal confidence score.

## 6. Probes may alter their target

Testing can be an intervention. The human-retention abstract [S4] warns that a test can itself affect later retention. In an adaptive software system, feedback, memory writes or state updates during a probe can similarly create the difference later attributed to prior experience.

Where possible, distinguish a read-only measurement from a training/update step, use an appropriately frozen snapshot or separate pre-probe and post-probe claims. Such separation is not always feasible for people or deployed adaptive systems; in that case explicitly include probe exposure in the history and limit attribution.

Our `probe_changes_target` example deliberately performs an update during the test. Its later success cannot establish that the tested disposition existed before the update.

## 7. Scope and observation coverage

A passing ordinary case says little about an untested rare case. A finite sample cannot establish equality or difference over an unrestricted future domain. Conversely, failure to observe a manifestation is not proof of absent disposition if the test lacks the opportunity or sensitivity to expose it.

Retention confidence must stay bound to the tested opportunity class, horizon, path, version and support. Testing the same constructed object again is useful reproducibility, not independent validation of real adaptation. D7 preserves that distinction explicitly.
