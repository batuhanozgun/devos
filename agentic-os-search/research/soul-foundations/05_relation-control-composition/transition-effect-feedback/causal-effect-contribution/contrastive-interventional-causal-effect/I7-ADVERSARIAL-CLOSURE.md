## 2. Attack A — “Transportability is obviously a separate child”

### Strongest case for promotion

Transport/generalization has characteristic failures:
- source and target populations differ;
- effect modifiers shift;
- treatment versions/fidelity differ;
- causal mechanism/context changes;
- selection/support differ;
- source effect does not replicate in target.

It often needs dedicated analysis and evidence.

### Counter-test

Ask whether the **object-level target** is different in kind.

Target causal claim remains:

```text
Effect of X vs X' on Y in target T
```

Source evidence `S` introduces an extra epistemic bridge:

```text
Does S support/identify the target-T effect?
```

If direct target-T evidence existed, transport would disappear while the causal object remains.

This indicates transportability is primarily about reuse/validity of evidence across applicability frames, not a third causal-effect object.

### Verdict

**No promotion at current depth.**

However, because fresh dedicated transportability literature could not be retrieved in this pass, it remains the strongest reopen trigger.

## 3. Attack B — “Identification deserves its own child”

Identification can require:

- selection assumptions;
- interference assumptions;
- measurement assumptions.

Could this be a sibling:

```text

```

### Failure

Identification answers:

> Can this evidence/assumption set recover the causal target?

It is not another causal-effect protected question.

Promoting it would mix object-level causal truth with evidence validity and partially duplicate Information/Standing.

### Verdict

**Reject child. Preserve identification assumptions/threats explicitly when load-bearing.**

## 4. Attack C — “Interference/spillover is structurally different enough for a child”

Interference breaks independent-unit models and can create:
- direct effects;
- spillover effects;
- saturation effects;
- system-level effects;
- displacement.

### Counter-test

Define intervention as a system/assignment regime:

```text
policy/configuration X across connected units
vs
policy/configuration X'
```

and define the outcome target appropriately.

The protected question remains contrastive.

Interference changes the necessary treatment/target representation and evidence assumptions, but not the causal question's semantic kind.

### Verdict

**Retain as high-value profile/dimension, not child.**

## 5. Attack D — “Direct/indirect/mediated effects form a stable hierarchy”

They can require materially different assumptions and analyses.

But they are specific path-constrained contrasts.

A total effect, direct effect and mediated effect can all be asked about the same intervention/outcome; they overlap rather than partition a population of causal questions cleanly.

### Verdict

**Profiles / specialized contrasts, not immediate children.**

## 6. Attack E — “Intervention fidelity belongs entirely to Transition/Propagation, so this child is stealing it”

Accepted Transition owns:

```text
what change actually became real/effective and where
```

Contrastive Causal Effect does not own that transition history.

But causal-effect identity/evidence needs to know:

```text
which realized regime/exposure was actually contrasted
```

Otherwise source/config labels can stand in for materially different treatments.

Thus:

```text
Transition establishes realization facts
Contrastive Effect consumes exact realized-regime semantics
```

No boundary theft.

## 7. Attack F — “Non-manipulable exposures invalidate the word Interventional”

The name `Contrastive / Interventional` could be read too narrowly.

The child must allow causal questions about states/exposures not directly settable by one atomic action, provided alternatives are sufficiently well-defined.

Thus `Interventional` is not a requirement that every cause be literally manipulable.

Canonical protected question should preserve both words:
- **Contrastive** — the semantic requirement;
- **Interventional** — a major causal interpretation/evidence tradition and common use case.

### Verdict

Name survives with explicit gloss; literal manipulability is not universal.

## 8. Attack G — “Average vs individual effect is a deep enough split”

An average effect can conceal harm/benefit heterogeneity and cannot be assigned to one individual by identity.

Yet both are causal contrasts differing in target/aggregation.

The individual effect may be especially difficult/unidentifiable, but epistemic difficulty does not create a distinct semantic region.

### Verdict

**Target/aggregation profile, not child.**

## 10. Attack I — “Before/after evidence is enough in deterministic engineering systems”

Sometimes a deterministic intervention plus known mechanism can be extremely strong evidence.

But the causal claim is still contrastive; before/after chronology alone does not establish the counterfactual difference if other changes occurred.

In tightly controlled deterministic cases, mechanism/intervention evidence can make alternative explanations negligible.

That does not justify replacing causal contrast with chronology universally.

### Verdict

Non-collapse survives:

```text
before/after != causal effect by identity
```
