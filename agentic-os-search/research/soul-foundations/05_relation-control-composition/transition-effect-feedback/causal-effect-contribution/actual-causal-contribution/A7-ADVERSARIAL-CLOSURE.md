## 1. Objective

## 2. Attack — “Causal configuration/set is architecture leakage”

### Objection

Talking about joint sets/configurations may smuggle hypergraphs, fault trees or structural-causal models into Foundation.

### Test

Pattern:

```text
A AND B → E
```

If represented as two independent claims:

```text
A caused E
B caused E
```

we can falsely imply independent sufficiency.

### Verdict

The semantic distinction is load-bearing even if the later representation is not predetermined.

Foundation requirement:

> Preserve joint/interaction semantics when flattening would change the causal claim.

No graph/schema formalism is adopted.

**Attack fails.**

## 3. Attack — “Actual causation collapses into Transition + Binding + evidence”

### Objection

If we know exact events, order, bindings and evidence, perhaps causal relation adds nothing.

### Counterexample

Two explanations can share the same timeline:

```text
A before B before E
```

while differing on whether:
- A caused E;
- B caused E;
- common factor C caused both;
- A was preempted;
- A and B jointly caused E.

### Verdict

Chronology/binding constrains causal candidates but does not determine the causal proposition.

**Attack fails.**

## 4. Attack — “Actual causation is only contrastive effect plus episode facts”

### Objection

General/interventional effect knowledge plus episode state could decide actual causation.

### Counterpressure

A factor can have strong general causal effect yet not operate in one episode; one episode factor can actually cause an effect without establishing its general effect magnitude.

Preemption, redundancy and exact path operation remain episode-specific.

**Attack fails; parent two-child split survives.**

## 5. Attack — “Positive causation and omission require separate children”

### Objection

Omission is philosophically and evidentially different enough to require its own region.

### Test

Both still require:
- exact explanandum;
- exact episode presence/absence;
- causal relevance to trajectory;
- evidence;
- alternatives;
- depth.

Omission adds relevance/contrast guardrails because arbitrary absences overgenerate.

### Verdict

Different guardrail, same protected question and grammar.

**No child split.**

## 6. Attack — “Systemic causation is under-promoted”

### Objection

Organizational/systemic causes are qualitatively different from proximate causes.

### Test

Systemic factors can be:
- actual episode conditions;
- enabling contributors;
- prevention failures;
- members of causal configurations;
- depth-relative to inquiry boundary.

They still answer:

> What actually contributed to this effect?

### Verdict

Systemic is load-bearing depth/profile, not separate child.

**Attack fails.**

## 7. Attack — “Control/barrier failures deserve a child”

### Objection

Barrier analysis has distinct methods and corrective action.

### Counterexample

A defective barrier may be causally irrelevant because:
- harmful path never reached it;
- another barrier stopped the effect;
- another sufficient route dominated;
- the protected effect was caused upstream before the barrier mattered.

Thus Control defect first needs actual episode causal qualification.

### Verdict

Barrier/control failure remains causal role/profile plus Control boundary.

**Attack fails.**

## 8. Attack — “Occurrence / severity / duration are separate child populations”

### Objection

Causes of initiation differ from causes of progression/harm.

### Test

Changing from `fault occurrence` to `user harm` changes the explanandum, not the kind of causal question.

The same grammar applies.

### Verdict

Exact explanandum remains a mandatory dimension; no children.

## 9. Attack — “Causal configuration should itself be a child”

### Objection

Joint/overdetermined/preempted causation is structurally richer than individual cause claims.

### Test

- individual causation is arity-1 configuration;
- episodes can mix individual and joint relations;
- overdetermination creates alternative sufficient configurations;
- same evidence/depth/alternative question remains.

### Verdict

Configuration/arity is structural dimension, not population.

**Attack fails.**

## 10. Attack — “Frame-blindness belongs only to Information/general epistemology”

### Objection

`all observed causes != all material causes` sounds like generic uncertainty.

### Response

The guardrail is causal-claim specific:
- investigation closure can be wrong because causal-search universe is incomplete;
- instrumentation can systematically hide causal paths;
- every identified contributor can be correct while explanation completeness remains unknown.

Information Selection/Exposure explains evidence visibility; Actual Causation owns the claim that the causal model/explanation is or is not complete.

### Verdict

Cross-ground boundary is stable; no epistemology sibling needed.

## 11. Attack — “Responsibility naturally follows actual causation”

### Counterexample

An Actor can causally contribute while:
- acting within authority;
- fulfilling duty;
- reasonably relying on valid information;
- being only one member of a larger systemic causal set.

Conversely accountability can exist without direct physical/proximate causal contribution.

### Verdict

```text
causal contribution != responsibility/accountability
```

Actors boundary survives.

## 12. Attack — “One formal actual-causation definition is needed for consistency”

### Objection

Without one SCM/counterfactual/mechanistic formalism, Foundation is too vague.

### Response

Foundation's job is to preserve decision-critical semantic distinctions across domains, not select one formalism before domain/architecture requirements exist.

The current grammar requires:
- exact explanandum;
- actual contributors;
- configuration/path semantics where material;
- evidence/alternatives;
- depth.

Domains may later realize this through structural models, fault trees, mechanism analysis, investigation trees, STPA/CAST or other methods.

### Verdict

No formalism is promoted at Foundation stage.

## 13. Attack — “Probabilistic actual causation breaks the grammar”

### Test

A contributor can alter the trajectory probabilistically rather than deterministically and still be analyzed for actual episode contribution under domain-appropriate evidence/contrast.

The grammar does not require deterministic necessity/sufficiency.

### Verdict

Probabilistic causation is a profile/claim property, not child.

## 14. Attack — “But-for should remain default definition”

### Counterexample

Overdetermination:

```text
A sufficient
B independently sufficient
both occur
```

Removing either leaves E.

Preemption also complicates naive necessity.

### Verdict

But-for is useful evidence/contrast test, not universal definition.

**Attack fails.**
