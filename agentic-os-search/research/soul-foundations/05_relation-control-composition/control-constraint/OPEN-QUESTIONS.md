## Residual questions

1. **Label clarity:** should future work rename the region `Governing Control / Constraint` if `Control` is repeatedly confused with feedback-control theory or arbitrary Environment limitation?
2. **Control-objective scope:** what universal minimum is needed to state the protected effect/action scope without importing threat-model or architecture-specific path enumerations?
3. **Coverage threshold:** when must alternate-path analysis be exhaustive enough for a control claim, and how should material-path scope be justified under uncertainty?
4. **Indirect effect:** what exact composition of delegation, capability, reachability and causation is needed to say one Actor can indirectly cause effect E?
5. **Authority constitution:** in institutional domains, when does authority merely validate a decision versus constitute the control/rule itself?
6. **Conflict/precedence:** what minimum semantics are universal for conflicting controls, hierarchy, exceptions and waivers without adopting a fixed policy-combining language?
7. **Defaults / indeterminacy:** which domains require explicit `not applicable`, `indeterminate`, `escalate`, fail-open or fail-closed distinctions?
8. **Obligation under impossibility:** how should later composition preserve required-but-not-actionable situations without collapsing control failure, impossibility and Actor responsibility?
9. **Separation of duties:** what minimum independence relation is needed beyond distinct Actor identities when collusion, shared principals, shared context or agent delegation are possible?
10. **Enforcement strength:** can advisory/procedural/mechanical realization be compared without implying a false universal hardness scale?
11. **Revocation / propagation:** how do changes in policy/authority/waiver become effective across distributed enforcement paths? Deferred to Transition unless a missing current-Control distinction emerges.
12. **Control assurance:** what evidence is sufficient to claim a control is operating, covered and effective, especially when local monitoring cannot observe alternate paths?
13. **Causal effectiveness:** when does `control prevented/mitigated effect E` require counterfactual or causal-contribution analysis beyond current Standing/Control semantics?
14. **Safety versus governance:** do materially different future physical/social domains expose a stable child split that current holdouts did not?
15. **Independent replication:** would an independent researcher/model retain bounded-leaf status and the same governing-control grammar?

## Explicit reopen triggers

Reopen Control / Constraint if:
- later Transition work cannot preserve current Control versus change/effect boundaries without a missing Control distinction;
- alternate-path/bypass cases repeatedly require a stable protected question not expressible as a Control coverage profile;
- a `basis / decision / enforcement / monitoring` layer develops a low-overlap cross-domain population with its own first-order failure family;
- normative/institutional and technical/physical controls cannot remain coherent under the same governing-question grammar;
- the distinction between arbitrary Environment limitation and Control cannot be applied reliably;
- authorization/obligation cases expose a missing Actors or Composite Standing distinction;
- materially different domains repeatedly require a control modality/result semantic outside the shared grammar;
- an upstream accepted ground or Binding/Standing result changes materially;
- independent review finds a stronger decomposition.

## Non-reopen conditions

Do not reopen merely because:
- an implementation uses RBAC, ABAC, XACML, Rego/OPA, ACLs, capabilities or another policy technology;
- architecture creates separate policy-decision and policy-enforcement services;
- a specific domain has a large control catalog;
- one control is human and another automated;
- a control uses multiple enforcement layers;
- monitoring or compliance reporting becomes large;
- threat modeling uses attack graphs or reachability tools;
- safety practice uses preventive/detective/mitigating terminology;
- implementation requires fail-safe defaults, caches, policy distribution or revocation mechanisms.
