# 2. Missing-child probe — semantics / interpretation

Candidate missing child:

> `Meaning / Interpretation`

Why it looks plausible:

- form and content can diverge;
- vocabulary/schema/language matter;
- interpretation failure is independent of storage.

Reduction test:

The problem is already load-bearing inside **Informational State / Representational Constitution**:

- what content is expressed;
- what form encodes it;
- what interpretation binds form to content;
- what referent/scope it is about.

Transformation/translation that changes interpretation belongs to Dynamics.

**Verdict:** important internal region, not missing peer child.

---

# 3. Missing-child probe — signal / channel / transmission

Candidate:

> `Signal / Communication`

Why plausible:

- Shannon-style information and communication systems have distinct channel/noise problems;
- delivery/exposure can fail independently of content.

Boundary/reduction test:

- physical/network channel conditions can be Environment;
- encoding/transmission/exposure/receipt are Informational Dynamics when the focal issue is informational mediation;
- message/result/content is Informational State;
- accessibility/visibility is a cross-cutting condition.

A dedicated universal child would split one process chain by implementation medium rather than first-order semantic problem.

**Verdict:** no new child.

---

# 4. Missing-child probe — reasoning / inference / knowledge production

Candidate:

> `Inference / Reasoning`

Why plausible:

- inference can produce information not explicitly present in inputs;
- reasoning correctness has distinctive failures.

Reduction/boundary test:

- derivation/inference as transformation of informational inputs → Informational Dynamics;
- resulting conclusion/model → Informational State;
- reasoning activity as a deliberately selected/controlled pursuit may additionally be Work;
- factual correctness/evidential sufficiency is not guaranteed by storing the conclusion.

**Verdict:** important Dynamics specialization and Work boundary; not peer child.

---

# 5. Missing-child probe — evidence / truth / trust / epistemic status

Candidate:

> `Epistemic Standing / Evidence`

Why plausible:

- claims can be supported, contradicted, uncertain or unreliable;
- this matters directly to SOUL reasoning.

Attack:

- evidence is relative to a target claim/question/body of evidence;
- confidence/reliability can qualify many informational states;
- epistemic standing changes through Dynamics such as revision/retraction/verification;
- source authority belongs partly to Actors/policy/control;
- actual truth is not created by informational standing.

Promoting this region would produce a broad relation/standing bucket crossing both children.

**Verdict:** cross-cutting load-bearing dimension/relation family, not immediate child.

---

# 6. Missing-child probe — context / relevance / attention / retrieval

Candidate:

> `Context / Working Set`

Why plausible:

- SOUL/LLM systems are extremely sensitive to which information is currently visible;
- context omission/noise has distinctive failures.

Attack:

- eligible/source representations → State;
- retrieval/ranking/filtering/compression/assembly → Dynamics;
- resulting current context/view → State;
- relevance is relative to Work/Actor/query/purpose;
- access constraints partly belong to Actors/policy/Environment.

Context is therefore a highly important **formation/use pattern** crossing State and Dynamics, not a clean peer partition.

**Verdict:** no new child.

---

# 7. Missing-child probe — quality

Candidate:

> `Information Quality`

Why plausible:

Quality traditions explicitly name intrinsic, contextual, representational and accessibility regions.

Attack:

- the categories are fitness/use/assessment dimensions;
- quality can be assessed for many state types;
- quality assessment itself is a Dynamic producing quality metadata/annotations as State;
- no universal objective quality taxonomy is implied by W3C DQV.

**Verdict:** cross-cutting assessment lens, not child.

---

# 8. Missing-child probe — provenance / source / lineage

Candidate:

> `Provenance`

Why plausible:

Provenance has deep formal literature and independent failure modes.

Attack:

- lineage connects informational states to generating/using/deriving activities and agents;
- provenance information can itself be represented as State;
- generation/derivation belongs to Dynamics;
- attribution crosses Actors;
- source/lineage metadata can need persistence.

A peer Provenance child would duplicate both State and Dynamics.

**Verdict:** load-bearing cross-cutting relation/lineage family, not immediate child.

---

# 9. Missing-child probe — persistence / memory / Records

Candidate:

> `Persistent Information / Records`

This is the strongest rival because a complete deep corpus already exists.

## Promotion argument

Persistence clearly introduces distinct protected outcomes and failure families:

- future identifiability/interpretability;
- preservation closure;
- history/standing reconstruction;
- retrieval sufficiency;
- retention/disposition.

This satisfies several child-promotion criteria.

## Anti-promotion argument

However, immediate siblinghood with State/Dynamics produces structural overlap:

- every persistent representation is still an informational State;
- every persistent lifecycle includes Dynamics;
- provenance and standing may themselves persist;
- context/memory read converts persistent state into current selected state;
- persistence is activated by horizon/use obligation rather than by a disjoint kind of informational phenomenon.

## Judgment

The correct conclusion is not that Records is minor. It is:

> **Records is a deep, general-purpose specialization/profile of Information activated by persistence requirements, but the persistence axis does not currently form a clean immediate-child partition alongside State and Dynamics.**

The Records corpus remains directly reusable and reopenable under its own conditions.

**Verdict:** not promoted as immediate child; retained as explicit deep specialization.

# 12. Missing-child probe — normative information, policies, goals and requirements

Candidate:

> `Normative / Intentional Information`

Attack:

A representation may encode:

- a policy;
- a requirement;
- a goal;
- a prohibition;
- a prediction.

Its content/mode belongs to Informational State. Creation/revision/publication belongs to Dynamics and often Work. Binding obligation/authority/commitment belongs outside Information.

**Verdict:** representation mode/content dimension, not peer child.

# 13. Reduction attack — eliminate Information as a top-level ground

Rival:

> All Information semantics can be represented as relations/properties of Environment, Work and Actors.

Counterexamples retained from N1–N5:

1. actual state fixed, observation/model changes;
2. source data fixed, derived view changes;
3. Work execution fixed, dashboard/status representation changes;
4. Actor fixed, belief/claim changes;
5. same content persists while currentness/standing changes;
6. model-visible context changes without source state changing.

These cases repeatedly expose state/dynamic failures of mediation that cannot be reduced without losing important distinctions.

**Verdict:** reduction still rejected for research purposes.

---

# 14. Split-parent attack — make State and Dynamics separate top-level grounds

Rival:

> If State and Dynamics are real children, perhaps they should be separate top-level grounds rather than one Information parent.

Attack:

This would fragment one coherent mediated-information problem:

- Dynamics is defined by production/change of informational State;
- State interpretation depends on provenance/formation history;
- shared cross-cutting dimensions bind both;
- actual-world/Work/Actor boundaries are common to both;
- persistence/context/evidence cases repeatedly require both.

The parent adds explanatory compression rather than merely grouping unrelated children.

**Verdict:** retain shared parent.

---

# 15. Merge-child attack — collapse State and Dynamics into one leaf

Rival:

> State/Dynamics is just a generic analytical dimension and not worth hierarchical promotion.

Attack result:

The distinction survives the project's child-promotion criteria:

- distinct first-order questions;
- independent failure modes;
- repeated external traditions;
- collapse repeatedly confuses procedure with result, derivation with output, revision with state and context formation with context state;
- later SOUL/Development OS reasoning materially depends on knowing whether an error is in the informational state or the process that formed/changed it.

The split is therefore more than vocabulary symmetry.

**Verdict:** keep two children.

# 16. Rename attack

Current routing label:

`Information / Representation State`

Problems:

- `State` excludes/underplays Dynamics;
- `Information` is polysemous, including Shannon-style non-semantic senses;
- `Representation` may exclude pre-semantic signal/access concerns if interpreted narrowly.

Candidate refined label:

> **Information / Representation**

Alternative stronger-semantic labels considered:

- Representational / Informational Mediation;
- Information & Representation;
- Informational Mediation / Representation.

No alternative clearly dominates across readability, continuity and scope.

**N6 recommendation:** drop `State` at parent level and retain **Information / Representation** as the least-disruptive bounded research label, with explicit scope definition preventing residual `all information` interpretation.

This is a research naming refinement, not an ontology class name.
