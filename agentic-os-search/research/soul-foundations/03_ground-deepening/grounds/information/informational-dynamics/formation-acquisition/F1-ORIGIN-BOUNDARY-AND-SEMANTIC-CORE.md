# F1 — Origin, Acquisition and Representational Realization

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded analysis; candidate grammar subjected to F2–F7

## 1. The object is a relation, not a storage action

Formation asks how a specified informational occurrence/result became instantiated from a source act, phenomenon, signal or resource at a specified locus. It includes the source-facing relation needed to interpret what that result can actually represent. Writing bytes is one possible realization, not the defining event.

Three foci must not be conflated:

1. **The target:** what the inquiry concerns, including property, interval, scope and representational mode.
2. **The encountered origin:** the actual source episode, signal, statement, resource or generated artifact and the way it was encountered.
3. **The formed representation:** what became instantiated, with what identity and interpretation, at the receiver or locus being considered.

The target and immediate source may differ. The immediate acquisition claim can concern an assertion episode; the reported proposition may concern an unobserved deployment. The new record can correctly establish what was said while leaving deployment truth unresolved. A record can also miscapture the statement yet coincidentally assert the actual world condition. Source fidelity and target truth are neither equivalent nor ordered levels of one success flag.

This three-focus distinction is this pass's analytical grammar, not a proposed schema or assertion that every acquisition stores three objects.

## 2. New in which sense?

A representation can be new as an occurrence, as a carrier/token, at a receiving boundary, as semantic content, or as evidence for a target. These are different claims.

A second delivery can instantiate a new local copy without a new originating observation. Two genuinely different observation episodes can produce identical content. A newly computed conclusion can be logically informative without adding a new observation. Conversely, new bytes can add no relevant information at all.

PROV's distinct production and derivation concepts provide one bounded comparison point [S6]. They are not imported as the Formation ontology: PROV entities also extend beyond informational objects, and production alone does not decide the epistemic novelty of a result.

```
new carrier/token != new origin episode != new content != new independent evidence
same content != same observation episode
newly known to receiver != newly true in the world
```

Neither a URL nor a database identifier resolves every one of these distinctions. Exact identity is relative to the question and protected use, consistent with the accepted State and Binding results.

## 3. Formation, derivation and exposure can coexist

Suppose a tool finds a document, converts it to text and selects an excerpt. The source-facing question is whether the captured text is bound to the intended resource/version and represents it with adequate fidelity. The derivation question concerns the meaning and validity of transformations. The exposure question concerns which existing material becomes visible to the current consumer.

A correct choice of URL can be followed by corrupted acquisition; a faithful captured corpus can be followed by omission of its decisive paragraph. The four capture/selection combinations in case C9 demonstrate this separability for a stipulated fixture, not a universal parser benchmark.

The same distinction applies to endogenous sources. A model-generated statement is a real output occurrence that can be captured. Its appearance is not an observation of whatever world event it describes. Whether the statement follows from inputs is a Derivation question. Whether the consumer receives it is an Exposure question. Formation cannot promote it to eyewitness evidence merely because an external tool returned it.

## 4. No universal raw-data-first stage

The accepted parent's simple measurement example is useful, but not a necessary chronological ordering. VIM explicitly permits models and calculations within measurement [S1]. SSN/SOSA uses a broad observation concept, and its non-normative forecast example further challenges label-based routing [S4].

The refinement is not that formation and derivation are identical. It is that their protected questions can be interleaved within one procedure. A model-assisted measurement must still answer source/target/realization questions; its calculations still need appropriately scoped derivation support. Calling an output raw or observed does not discharge either obligation.

A synthetic dataset, simulation or forecast can be acquired as a model-produced representation without being a new empirical observation of the represented target. This is mode preservation, not exclusion of generated information from Information.

## 5. Formation occurrence versus formation adequacy

A false, truncated, ambiguous or misattributed record can genuinely exist. Therefore failure to establish the intended source relationship is not identical to absence of any information formation. Name the intended result and failure: no record, wrong source, incomplete capture, distorted content, invalid interpretation, or inadequate support for the target claim.

A failed request can produce a valid error record about that attempt. It does not necessarily report the remote target's condition. A valid negative answer, missing value and transport failure are not interchangeable just because each prevents a positive value from being returned.

## 6. Candidate core

A bounded inquiry retains target/source/result roles, acquisition or origination locus, encounter/procedure and relevant dependencies, representation mode and identity, coverage and detectability, time/version, and evidence/alternatives. Optional qualifiers become necessary when dropping them changes the claim.

This allows qualitative assertions, physical readings, imported resources, human elicitation and generated representations without forcing them into a measurement-only hierarchy. Whether this should remain one leaf is tested, not settled by the existence of this grammar.
