## Protected question

How did a specified informational occurrence/result become instantiated from an origin or source at a bounded locus, what source-to-result relationship is actually established, and what target claims can the available acquisition evidence discriminate?

## Principal findings

- Target, immediate source and formed representation can differ.
- Formation occurrence, capture fidelity, target truth, decision sufficiency, legitimate authority and operative use are separate claims.
- Token/receiver novelty, origin-episode novelty, content novelty and independent evidence are not interchangeable.
- Acquisition is not necessarily passive, model-free or chronologically prior to derivation.
- Coverage of formed records cannot silently substitute for coverage of material source opportunities.
- Record-only exact identification depends on distinguishing the relevant alternatives; F2 states the bounded mathematical condition.
- Missing, null, unknown, zero and negative detection require source-contract and coverage-sensitive interpretation.
- Source-reference, production and receiver-observation times have distinct roles.

These are the original bounded semantic result under the pressure and limits in F5/SOURCE-LEDGER, not a universal implementation recipe.
