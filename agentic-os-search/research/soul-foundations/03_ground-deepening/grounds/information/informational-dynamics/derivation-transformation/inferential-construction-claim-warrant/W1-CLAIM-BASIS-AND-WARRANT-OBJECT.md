# W1 — Claim, Basis and the Warrant Relation

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded node analysis; structural judgment in W4/W6.

## 1. A produced answer is not the object-level warrant

An actor can produce a conclusion, offer an argument for it, happen to be right, or reach it through a different route than the argument suggests. None of those relations is identical. The focal object here is the support relation between a qualified claim and an identified inferential basis, under an applicable reasoning regime. It is not merely a fluent explanation or a list of cited files.

Keep separate: candidate production; actual contribution of an input; an offered justification; the validity of a conditional inference; the adequacy of premises/model for the intended target; actual truth; decision sufficiency; and legitimate authority. Some of these are already protected by the parent and neighboring grounds. This pass deepens the support relation instead of absorbing their whole remit.

A public proof can be valid even when it is not a faithful report of how its producer first guessed the answer. A faithful record of an argument can describe invalid reasoning. Turpin et al. supply a historical, model-specific intervention study that separates convincing explanations from dependence on prompt factors [S6]. It is not a current-model rate, a universal claim that explanations are false, or an experiment conducted in this pass.

## 2. Claim content includes strength and scope

“H”, “H follows if A”, “H is one possible explanation”, “H is preferred among these candidates”, and “P(H|E,M)=p” are different conclusions. So are a population prediction, a case-specific statement and an intervention effect. A number, a causal verb or a word such as verified must not silently upgrade the conclusion.

An explicitly tentative hypothesis can be worth testing without being established, uniquely supported or even the best explanation. Consistency with observations is a minimum compatibility claim under the stated model class, not by itself a ranking or a posterior probability. A generated alternative outside that class changes the comparison problem; it is not automatically ruled out because it was absent from the first list.

This is not a demand that every useful idea be a deductive consequence of a source document. Background knowledge, a learned capability, an empirical model or an assumption can legitimately enter the basis. Their role must remain explicit where excluding them changes the claimed support. No requirement to expose or reconstruct every internal computation follows.

## 3. Conditional correctness does not discharge its conditions

C01 uses p and p-implies-q to establish q in a finite classical model. Removing p leaves a countermodel. The calculation does not establish that p holds in the actual target. An output can therefore be a correct conditional result while the corresponding unconditional claim remains unestablished.

The distinction extends beyond symbolic logic: a correct posterior under a stipulated prior/likelihood, or a correctly calculated causal effect in one structural model, does not establish that the selected model is warranted for the target. The basis can itself require source acquisition, empirical validation or independent argument. Repeating the conditional calculation does not supply the missing bridge.

Do not turn this into infinite regress. It can be appropriate to use an explicitly bounded working model, report a conditional result, or give a range across serious alternatives. The question is whether unresolved conditions could materially change the claim, not whether every background assumption in all knowledge has been eliminated.

## 4. A qualified basis, not an obligatory data structure

A defensible analysis can make recoverable, when material:

- the precise claim, target, reference time and mode/strength;
- the premises/evidence and their relevant source, interpretation and dependence;
- the rule, model, background assumptions and admitted alternatives;
- what relation the result is supposed to satisfy: necessity, probability, bound, comparative explanation or defeasible support;
- scope conditions and known rebuttals or attacks on the support bridge;
- what was actually checked and what remains unknown.

These are semantic questions, not mandatory stored fields, ontology classes or an adopted workflow. A small arithmetic inference need not carry a large dossier. A causal deployment claim may need much more than its arithmetic.

## 5. Failure inventory before hierarchy

The important failures are not reducible to one bad-output category: omitted condition; inconsistent basis; incomplete proof search misreported as negation; probability direction reversed; dependence ignored; good source-fit transported without a bridge; explanation ranking inflated into certainty; contrary evidence or a defeater omitted; circular support; inferential selection hidden; calibration scope expanded; joint certainty borrowed from marginal claims; and a confidence value treated as action authority.

W2/W3 and checks give direct paths for these failures. W4 asks whether they require immediate children, regimes, or cross-cutting facets. The inventory does not itself settle that organization.
