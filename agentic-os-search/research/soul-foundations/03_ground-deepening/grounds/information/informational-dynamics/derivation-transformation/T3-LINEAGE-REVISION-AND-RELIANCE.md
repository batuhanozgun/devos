# T3 — Lineage, Revision and Reliance

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded analysis; maintained-state inquiry tested as a serious child candidate

## 1. A source list is not a support structure

A result can require a and b together, or c alone. C08 evaluates (a AND b) OR c over all eight Boolean assignments. Removing a does not remove support while c remains. Removing c does not remove support when both a and b remain. A flat list of three sources conceals the difference between joint and alternative routes.

The expression concerns a stipulated support rule. It is not a universal actual-causation test, a proof that each cited source was used, or a confidence score. A historical contributor, a sufficient proof premise and a currently admissible evidence source can have different roles. The accepted Binding and causal research remain controlling at their own scopes.

Positive-query provenance provides algebraic examples of joint and alternative dependencies; the primary difference-query study [S5] also challenges the universal extension of one such algebra. We do not adopt an annotation semiring or claim that provenance is impossible in nonmonotonic queries.

## 2. New input can invalidate an old result

C09 executes an in-memory SQLite query selecting tasks for which no blocking row exists. Inserting a blocker changes the result while the task rows used as positive inputs are untouched. A dependency policy that watches only modified positive contributors misses this change.

The current answer depends on the absence of a relevant blocker at a specified snapshot, not only on named records already present. Similar pressures arise from default assumptions, closed collections, negation and certain aggregates. Their exact semantics are method-specific. An incomplete real source collection cannot support the fixture's closed-world interpretation by resemblance alone.

This is stronger than a general admonition to refresh caches: a result's applicability can depend on conditions that were not positive output contributors. It is not an argument for one mandatory dependency database or universal invalidation engine.

## 3. Three meanings of a change must remain distinct

A world may change; an earlier representation may be discovered wrong; or a representation may cease to be an accepted basis for reliance. These can produce similar new text but different temporal and evidential claims. A retrospective correction must not automatically imply that the earlier world changed at the correction time. A current observation must not rewrite what was previously known.

Records' accepted change/time synthesis distinguishes effective versus recorded time, correction, supersession, reliance withdrawal and availability. C13 further shows that independently true p and q at nonoverlapping intervals do not establish p-and-q at a common time. Joining latest-arriving statements without their reference scopes can create a condition that never held jointly in the fixture.

## 4. Withdrawal is not negation

C12 removes reliance on q from a finite state while retaining p. The resulting permissible models include both q and not-q. It is now unestablished, not thereby false. If a different independent route still supports q, removal of one source need not remove even that warrant. Conversely, preserving the old wording does not preserve its former support.

Crossref's update guidance gives an independently maintained documentary example: an update notice and the target article can remain different records [S7]. This is publishing-specific pressure, not a universal rule that every corrected artifact needs a DOI or that retraction proves every proposition false.

## 5. Revision can have more than one admissible result

C11 begins with p-and-q, receives not-both and maximizes preservation of old positive literals under that constraint. Two choices tie: p-and-not-q, or not-p-and-q. The new constraint alone does not select one. A skeptical result retains what both alternatives license; an explicit preference may legitimately choose, but it is an additional basis.

This limited example is consistent with the primary belief-change paper's treatment of multiple extensions, choice and skeptical revision [S8]. Our code is a finite illustration, not an implementation or verification of its general operator. We do not import a rule that the latest assertion always wins or that all reasonable revision policies are equivalent. The paper itself exposes disagreement over iterated-revision conditions.

In contrast with a proof of a conclusion from fixed premises, revision asks what a continuing informational state should retain, withdraw or replace under its admitted constraints. Keeping all earlier claims unchanged can violate the revision contract; simply adding the new sentence can preserve the conflict rather than address it. A legitimate revision may intentionally stop preserving old implications.

## 6. Computed proposal, applied revision and propagation

C14 computes a candidate replacement without altering the current dictionary until an explicit application step. Producing a proposed new state is not installing it. Installing it locally is not exposing it to every downstream consumer. Neither operation proves that its authority, evidence basis or benefit is adequate.

This does not prescribe two-phase commits or an approval workflow. It preserves distinctions already protected by Work, Control and Propagation. For Information, the relevant question is exactly which maintained representation/reliance state changed, under which basis, and what historic state remains distinguishable.

## 7. Why revision cannot be dismissed as an update verb

Routine byte editing, source correction, contraction, revising a belief set, changing a current designation and responding to world change do not all have the same success criteria. The proposed inquiry is not every operation named update. It is the temporal relation over continuing information and its support/reliance status.

An artifact can perfectly report the inconsistency and contain a valid diagnosis while leaving the managed current-state designation inappropriate. Transfer and inference checks alone do not settle that maintained-state question. T4 therefore tests revision as a separate research region rather than assuming that the original parent-level phrase 'specialization of Derivation' implies permanent leaf status.
