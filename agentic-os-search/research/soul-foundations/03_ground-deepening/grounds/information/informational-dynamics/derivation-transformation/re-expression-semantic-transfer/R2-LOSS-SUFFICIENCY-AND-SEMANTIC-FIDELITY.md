# R2 — Loss, Sufficiency and Semantic Fidelity

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded argument and explicit finite constructions.

## 1. Truth-preserving does not mean purpose-preserving

In C02 a source fixes both completion and the presence of a blocker. A derivative asserting only completion is a source-supported weakening, yet cannot settle the blocker question. Conversely, narrowing two permitted source interpretations to one excludes an alternative without justification. Omission and unsupported strengthening are opposite directions of a transfer defect; either can be hidden by smooth prose.

For a stipulated nonempty class of source-compatible interpretations S and target-compatible interpretations T in an aligned comparison universe, S being a subset of T expresses one form of truth-preserving weakening. It does not establish useful precision. Requiring equality would disallow legitimate abstraction. An exact protected question q may instead require equality of its possible answers q[S] and q[T], including ambiguity, under a fixed interpretation bridge. This is illustrative set semantics, not a universal natural-language evaluator.

The RDF purpose warning is an independently specified formal counterexample [S3]. It concerns RDF 1.1 rather than a universal semantics of every document. Our examples extend the diagnostic question; they do not import RDF as an implementation.

## 2. The inherited decoder criterion is about recoverability, not every meaning claim

Formation F2 and Derivation T2 already established the exact finite-source condition. For a declared class X, transformation f and question q, a decoder r with r(f(x))=q(x) exists exactly when equal outputs never identify different q answers. C01 verifies the condition by two calculation paths over eight source worlds and all 256 Boolean questions: 16 survive the two-bit projection. This is a larger construction of an inherited fact, not a new mathematical discovery.

The existential decoder is essential to interpret correctly. It establishes that the target contains enough information for some decoder, not that the intended recipient will use that decoder or that a literal target sentence has the desired meaning. In C11 two inverse substitutions recover the original label but the intermediate label has the opposite stipulated meaning. Recoverability survives; intended semantic fidelity fails.

Accordingly, a practical transfer claim needs the actual intended interpretation d and relevant background B, not merely existence of a repairing decoder: compare d(f(x),B) with the protected source answer under the declared contract. B must not silently smuggle in the missing target answer. A versioned dictionary can legitimately provide interpretation; an oracle that supplies the omitted fact changes what is being tested.

## 3. Legitimate loss needs a bounded purpose, not a universal prohibition

A shorter representation can preserve all questions needed for one use and fail a newly introduced question. C14 drops actor identity while retaining a count. The public-count contract passes; the identity-dependent contract fails. The latter failure does not retroactively invalidate the former judgment. It does invalidate any unqualified claim that the derivative preserved everything needed for all later work.

The relevant question family cannot be selected solely by what the derivative happens to contain. Its justification comes from the source task and protected use, and may require inquiry when the future use is not yet known. A low-cost orientation may legitimately defer detail to recoverable sources. Unknown future requirements cannot be certified by a finite checklist as exhaustive.

The same action is also not the same semantic content. Two different accounts can induce one unchanged action yet differ in attribution, explanation or audit meaning. Policy-specific decision preservation is a narrower claim than general semantic fidelity.

## 4. Small numerical error can be a large decision error

C09 maps 49.6 to 50. The absolute error is within a stipulated 0.5 tolerance, but a decision using >=50 changes. No empirical rate or universal tolerance is inferred.

For a scalar threshold t, if |x-y| <= epsilon and |x-t| > epsilon, x and y are on the same side of the threshold. The two cases x>t+epsilon and x<t-epsilon prove the claim directly. A finite rational-grid check tests 960 eligible pairs and finds no violation. Near the boundary that guarantee is unavailable; an interval or an explicit undecided answer can preserve what is supported better than a false point certainty. Choosing the tolerance, threshold and acceptable response remains use-specific.

This distinction prevents a generic numeric, lexical or embedding-similarity score from becoming an unqualified proof of meaning preservation. Such scores can still be useful evidence for the property they actually test.

## 5. What source-derived and output-derived questions each miss

C13 asks questions only about the surviving completed field after a blocker is dropped. Every such check passes. A separately specified source/task blocker question fails. The test demonstrates a coverage blind spot in the stipulated evaluator, not a failure rate for any named metric.

The reverse direction also matters: checking that required source content is present does not exclude an unsupported addition. QuestEval explicitly combines the two directions within one particular learned framework [S6]. Its question generation, answer comparison and saliency choices remain method dependencies, not an exhaustive source-to-task contract. Passing every question that happened to be generated is not proof that every consequential question was generated.
