## 2. Common relation, not a universal operator

A revision claim needs the focal continuing information and its scope, old state, admitted change input, applicable constraints/comparison policy, resulting state or alternatives, and evidence of realization. Support, temporal interpretation, history, authority and future-use obligations enter when material. These are semantic questions, not required database fields or ordered implementation stages.

Captured input is not automatically an accepted premise. A reported failure, source withdrawal, new observation, hypothesized explanation, actual world event and changed revision policy have different possible meanings. A justified outcome can be qualified retention, contraction, rejection/suspension of an input, multiple scoped alternatives, or an explicit choice. No newest-message-wins or unique-successor rule is inferred.

M1 protects withdrawal targets, grounded/alternative support and context plurality. M2 protects reference time, policy, applicability, installation and replay. The resumed analysis adds more discriminating tests of what preservation and constraint satisfaction mean.

## 3. Stronger state-preservation finding

Original C12 held the initial ordering fixed and compared two different revision operators. New D01 holds the operator fixed: two states agree on every one-step belief answer in the four-world domain, yet differ after the sequence p, q. The published KR2022 Definition 7 / Example 8 is reconstructed from its explicit text; the new Figure 2 was not visually recovered. D05 also shows that repeated admitted input can change future responses without changing the current belief answer.

Therefore a summary that preserves today's answer, or even an entire one-step answer profile, is not automatically an adequate substitute for the maintained state when further revision is part of the protected use.

A precise sufficient preservation condition can be stated for an explicit deterministic model. Let T_u update a full state by admitted input u, let A compress it, and let O give the protected observable. If a coarse transition S_u and observation O_bar satisfy

```
A(T_u(e)) = S_u(A(e))
O(e) = O_bar(A(e))
```

for the relevant reachable states and admitted inputs, induction preserves the observations along their finite update sequences. A deterministic S_u on A-images exists exactly when equal A-images have equal A-images after every such input, with the domain closed under the modeled transitions. This is standard transition-factorization reasoning and a specialization of P2's translation perspective, not a newly claimed general representation theorem.

Scope matters. The protected inputs, observations and horizon must be declared; our five finite cases do not verify this law for an actual agent. Preserving a state through compression/handoff differs from deliberately revising that state or its policy: legitimate substantive revision may intentionally change future behavior. Current-only uses may need much less state, and explicit policy change may justify discarding an old disposition. No OCF, complete log, frozen behavior or maximal memory prescription follows.

## 4. Admissible does not mean required or uniquely selected

D02 reconstructs two admissible extensions and distinguishes selecting one from keeping only their common consequences. In model-set terms the skeptical result allows either alternative; intersecting their incompatible model sets incorrectly produces inconsistency. This does not prescribe skepticism for every task.

D03 separates a compatibility filter from a premise that the result must entail. A state can remain compatible with b while still allowing not-b. The new mixed-time version exposes the same risk in a literal reading of P3's dynamic-constraint paragraph; the [source addendum](M5-SOURCE-AND-EXECUTION-ADDENDUM.md) keeps that source tension and visual/interpretive uncertainty open. The bounded static distinction does not depend on resolving it.

D04 shows that inclusion-maximal preservation need not preserve the greatest number of old commitments. Both a one-commitment set and an incomparable two-commitment set can be maximal by inclusion. A numerical preference is an additional comparison rule, not a consequence of the word minimal. These distinctions qualify revision policy; they do not supply the appropriate empirical policy for SOUL.
