# W3 — Defeaters, Selection and the Limits of Reliance

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded analysis; no Revision or Selection pass activated.

## 1. A claim can lose this support without becoming false

Pollock distinguishes a reason against a conclusion from a reason that attacks the link between premises and conclusion, and discusses alternative support routes [S7]. His chapter presents his own theory among competing accounts; this pass uses the distinction, not all of its cognitive or defeat-semantics commitments.

C07 stipulates a tiny defeasible rule: an alert supports H unless a sensor-path defeater is present. Adding that defeater leaves the alert itself intact but makes this route unavailable. Both H and not-H remain possible under the stipulated residual evidence. A separate admitted support route can still support H; explicit contrary evidence is another input and can yield an opposed or contested status.

This is not a general implementation of Pollock, argumentation semantics or a real sensor diagnosis. A mere imaginable exception is not automatically an established defeater. Its credibility and relevance also need support. The distinction prevents two opposite errors: ignoring a material challenge because the original observation remains unchanged, and treating every challenge as proof of the opposite conclusion.

## 2. Support is not just a connected graph

A conclusion may require premises jointly, have alternative reasons, or be challenged at one specific inference. The accepted parent already preserves joint and alternative dependencies. C08 adds a simple grounding check: rules a->b and b->a with no seed derive neither under the specified forward-rule semantics; a seed permits both. Graph reachability does not create an original basis.

This is not a blanket ban on feedback, recursive definitions, mutual constraints or fixed-point mathematics. Such structures can be justified under their actual semantics. The error is counting a cycle of repeated assertions as independently grounded evidence without such a basis. Nor does adding one seed establish its truth merely because closure can then be calculated.

## 3. How evidence was selected can change the inference

The ASA's official 2016 release reproduces principles separating p-values from hypothesis probabilities and warns about selective analysis/reporting [S2]. It does not prescribe a universal alternative method. C09 supplies a completely specified null experiment: four independent p-values uniformly distributed on {1/20,...,1}. Each individually crosses <=1/20 with probability 1/20, while at least one crossing occurs with probability 29679/160000, approximately 0.1855. Enumeration of all 160,000 outcomes agrees with the formula.

The numerical result is not an empirical false-discovery rate for agents, nor evidence that all multiple or sequential methods are invalid. Valid methods can explicitly account for their selection/stopping rule. The protected distinction is that a result selected after several opportunities cannot automatically borrow a single preselected test's guarantee.

Selection/Exposure still owns how existing information becomes visible. Warrant owns what can be concluded from that selected information under the actual selection mechanism. One operation can implicate both without this boundary probe completing the Selection research.

## 4. Calibration is about a specified population and condition

Guo et al. state a calibration criterion tying predictive confidence to accuracy and describe same-distribution assumptions for their calibration setting [S5]. This is a historical methodological source, not a verdict on today's models.

C10 constructs ten equiprobable cases, all with reported confidence 4/5. Eight predictions are correct overall, but the two five-case subgroups have accuracies 1 and 3/5. The predictor satisfies the stipulated overall confidence-bin criterion while neither subgroup matches 4/5. This exact population construction is not a finite-data estimate or proof of individual probabilities. It shows why changing the conditioning scope requires a new warrant rather than inheriting the aggregate verdict.

The issue is not solved merely by renaming a score confidence. One must identify whether it is a model probability, frequency guarantee, ranking, fit statistic, elicited judgment or calibrated prediction, and for which target and conditions it is supported. This pass selects no universal scalar or threshold.

## 5. Epistemic support does not decide action or install revision

C12 holds the posterior at 4/5 while changing an explicit loss table. Equal false-positive/false-negative costs favor acting; a false-positive cost of ten versus a false-negative cost of one favors waiting. This is a small expected-loss calculation, not a general decision theory or recommendation for a real task. No operational permission follows from either optimum.

Work and Actors/Control retain objective, authority and actual action. Standing retains appropriate qualification and assessment. Maintained-Information Revision retains what is actually kept, withdrawn or replaced in a continuing information state. This Warrant pass can establish that a support route is defeated without thereby updating every maintained representation or downstream consumer. It does not make Revision redundant.

## 6. What a next question should address

An actor faced with uncertainty need not always request more source text. It may need to expose a missing condition, test whether a model applies here, distinguish alternatives, check whether two reports share an origin, recover a hidden selection rule, or separate a defeated reason from a refuted claim. In another case, the available basis may already be sufficient for a clearly conditional answer. These are prospective diagnostic implications, not new behavioral rules installed in SOUL or demonstrated work-quality gains.
