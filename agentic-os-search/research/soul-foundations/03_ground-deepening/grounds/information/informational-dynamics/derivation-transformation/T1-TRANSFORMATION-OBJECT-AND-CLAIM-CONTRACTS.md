# T1 — Transformation Object and Claim Contracts

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** bounded node analysis; structural decision in T4/T6

## 1. More than an output artifact

The object is an informational transition or dependency: existing information participates in a new representation, derived conclusion or changed maintained informational state. Input and output need not be separate files; unchanged payload can acquire a different reliance designation, while different bytes can preserve a protected meaning. An actual transformation can be defective. Its occurrence, adequacy and evidential establishment are different claims.

Use the term **transformation contract** for the meaning of the task: what is to be preserved, inferred or revised, with which assumptions, scope, tolerances and intended use. This is a semantic condition, not necessarily a written specification, API contract, software type or mandatory stored object.

A common source-to-output record can establish production history without settling whether the result is justified. PROV distinguishes use/production from derivational influence and does not itself determine every influence relation [S1]. Our additional distinction is between a historical dependency and the inferential support that licenses a claim. A misleading source can influence a result without supporting it.

## 2. Five questions that must not become one success flag

| Question | Possible evidence | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Did the result or change occur? | exact result/readback, observed transition | correct meaning or sound reasoning |
| Which inputs actually contributed? | suitable execution/lineage evidence, causal analysis where claimed | that those inputs justify the conclusion |
| Was the relevant meaning transferred adequately? | source/output comparison under the protected-use contract | truth of the source or all omitted matters |
| Is the derived claim licensed? | proof, model, statistical or other appropriate warrant with assumptions | actual truth if premises/model are wrong; automatic decision sufficiency |
| Is the new maintained information state appropriate under its revision basis? | old/new state, new constraints, support/priority and history | legitimate authority or downstream application everywhere |

These questions describe claim scopes. T4 separately asks which recurring first-order inquiries justify child research; a table of claims is not automatically a child hierarchy.

## 3. Fidelity, warrant and world truth separate

C01 faithfully copies a false source assertion. C03 reaches a proposition true in the stipulated actual world through an invalid inference from a disjunction. Neither world truth nor source fidelity repairs the other missing relation. A useful assessment can state that a result is accurately quoted, weakly supported, currently unverified, or sound conditional on assumptions, rather than collapse these into one trusted label.

The output contract matters. Reporting what a source says protects attribution and meaning. Correcting that source against new evidence may deliberately depart from its content. A hypothetical alternative may be worthwhile without being asserted as actual. False content is not excluded from Information, and a generated candidate is not automatically a hallucination in every task. Unsupported assertion under a factual-summary contract and explicitly marked hypothesis under an exploration contract are different cases.

The historical summarization study [S4] independently distinguishes faithfulness to a document from factuality and permits discussion of background-supported content. We use that distinction, not its historical model rates, as pressure against one universal output-validity test.

## 4. Conditional conclusions and implicit resources

Let inputs assert p implies q. Producing q is not licensed unless p or another sufficient basis is supplied. C05 checks that exact contrast. A method may legitimately draw on background knowledge, training-derived capability, a model or additional assumptions, but those are not magically contained in the cited document. Their role must remain visible when it changes the claim's warrant.

This is not a demand to reconstruct every hidden token-level computation. The epistemic question is what support is established at the claimed scope. In opaque systems actual input use may remain uncertain even when a proposed derivation is valid. Conversely, a detailed reasoning trace can faithfully describe an unsound argument.

Classical deduction, approximate estimation, abductive explanation and candidate generation need not share one success criterion. Logical validity is a useful special case, not the definition of every good informational transformation.

## 5. Identity, scope and operation labels

Copy, summarize, merge, update, infer, normalize and correct do not classify the node's internals reliably. One correction may re-express a statement, derive a replacement from evidence and revise the maintained current version. One query may expose existing records and calculate a new aggregate. Identity/version, source roles, units, population, time and modality qualify these relations; omitted qualifiers can change the question rather than just reduce detail.

RDF's explicit truth-preserving entailment definition also warns that an entailed subgraph need not remain fit for a particular purpose [S2]. The Foundation inference is that a task contract cannot be replaced with entailment alone.

## 6. Ground and sibling limits

Formation still asks how the source-facing representation became available at the focal boundary. Selection asks which existing inputs or outputs reach a use. State asks what their content and constitution are. Work owns the pursuit, Actors/Control own standing and governing authority, and Propagation owns realized spread. Derivation studies the informational bridge and its semantic or epistemic effect, not a replacement workflow or control engine.

T2 and T3 deepen these distinctions enough to test the node structure. They do not constitute completed dedicated research of any proposed child.
