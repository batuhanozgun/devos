## 2. Correction that survived the pressure

The inherited decoder criterion remains correct within its formal scope. This pass narrows its use: some decoder existing is not the same claim as the derivative having the right meaning under the intended target interpretation. Native interchange cases and the constructed inverse-mislabeling case make that gap concrete. A usable transfer can depend on external interpretation resources; self-contained text is not universally required.

Likewise, small numerical or representational differences need not be small relative to a decision boundary. The threshold-margin construction is a bounded demonstration, not a new general evaluation metric.

## 3. Search and domain limits

Several searches returned generic homepages despite specific title snippets; those were not treated as recovered papers. Exact primary pages/PDFs were opened for the sources actually used. PDF visual material relevant to selected passages was inspected with screenshots. No OCR, live model call or human translation evaluation was performed.

The study includes symbolic, linguistic, numeric and structured-format pressure, but not a comprehensive multimodal, sign-language, diagram-comprehension or cognitive theory. R3's chart/table probes beyond the executed role/order fixtures are conceptual. A strong counterexample from those domains could reopen the organization.

Catalog discovery and previously read AI Knowledge Strategies metadata/current routes plus the limited RAG excerpt informed questions about source, context and generated output. ## 5. Structural information gain

After these probes, added distinctions concerned interpretation dependencies, permitted loss, scope, equivalence and validation coverage within the source-to-derivative question. They did not supply a demonstrated new first-order child beyond the rivals explicitly examined in R4. This supports a bounded stop, not exhaustive search or proof that no other meaningful decomposition can exist.
