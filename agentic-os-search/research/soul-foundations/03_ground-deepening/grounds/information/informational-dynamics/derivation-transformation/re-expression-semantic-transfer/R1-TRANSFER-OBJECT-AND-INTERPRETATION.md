# R1 — Transfer Object and Interpretation

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Status:** dedicated bounded analysis; structural judgment in R4/R6.

## 1. The object is an interpreted source-to-derivative relation

Re-expression asks whether a derivative, under the interpretation appropriate to its recipient and use, carries the source meanings or distinctions it is supposed to carry. A byte string, fluent sentence or syntactically valid record is not by itself the whole transfer object. Source and target may have different forms, granularity, vocabulary and allowable omissions. They may be different views rather than distinct persistent files.

A minimal analysis names the exact source/view, derivative, source/target interpretation, protected question or representational obligation, permissible abstraction, and material dependencies. It need not store these as fields. A routine conversion can rely on a stable shared convention; an ambiguous or consequential handoff requires more explicit binding.

Three importantly different evaluations remain: what information could in principle be recovered; what the intended interpretation actually expresses; and what a particular consumer can successfully use. A reversible encoding can satisfy the first while a missing decoder defeats the third. More subtly, two inverse mistranslations can preserve round-trip bytes while the intermediate text means the wrong thing under the intended target language. These are not equivalent success claims.

## 2. Meaning is not a bag of retained words

C03 swaps reviewer and approver assignments between two synthetic actors. Every token is retained, but the role relation changes. C04 retains both operations of a two-step instruction while changing their order; the stipulated sequence semantics yields a different outcome. These tests do not implement a natural-language interpreter. They show exactly which relation and ordering obligations the chosen token-only checks cannot certify.

Other unexecuted conceptual probes include negation scope, quantifier order, a condition versus its consequent, a quotation versus the quoted proposition, and an estimate versus a measurement. For example, each actor approving some version does not require one version approved by every actor. Preserving every name and verb would not settle that binding.

The accepted State/Binding results already protect identity, scope, form and role. This child investigates what happens to those features during re-expression. It does not create a new generic Relation or Meaning ground.

## 3. Source fidelity can preserve uncertainty and disagreement

An adequate derivative may need to preserve what the source does not decide. Re-expressing two remaining alternatives as one confident answer is unsupported narrowing unless a legitimate additional basis is introduced and marked. A source that records conflicting assertions can be faithfully represented as a conflict; it need not be flattened into a supposedly coherent consensus.

This is not a requirement to copy ambiguity forever. A justified disambiguation may use context or fresh evidence. The transfer judgment must not credit that additional warrant to the source alone. Correcting a source is also legitimate under a correction task, but differs from accurately reporting its contents. Inferential Construction and Revision retain these adjacent questions.

Classical possible-world semantics below is only a finite illustrative model. If source assertions are inconsistent, blindly treating their model set as empty would make entailment vacuous. Representing who asserted what, or choosing an explicit nonclassical treatment, is different from claiming all consequences as source-supported truths. No logic is adopted universally.

## 4. The recipient's interpretive resources are part of the claim

In C10 the identical code A7 means blocked in one stipulated dictionary and ready in another. The transfer can be correct under the intended version and wrong under another without any changed code bytes. Shared vocabulary, codebook, units, schema, language, surrounding context or a source link may supply interpretation resources.

A resource that exists somewhere is not necessarily available to the recipient when needed. But not every representation must become self-contained: a reference-backed derivative can be adequate if the relevant source/decoder is actually recoverable within the declared use conditions. Such a route must not be described as preservation inside the standalone summary. Records handles future survival; Selection handles exposure; Environment/Actors/Work supply actual capability and use conditions.

The MQM paper's context-sensitive annotation instructions provide a linguistic pressure case [S7]. Its exact taxonomy and scoring are not imported here. The primary RFC interchange examples [S2] independently show that producer and consumer interpretation capabilities matter even for structured values.

## 5. Source truth and authorized use remain outside automatic transfer success

Faithfully translating a false claim does not make it true. Faithfully representing an instruction does not grant its speaker authority. A publicly readable but source-faithful derivative can still disclose information it was not permitted to disclose. Conversely, authorized omission can be correct under a restricted-view contract even though identity questions cease to be answerable.

C14 demonstrates only this purpose-relative preservation contrast; it does not verify privacy or grant redaction authority. R2 preserves allowed-loss and uncertainty distinctions without turning every transformation into an information-maximization task.
