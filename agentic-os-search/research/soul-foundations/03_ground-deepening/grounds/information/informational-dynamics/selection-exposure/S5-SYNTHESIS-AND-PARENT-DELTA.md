## 2. What must be distinguished?

A use can be served badly in at least two different informational ways: the supplied view is not the view the inquiry needs, or the appropriately designated information is not available to the consumer at the required endpoint. Correctness of source content does not establish either condition. A perfect copy of an inadequate selection remains inadequate; a correct shortlist can remain only a shortlist when the current use needs the underlying content.

Selection relates a need/inquiry state and an admissible candidate universe to a view: which resources, passages, versions, alternatives and order are required or sufficient under the declared purpose? Exposure relates specified information to consumer-relative availability: what is actually reachable or present, for whom, where and when, at the required representation and access mode? These are different protected relations, not an assertion that all systems must perform a choose-then-send sequence.

The source universe, index, returned candidates, eligible resources, intended view and actual consuming view must not silently substitute for one another. Coverage statements need the corresponding denominator. A closed complete exact lookup can justify a bounded absence inference; an open or partial discovery miss generally cannot supply the same guarantee. E01 preserves both directions, rather than forbidding all negative evidence.

## 3. A good item is not necessarily a good set

Several individually relevant documents can repeat the same evidence while omitting a blocker, exception or complementary premise. The stipulated E02 case has perfect item precision but incomplete task coverage. Its alternative ranking is an illustrative mechanism, not a recommended universal solution. A diversity score can still select irrelevant novelty or omit jointly needed material; semantic similarity does not establish source independence.

Adequacy is relative to the whole selected view and the use, not necessarily the sum of item scores. E03 makes feasibility explicit: insufficient budget or legitimate access restrictions can leave no sufficient eligible set. The correct conclusion is bounded insufficiency or an authorized change of prerequisites, not relabeling best effort as success. Excessive disclosure is not the default remedy.

The information need itself can evolve during inquiry. S2's P7 counterframe corrects any reading of a fixed q as a universal lifetime assumption. A system can meet a prior query and fail a newly discovered requirement. Tracking that change differs from quietly changing the user's authorized goal. No automatic goal-rewriting policy follows.

## 4. Identity, version and consumer state

A resource address can resolve to different representations. An offset can name different text after an edit. A quote can have multiple matches. E04 shows why exact selected identity may require representation/version/context binding. This is purpose-relative: an exploratory title search does not need the same granularity as a reproducible passage claim.

Freshness is also qualified. E05's newest members cannot be interpreted together; the compatible pair contains an older schema. Latest, currently applicable, mutually coherent and appropriate as-of are different conditions. Their required strength depends on the use; there is no universally mandatory freshest snapshot.

Exposure claims must stop at the endpoint actually observed. Being catalogued, having a locator, being accessible through a path, being included in a current working input and being successfully used are different claims. Not every use requires every step. E06's local JSON observations separate chosen IDs from present content without claiming a model consumed either. E07 distinguishes a complete planned bundle from the budgeted view actually supplied. P6's historical model evidence separately warns against equating input presence with successful use; our truncation case is not its replication.

## 6. Why the parent is split at this depth

S4 compares the rich-leaf rival seriously. The new pair is not justified solely by seven green cases or different operations. Need-to-view fitness and consumer-relative availability can vary under unchanged source content and have distinct research obligations even when no new acquisition, revision or explicit transport stage occurs. Retaining both only as an undifferentiated visibility metric would lose that distinction. Direct facets remain a credible lower-complexity alternative; the current preference is for explicit research duties at Foundation scale.

The cost is shared identity/time/eligibility material and another level of research. The decision remains a same-assistant bounded organizational judgment. A later child pass can refine or merge the questions if the promised diagnostic benefit fails or exposure becomes merely a duplicate of Formation/Propagation. No uniqueness theorem, independent taxonomy verification or empirical superiority is claimed.

## 7. SOUL and Development OS implication, not design adoption

A future research worker may possess correct sources yet give another actor a redundant or incomplete evidence set. It may also choose the right evidence but leave the next actor with only filenames, an old view or missing qualifications. These failures call for different questions before adding more documents. A useful handoff must identify the protected informational need and the actual availability it establishes, without claiming that the receiving actor has understood or acted on it.

This is an analytical implication, not a new SOUL prompt, schema, orchestration rule or implementation. Concrete workloads still need measured relevance, completeness, access, latency, interpretation and actual use evidence.
