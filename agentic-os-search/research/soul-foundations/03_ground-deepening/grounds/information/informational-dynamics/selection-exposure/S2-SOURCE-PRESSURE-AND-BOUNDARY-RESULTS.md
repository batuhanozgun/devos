# S2 — Source pressure and boundary results

**Date:** 2026-09-14. **Standing:** source-informed analysis; structural comparison remains S4.
**Primary reading extents:** P1–P6 in SOURCE-LEDGER; later P7 below. Analytical applications are not observed product defects.

## 1. A score on one surface cannot stand for the whole inquiry

P1 separates relevance to an information need from matching query words; P2 supplies an explicit relevance/novelty tradeoff over already selected items. Neither supplies a universal adequacy oracle. E02 therefore asks a narrower question under an explicit required set: can a perfectly relevant prefix still omit a necessary kind of information? Its fixed arithmetic answers yes without proving MMR always solves the problem.

Our extension is use-relative set admissibility. Let C be a bounded candidate set and A_q a family of subsets/orders satisfying a stated information requirement q, with identity, permissions, budget and joint-consistency constraints when material. Selecting a high-scoring member outside A_q does not satisfy q. If A_q is empty under the fixed candidates/constraints, a selector cannot repair the situation by calling its best output sufficient. Changing q, candidate discovery, permissions or budget is a separate, potentially authorized intervention; it is not a hidden successful search.

This notation is an analytical model, not proof that an actual task supplies a known q or complete C. It does not reduce natural-language adequacy to set cover. E03 uses an intentionally tiny fully known instance. Exact task semantics and unknown sources remain evidence needs.

The paper distinguishes evolving needs and accumulated information from a single fixed-query/final-set model. This is conceptual and historically grounded pressure, not a new human experiment or a universal account of all search. We do not adopt its interface proposals or claim that all real searches require changing goals.

**Correction to an overbroad reading of S1:** fixing q is a local evaluation frame, not an assumption that the user's informational need stays unchanged throughout research. A useful discovery can revise the next question, source territory or strategy. Such changes must remain visible so a metric on yesterday's need does not certify today's sufficiency. This does not authorize an agent to substitute a new end goal for the user's goal. The seven finite cases remain static, bounded tests; none validates adaptive need discovery.

## 3. The selected object needs more than an address

P3 distinguishes the resource representation from its fragment selector. The analytical lesson is not that every item needs a particular metadata format, but that selection must bind whichever identity/version/interpretation distinctions make its target determinate. E04 separates an unchanged offset from an unchanged selected passage and an exact quote from a unique occurrence.

P4 additionally distinguishes reusable HTTP responses from application-level display behavior. E05 is our separate consistency example, not an RFC example: individually newest members can violate a joint version requirement. A historical or older compatible view can be right for a declared use; the strongest possible freshness is not universally the goal.

## 4. Availability has an endpoint; use has further conditions

P5 places delivery evidence at an application-dependent endpoint rather than treating every acknowledgment as the same guarantee. P6's historical language-model experiments provide a different pressure: even information inside an input can fail to be used successfully. We use no current-model generalization or imported numerical score.

E06 realizes all four combinations of selected-set adequacy and exact local content availability. That demonstrates their logical and local operational separability, not that two Foundation children must follow. A locator is sufficient when the intended service is discovery of a locator; it is insufficient when the protected endpoint requires the content already present. This qualification avoids condemning lazy retrieval or references universally.

E07 checks the actual budgeted input, not a producer's larger intended bundle. Order and truncation are part of what reaches the endpoint. This finite truncation result differs from P6's empirical within-context use effects; they must not be conflated.

## 5. Boundaries and legitimate limitations

Availability need not entail interpretation, acceptance or action. Exposure can be realized through an existing shared resource without creating new content; receiver-side copying or interpretation may additionally raise Formation/Re-expression questions. Permission is governed by Actors/Control, not granted by a relevance score. A compliant restricted view can be intentionally incomplete for an unauthorized broader purpose.

Source truth and evidential sufficiency remain Warrant/Standing/Work questions. Selection can protect contrary evidence and context without certifying their correctness. Propagation protects actual cross-ground spread; a narrowly qualified information-to-consumer endpoint must not claim universal delivery, observation or world-effect coverage.

## 6. What remains to decide

The evidence requires distinct diagnostics for choosing a use-fit view and establishing consumer-relative availability. It does not mechanically settle whether those should be immediate children or explicit facets of one rich leaf. S4 must test the stronger rich-leaf rival, especially against Formation F4 and Revision M5, rather than promoting a pipeline or multiplying nodes because examples can fail independently.
