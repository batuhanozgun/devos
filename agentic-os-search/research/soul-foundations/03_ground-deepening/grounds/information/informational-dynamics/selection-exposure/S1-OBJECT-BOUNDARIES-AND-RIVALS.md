## 1. What is protected?

An existing informational resource can be correctly formed, represented, supported and maintained yet fail to serve a use because the wrong resource, fragment, version, subset or order reaches that use—or because the chosen information never becomes available there. Conversely, accessible selected information need not be true, sufficient to justify a claim, interpreted, accepted or acted upon.

Fix a consumer, use/question, decision time or as-of scope, candidate universe and relevant interpretation/authority context. These are analytical variables, not required stored fields. Selection acts on already-existing information relative to that frame. An external fetch may also form a local copy; a selected excerpt may be transformed. Shared operations do not collapse the separately protected questions.

## 2. Distinguish the information need from its proxy

A query, keyword, embedding or category is a representation of an information need, not its complete definition. A system may answer its literal query correctly while omitting the evidence needed for the authorized Work. This is not always a faulty search implementation: the requested universe or question may be wrong.

A bounded exact identifier lookup, exploratory discovery, exhaustive contrary-evidence search and historical reconstruction have different success conditions. More matches or a better average item score does not uniformly improve them. The protected loss may concern missing one blocker rather than retrieving many relevant descriptions. Appropriate abstention or explicit limits can be a correct result.

## 3. Universe and availability are qualified

Distinguish intended information universe, known/catalogued resources, indexed resources, reachable resources, eligible resources and actual exposed resources. Equal cardinalities do not establish equal membership. A perfect search over an incomplete index does not certify absence from the source universe. A deliberately restricted recipient view may correctly omit protected information; disclosure is not universally better.

Eligibility can depend on referent, role, purpose, time, source standing, resource capability and current permission. Actors/Control determine legitimate authority; Selection must not silently treat relevance as permission or a stale authorization label as actual access. Source existence, a discoverable locator, possession of bytes and availability in the consuming context are distinct predicates.

## 4. Item quality is not set adequacy

A task may require complementary facts, contrary evidence, provenance, a dependency chain, an exception or a jointly interpretable snapshot. Several individually relevant items can repeat the same source and leave a decisive subquestion unsupported. Deduplication by bytes can also erase separate occurrences whose identity matters. Neither maximum diversity nor maximum duplication avoidance is universally appropriate.

Ranking defines order under a criterion. A budgeted prefix changes what can be exposed. Non-additive requirements mean that independently scoring items may select an inadequate set. Coverage needs a denominator; known subquestions do not prove discovery of all material subquestions. Evidence sufficiency remains claim-relative Warrant/Work, while selection sufficiency concerns whether the required inputs are present under that declared use.

## 5. Identity, state and timing

Correctly locating a title need not locate its exact source, fragment, version, stance or applicability. Removing an essential qualifier changes the view's usable meaning; semantic transfer retains that problem. A latest recorded item can be inappropriate for an as-of question. Individually recent items may still be mutually incompatible versions under a shared task contract.

The required guarantee may be eventual availability, a coherent snapshot, latest approved version, a deadline-specific view or an intentionally historical state. No universally strongest or cost-free freshness requirement follows. A pre-existing update may invalidate a selected result before exposure; the publication observation and later use time differ.

## 6. Selection versus exposure: strongest initial split

Candidate A, **Use-conditioned selection / set construction**, asks which candidate resources/parts/order supply the required input view for a use. Failure can survive perfect delivery: the delivered set is the wrong set. Its corrective questions concern universe, need, eligibility, completeness, complementarity, ranking and allowed omission.

Candidate B, **Recipient-scoped exposure / availability**, asks whether the designated view becomes actually available to the designated consumer under the required locus, version and time conditions. Failure can survive a correct selected set: only a locator is delivered, the wrong audience receives it, required bytes are omitted from a request, access is unavailable or the cached view is stale. Corrective questions concern effective availability and its observation, not rescoring candidates.

The strongest leaf rival treats both as one qualified source-to-consumer relation with explicit independently checked facets. It avoids treating a temporal pipeline as ontology. The pair merits promotion only if it preserves distinct first-order inquiry beyond such adequate facet routing; shared notation and overlap are not objections by themselves. The parent N6's earlier combined-label judgment is a starting hypothesis, not a prohibition on dedicated depth.
