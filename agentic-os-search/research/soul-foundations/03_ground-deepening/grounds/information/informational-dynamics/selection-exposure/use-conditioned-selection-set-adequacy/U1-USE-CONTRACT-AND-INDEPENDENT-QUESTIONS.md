# U1 — Use contract and independent question inventory

**Date:** 2026-09-15
**Standing:** pre-external-reading analysis; open hypotheses, not a child/leaf decision.
**Basis:** accepted Selection S1–S5; Formation F2/F4/F6; State constitution; Revision M5; Records organisation/retrieval.

## 1. The focal relation

The target is not the biggest context, the best-ranked document or a predetermined final decision. It is the relationship between a protected informational use, the available admissible alternatives and the view selected to serve that use. A faithful, accessible view can still be inadequate. Conversely, a useful bounded view can intentionally omit information that the present use does not require.

A use may be making a choice, testing an explanation, checking an exception, designing the next question, recovering a reason, or reconstructing an earlier state. Selection must state what it claims to support without taking over Work's authority to define goals, Warrant's justification, or Exposure's actual consumer access.

The user's concern about later SOUL decisions is therefore not answered by counting saved findings. A finding may matter because it rules out a mechanism, states when a guarantee fails, supplies a discriminating question, or preserves an unresolved alternative. Not every useful finding directly changes the chosen action, and not every stored finding must be selected now.

## 2. Need, query and adequacy rule are not interchangeable

A query is a proxy for an informational need. An adequacy test is another representation of that need. Both can omit something material even when retrieval or scoring perfectly implements them. A named checklist of known subquestions does not prove that all decision-relevant subquestions are known.

The inherited parent separates item relevance from whole-set coverage. This child must go deeper: coverage of what, and why is that coverage sufficient for the protected use? Does the use require identifying the exact state, identifying a permissible action, supporting a stated explanation, recovering a source's exact words, or exploring what should be asked next? The same selected content can pass one and fail another.

An important rival is to make need/adequacy specification a separate child from constrained view construction. Its benefit would be exposing the otherwise invisible error of optimizing the wrong information requirement. Its cost is potentially absorbing goal negotiation and claim validation from Work/Warrant. It remains open, not rejected because one large relation could describe both.

## 3. Decision preservation is a candidate narrower sufficiency notion

Under an explicitly supplied finite model, exact state identification may be stronger than necessary for a particular action. Several indistinguishable states can admit the same best action. Conversely, agreeing with the action taken on one observed case does not show that a compressed view supports the choice across the still-compatible alternatives.

A candidate test is whether every set of states left indistinguishable by the view admits at least one action that meets the declared loss/constraint requirement throughout that set. For exact optimality this asks about common optimal actions, not necessarily a unique action or a fully identified world. Approximate use would need a declared allowed regret or loss. These are proposed formal lenses to derive and test, not a discovered natural-language adequacy oracle.

The baseline also matters. Preserving what the full available archive supports does not establish that the archive is complete or correct about the world. A decision-stable view can still lose the explanation, provenance or permitted-use boundary that an accountable decision requires. No action-only criterion should silently erase those separate obligations.

## 4. Marginal usefulness can depend on what is already known

A fragment might be useful only together with another fragment or interpretive key. Two items individually unable to distinguish an outcome can become informative jointly. An information item with large novelty or uncertainty reduction may concern a variable that does not affect the authorized use at all.

Probe whether a one-step rule that stops when every individual remaining item has negligible value can miss a valuable combination or an adaptive sequence. Conversely, never stopping is not a remedy: acquisition, reading, interpretation, delay and disclosure can impose legitimate costs or restrictions. The model must not turn free-information reasoning into a recommendation to load everything.

The parent's budget and duplicate examples establish bounded effects, not universal monotonicity or submodularity of useful information. Test any such property under its assumptions rather than importing it from a convenient optimization method.

## 5. Changing needs, future retention and permissible omission

A view sufficient under today's loss, action set, scope and remaining alternatives need not remain sufficient after those change. The accepted Revision result already distinguishes present answers from future continuation. Here the selection question is which preserved knowledge is needed for the present contract and what claimed reuse horizon remains justified.

A fact not selected today may still need a recoverable route for later inquiry. Conversely, keeping every source in every active view is neither necessary nor sufficient. Selecting, retaining, revalidating and making information available are distinct obligations. Unknown future questions are a limit on an adequacy guarantee, not automatic permission for maximal disclosure or a universal retention rule.

Selection for exploration is a strong counterframe to fixed-query optimization. The next useful outcome can be a better question rather than an immediately improved decision. Do not silently reclassify all discovery as a fixed answer task, or all changing questions as new user goals.

## 6. Planned adverse cases and rival structure

Proposed probes, not yet executed: a coarse view that loses exact state but preserves a particular decision; the same view failing a changed decision contract; two complementary bits with zero individual but positive joint usefulness; a high-entropy irrelevant signal; approximate sufficiency sensitive to a declared loss tolerance; and a source-independent requirement proxy that misses an actual constraint.

Live structures: one qualified need-to-view leaf; need/adequacy specification versus admissible-view construction; exploration/adaptive inquiry versus fixed-contract selection. Discovery, ranking, diversity, set cover, graphs, embeddings and databases are candidate mechanisms, not children by name. Neither a mathematical criterion nor successful finite examples decides this hierarchy.

## 7. Research-to-reuse obligation

At a usable checkpoint, a future reader should recover: the question a result addresses; what it establishes; the conditions on that result; the counterexample or reopening condition; the exact evidence and reading/execution depth; and what was deliberately not established. Use the repository's existing findings-map/source routes rather than assuming one mandatory SOUL metadata schema.

This does not make a result timeless truth or a runtime instruction. A later SOUL choice still needs a current target, applicable evidence and legitimate authority. U2 will test these proposed distinctions with primary sources; U3 will separately specify and execute bounded checks. U4 still owes the structural comparison and omitted-use challenge.
