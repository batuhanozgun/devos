## 3. Child protected questions

### Informational Formation / Acquisition

> How does a phenomenon, source act, signal, assertion or source resource become a newly instantiated informational state, and what observability, sampling, capture, parsing or source-interface failures arise during that formation?

Distinct failures include biased/partial acquisition, procedure/result binding loss, assertion-vs-observation confusion and corrupted source-to-representation formation.

### Informational Derivation / Transformation

> How do one or more existing informational states contribute to a new or changed informational state/standing, and what semantic, identity, lineage, applicability or standing effect does that transition have?

Distinct failures include invalid inference, lossy normalization/translation, distorted aggregation/summarization, revision/history loss, reconciliation errors and lineage loss.

### Informational Selection / Exposure

> How do already-existing informational states become eligible, selected, ranked, filtered, communicated or otherwise exposed to a consumer/use, and what omission, coverage, ordering, visibility or freshness failures arise without requiring the source content itself to change?

Distinct failures include retrieval omission, wrong-version exposure, ranking bias, stale context, delivery-without-interpretability and incomplete propagation to consumer-visible views.

## 4. Why the three-child split survives

The split is retained because three independent diagnostic questions repeatedly survive:

1. was the informational state appropriately **formed from its source/origin**?
2. was an informational state appropriately **derived/changed from existing information**?
3. did the right existing information become **visible/available to the relevant use/consumer**?

A system can pass any two and fail the third. The corresponding remedies differ: fix acquisition, fix transformation/derivation, or fix selection/exposure.

## 6. Important specializations / composite patterns

Not immediate children at this level:

- **Revision / Correction / Retraction / Supersession** — mainly a Derivation/Transformation specialization plus standing relations and downstream exposure/propagation.
- **Communication / Distribution** — can combine re-encoding/transformation, exposure and receiver-relative formation; sender/receiver identity and authority cross Actors.
- **Propagation / Synchronization** — crosses recomputation/invalidation (Transformation) and downstream visibility/exposure.
- **Conflict / Reconciliation / Merge** — conflict is a State relation; reconciliation is largely Transformation and may require Work/Actor authority.
- **Persistence / Memory / Records lifecycle** — deep persistence specialization crossing State and all three Dynamics children.
- **Encoding / Parsing / Schema mapping** — formation- or transformation-side mechanism depending the transition.
