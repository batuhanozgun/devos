## External evidence families

External taxonomies are falsification instruments, not hierarchy authorities.

### E01 — ILO Human Factors and Ergonomics
Source: International Labour Organization, `Human Factors and Ergonomics (HFE)`.
URL: https://www.ilo.org/ergonomics
Direct evidence:
- work systems combine humans, tasks, tools/technologies, organization and work environment;
- physical, cognitive and psychosocial aspects interact;
- HFE aims at performance and well-being across sectors/occupations.
Project use:
- challenges any narrow technical-only or physical-only Work Conditions frame;
- also shows a work-system taxonomy can mix semantics owned by Work, Actors and Environment, so it cannot be copied directly as project children.

### E02 — ILO / IEA work-system guidelines
Source: ILO, `Principles and guidelines for human factors / ergonomics design and management of work systems`, 2021.
URL: https://www.ilo.org/publications/principles-and-guidelines-human-factors-ergonomics-hfe-design-and
Direct evidence:
- physical, cognitive and organizational HFE must be integrated in work-system design.
Project use:
- cross-sector support for multidimensional Work Conditions without establishing a universal child taxonomy.

### E03 — ILO psychosocial working environment
Source: ILO, `The psychosocial working environment: Global developments and pathways for action`, 2026.
URL: https://www.ilo.org/publications/psychosocial-working-environment-global-developments-and-pathways-action
Direct evidence:
- psychosocial environment is shaped by job design, organization/management, and broader policies/practices/procedures;
- factors include workload, role clarity, autonomy, working time and fairness/processes.
Project use:
- demonstrates organizational/institutional conditions can materially shape Work;
- also shows some working-condition classifications include Work-local design features, so boundary protection is required.

### E04 — O*NET Work Context
Source: O*NET OnLine, `Browse by Work Context`.
URL: https://www.onetonline.org/find/descriptor/browse/4.C
Direct evidence:
- Work Context is defined as physical and social factors influencing the nature of work;
- categories include interpersonal relationships, physical work conditions and structural job characteristics including pace/scheduling.
Project use:
- strong counterexample to a universal type taxonomy because the occupational model intentionally mixes social, physical and job-structural descriptors for a different purpose.

### E05 — SEIPS original work-system model
Source: Carayon et al., `Work system design for patient safety: the SEIPS model`, Quality & Safety in Health Care, 2006; PMC2464868.
URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2464868/
Direct evidence:
- person, tasks, tools/technologies, physical environment and organizational conditions interact to shape work/process/outcomes.
Project use:
- supports multifactor interaction and fit rather than isolated condition buckets;
- provides healthcare holdout evidence.

### E06 — SEIPS 2.0 / SEIPS 101
Sources:
- SEIPS 2.0: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3835697/
- SEIPS 101: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8543199/
Direct evidence:
- internal/external environment, tools, organization, tasks and people interact;
- external environment can include regulatory, legal, economic, political, cultural and societal context.
Project use:
- challenges technical/physical reduction;
- supports institutional/external conditions while requiring Actor/Information/Work boundary separation.

### E07 — Job Demands–Resources model
Source: Demerouti, Bakker, Nachreiner & Schaufeli, `The job demands-resources model of burnout`, Journal of Applied Psychology, 2001; PubMed 11419809.
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11419809/
Direct evidence:
- working conditions can be analyzed as job demands and job resources for burnout/disengagement outcomes;
- similar patterns occurred across human services, industry and transport.
Project use:
- serious rival for Enablers versus Constraints;
- rejected as a universal project child split because it is outcome-specific, employee/job-focused, and later literature distinguishes challenge/hindrance effects among demands.

Additional rival evidence:
- Crawford, LePine & Rich 2010 meta-analytic extension, PubMed 20836586, shows demand effects depend on whether demands are appraised as hindrances or challenges.
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20836586/

### E08 — OSHA hazard traditions
Sources:
- Hazard Identification and Assessment: https://www.osha.gov/safety-management/hazard-identification
- Industrial Hygiene: https://www.osha.gov/training/library/industrial-hygiene/industrial-hygiene
Direct evidence:
- workplace hazards include physical, chemical, biological, ergonomic, work-organization/process-flow and other categories;
- conditions can be controlled through engineering, administrative/work-practice and protective measures.
Project use:
- confirms hazards are first-order Work-relevant conditions;
- category families are hazard taxonomies, not general Work Conditions children.

### E09 — NASA support / operational environment
Sources:
- NASA Systems Engineering Handbook appendix: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/
- Concept of Operations annotated outline: https://www.nasa.gov/reference/appendix-s-concept-of-operations-annotated-outline/
Direct evidence:
- NASA distinguishes physical environment from support environment in system descriptions;
- support can include operational planning, command/upload support, maintenance, repair, replacement, spares and design-team support;
- systems include hardware, software, equipment, facilities, personnel, processes and procedures needed to produce capability.
Project use:
- strong systems/non-employment counterframe;
- shows one discipline can distinguish support infrastructure from ambient physical conditions, but this split does not recur invariantly across HFE/O*NET/JD-R/OSHA.

### E10 — Affordance / actionability relation
Source: Withagen et al., `Affordances can invite behavior`, New Ideas in Psychology, 2012.
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0732118X11000754
Direct evidence:
- ecological approaches treat affordances as action possibilities and emphasize animal/environment relationship.
Supporting open-access lineage:
- Frontiers, `The History and Philosophy of Ecological Psychology`: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.02228/full
Project use:
- supports keeping full actionability/feasibility relational rather than assigning it entirely to Work Conditions.

### E11 — Eurofound working-conditions / job-quality taxonomy
Source: Eurofound, `Working conditions in a global perspective`, 2019.
URL: https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/en/publications/all/working-conditions-global-perspective
Direct evidence:
- job quality is analyzed across physical environment, work intensity, working-time quality, social environment, skills/development, prospects and earnings.
Project use:
- another materially different taxonomy optimized for job quality rather than general execution feasibility;
- weakens claims that physical/social/technical/institutional categories form one universal Work Conditions hierarchy.

## Cross-source synthesis limits

Across these lineages there is strong convergence that Work is shaped by multiple interacting realized conditions, including technical, physical, organizational/social, resource/capacity and broader institutional/external factors.

There is **not** comparable convergence on one universal child taxonomy. The source families slice the problem differently because they protect different outcomes: worker health, job quality, patient safety, occupational description, system support or technical capability.
