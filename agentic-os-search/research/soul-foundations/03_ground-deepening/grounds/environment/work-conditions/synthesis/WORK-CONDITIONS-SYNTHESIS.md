## 2. What Work Conditions means

For this project:

> **Work Conditions are the realized circumstances that are materially load-bearing for whether and how focal Work can be performed at the selected scope and time.**

`How` is broader than binary possibility. Conditions can materially affect:
- start / continuation / blockage;
- usable or permissible method;
- safety and exposure;
- pace, waiting and throughput;
- reliability and recovery;
- resource sufficiency;
- achievable quality or precision.

This is broader than ordinary employment-language `working conditions` and applies to human, automated, technical, physical, scientific, institutional and mixed Work.

## 3. Why the node survives reduction

Work Conditions is not a second kind of reality.

The same concrete fact can be:
- Domain Reality for Work that studies/changes it;
- Work Condition for Work whose execution depends on it;
- both at once.

A later data/ontology representation may therefore model Work Conditions through typed relations over realized facts rather than duplicate them into a separate object class.

But the research node still survives because it protects a distinct question:

> Which realized facts are load-bearing for this Work's feasibility, method, safety, pace, reliability or sufficiency?

Without that question, support dependencies, access, capacity, readiness, hazards and execution windows can disappear from analysis even when Domain Reality is described correctly.

Therefore relational representability does not eliminate the first-order research problem.

## 4. Internal problem regions — explicit, not children

### Required support surface
Capabilities, services, equipment, materials, facilities, workspace, energy, communications, logistical support or other resources the Work materially depends on.

Important distinctions:
```text
exists ≠ relevant ≠ depended upon ≠ available ≠ sufficient
```

### Access and usable coupling
The actual path through which support can be used:
- physical access;
- connectivity/API/interface;
- logistical route;
- procedural access;
- effective permitted operation.

Full authority semantics are not owned here.

### Availability, readiness and sufficiency
A support element may exist but be:
- not initialized;
- inaccessible;
- degraded;
- unavailable;
- under-capacity;
- incompatible;
- unsafe for the protected use.

These are purpose-relative condition distinctions, not proposed universal status fields.

### Constraints, hazards and interference
Examples:
- heat/noise/weather/radiation/contamination;
- contention/congestion;
- unsafe facility/equipment state;
- communication disruption;
- isolation/confidentiality restrictions;
- realized regulatory/procedural prohibition;
- insufficient support coverage.

The same underlying variable may enable at one level and constrain at another.

### Dependencies and failure propagation
Support may depend on other services/resources/systems. Readiness can therefore depend on a wider support closure rather than one local component.

Important cases:
- hidden dependency;
- shared resource contention;
- common-mode support failure;
- partial degradation;
- one capability remains usable while another fails.

### Temporal validity and continuity
Conditions may be valid only during a time window:
- site access;
- weather/tide/traffic;
- filing/service window;
- staff/equipment availability;
- temporary emergency resources or permissions.

Work planning/scheduling remains distinct from the realized external window.

### Situational fit / actionability boundary
Full actionability depends jointly on:
- Work Conditions;
- Actor capability;
- Actor authority/permission;
- Work requirements and method;
- usable interaction surface.

Therefore:
```text
Work Condition ≠ full actionability
Actor capability ≠ Work Condition
```

### Boundary and role switching
The same concrete thing can change role as focal Work changes. Role does not determine identity.

## 5. Why no immediate children were promoted

### Enablers versus Constraints
Rejected as universal children.

Reason:
- the same condition can help or hinder depending level, protected outcome and perspective;
- external JD-R and SEIPS barrier/facilitator traditions show valence is contextual rather than one intrinsic object type.

### Resources / Access / Hazards
Kept as useful problem dimensions, not children.

Reason:
- access cuts across many resources/conditions;
- hazards can arise from resources, physical setting, organization, policies or interactions;
- capacity is a condition of a resource/service;
- dependencies cut across all of them.

### Technical / Physical / Social / Institutional / Logistical
Rejected as universal hierarchy.

Reason:
- materially different external traditions use different category sets;
- hybrid cases cross categories;
- categories reflect specialization and evaluation purpose more than stable universal research branches.

### Prerequisites versus Ongoing Conditions
Rejected as child split.

Reason:
- the same dependency may be needed before and during Work;
- conditions can degrade after Work begins;
- lifecycle boundary varies by Work.

### Support Systems versus Ambient Conditions
Useful systems-engineering lens, not universal hierarchy.

Reason:
- support and ambient conditions can overlap and switch with system/reference boundary;
- other external traditions partition the problem differently.

## 6. Technical Work Environment placement

The prior Technical Work Environment research remains valid and valuable.

Its strongest generalizable contributions include:
- capabilities/services/resources differ from component lists;
- interfaces matter;
- access/authorization effect differs from mere reachability;
- availability/readiness/capacity/sufficiency differ;
- remote dependencies and shared contention can be load-bearing;
- actual state differs from declared/observed state.

But TWE is not required for all Work Conditions cases. Nontechnical manual work, legal/institutional work, field work and emergency response can have important Work Conditions without a meaningful technical work environment.

Therefore:

> **Technical Work Environment is retained as a deep technical specialization/profile of Work Conditions, not a demonstrated universal immediate child.**

This changes placement interpretation, not the prior TWE findings.

## 7. Cross-ground boundaries

### Versus Domain Reality
Domain Reality asks:
> what realized state/dynamics is the Work about or judging outcomes against?

Work Conditions asks:
> which realized circumstances materially shape whether/how Work can be performed?

Same fact can carry both roles.

### Versus Work
Work owns:
- what Work exists / is required / selected / committed;
- internal lifecycle/readiness/blocked/suspended state;
- plan, coordination and ordering;
- deadlines/schedules as Work control semantics;
- effort, duration, waiting and closure semantics.

Work Conditions owns the realized circumstances that may make Work ready, blocked, unsafe, slow or insufficient.

Example:
```text
Work is blocked                    → Work state
required service is unavailable    → Work Condition
service outage blocks Work         → later cross-ground relation
```

### Versus Actors
Actors owns:
- identity;
- capability/competence;
- authority/permission/obligation;
- participation/responsibility/accountability.

Actual support availability/coverage can be a Work Condition without absorbing the support person's Actor semantics.

### Versus Information
Information owns:
- policy/rule/specification text;
- observations/reports/models;
- evidence/confidence/freshness;
- claims about conditions.

Work Conditions concerns the realized condition.

Example:
```text
monitor says service is healthy → Information/evidence
service is actually available   → Work Condition
```

## 8. Load-bearing relevance rule

Work Conditions must not become `everything around Work`.

A candidate condition belongs in the current Work-Conditions view when a plausible change in it could materially change one or more protected Work properties:
- feasibility/start/continuation;
- available/permitted method;
- safety/exposure;
- pace/waiting/throughput;
- reliability/failure/recovery;
- resource sufficiency;
- achievable quality/precision.

This rule is purpose-relative. It selects which real facts matter to the current Work analysis; it does not decide whether other facts exist.
