## A1 — Node-elimination attack

Attack:
> Work Conditions is merely Domain Reality facts plus a relation to Work; therefore the research node is redundant.

What survives the attack:
- ontologically/representationally, duplication is unnecessary; the same realized fact may carry a Work-Condition role;
- analytically, generic Domain Reality research does not force investigation of support closure, access, readiness, capacity, hazards, shared dependencies, execution windows or support failure propagation;
- these omissions can materially change feasibility, method, safety, pace, reliability or achievable quality of Work.

Verdict:
> reduction may be viable for later representation, but it does not eliminate the research problem.

## A3 — Strongest rival: Support Surface + Exposure/Constraint Context

Possible split:

```text
Work Conditions
    ├─ Required Support Surface
    └─ Exposure / Constraint Context
```

Why it could be useful:
- one asks what Work needs available;
- the other asks what surrounding conditions restrict or endanger it.

Counterexamples:
- a facility is both required support and source of hazard;
- a network is support when available and a constraint when congested/unavailable;
- staff coverage can be support, scarcity, coordination constraint and hazard control simultaneously;
- a regulation may enable one method and prohibit another;
- environmental temperature can be required process condition and worker hazard at the same time.

External SEIPS barrier/facilitator studies also show the same work-system element or interaction may help or hinder depending role, configuration and perspective.

Child-promotion test:
- distinct question: partly yes;
- distinct failure surfaces: partly yes;
- stable boundary across domains: no;
- reduction behavior: heavily overlapping;
- independent recursive research value: not demonstrated.

Verdict: keep as directional/functional views, not children.

## A4 — Enablers versus Constraints attack

JD-R and barrier/facilitator traditions provide a strong external rival.

Why it fails as universal hierarchy:
- valence depends on current level, protected outcome, Actor/Work relation and perspective;
- later JD-R evidence separates challenge and hindrance effects rather than making all demands uniformly negative;
- SEIPS studies show the same feature can be a facilitator for one role and a barrier for another.

Verdict: direction/effect relation, not intrinsic child type.

## A5 — Resources / Access / Hazards split attack

Why attractive:
- practical and recurrent;
- maps to common failure classes.

Why insufficient:
- access applies to resources, sites, people, procedures and services;
- hazard can arise from a resource, physical setting, organization, policy or interaction;
- capacity is state/sufficiency of a resource/service;
- dependencies and interference cut across all three.

Verdict: explicit dimensions/problem regions, not stable children.

## A7 — Technical Work Environment promotion attack

Could existing TWE be a universal immediate child?

Counterexamples:
- manual field work with materials, weather, workspace and access but no meaningful technical environment;
- legal filing with office/portal/window/jurisdictional conditions;
- emergency response with terrain, hazards, temporary resources/control arrangements;
- physical maintenance with site and safety constraints.

Verdict:
> TWE remains a valuable deep technical specialization/profile; universal child status is not supported.

Its prior research is retained, not invalidated.

## A8 — Readiness / availability / sufficiency attack

TWE strongly establishes that exists/running/reachable/ready/available/sufficient can diverge.

But these characterize current support condition relative to a protected use and can apply to many resource/interface types.

Verdict: high-value anti-collapse dimensions, not child.

## A9 — Temporal split attack

Candidate:
- prerequisites/readiness before Work;
- ongoing operating conditions during Work.

Counterexamples:
- dependency required before start remains required during execution;
- Work can become blocked after start when support degrades;
- external windows can open/close during execution;
- setup Work can create future conditions.

Verdict: lifecycle/temporal view, not child.

## A10 — Work leakage attack

Boundary test:
- `Work is blocked` = Work state/judgment;
- `backend unavailable` = Work Condition;
- `backend outage blocks Work` = cross-ground relation.

Likewise:
- Work deadline/schedule = Work control structure;
- actual external opening/service/weather window = Work Condition.

Verdict: boundary survives with relational composition deferred.

## A11 — Actor leakage attack

Counterexample:
- specialist is unavailable at required time.

Keep separate:
- specialist identity/capability/authority/obligation = Actors;
- current support coverage/presence = Work Condition in support role;
- Work assignment/coordination = Work.

Verdict: boundary survives.

## A12 — Information / institutional leakage attack

Keep separate:
- policy/rule/permit text or report = Information;
- institutional authority = Actors/control;
- realized current applicability/access/prohibition/service window = Work Condition when load-bearing.

Verdict: boundary survives with socially/institutionally constituted reality qualification.

## A13 — Domain Reality role-switching attack

Same system can be:
- CI platform as support → Work Condition;
- CI platform under repair → Domain Reality;
- both in live repair.

Same for hospital equipment, roads/weather, facilities.

Verdict: relational/non-exclusive roles survive; do not duplicate concrete identity.

## A14 — Ambient-context expansion attack

Risk:
> if every surrounding fact can influence Work, Work Conditions becomes `everything relevant`.

Counter-rule:
A condition enters the Work-Conditions view only if a plausible change could materially change a protected Work property: feasibility/start/continuation, method/permissibility, safety, pace/waiting, reliability/recovery, resource sufficiency or achievable quality/precision.

Verdict: load-bearing coupling protects the boundary, though operational materiality thresholds remain a residual question.

## A15 — Human-employment bias attack

Automated/nonhuman and nontechnical holdouts preserve the node without employee/job assumptions.

Verdict:
> project `Work Conditions` is broader than ordinary employment `working conditions` literature.

This is a terminology guardrail, not a reason to rename the node at present.
