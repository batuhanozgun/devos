# Work Conditions Finding 04 — External Cross-Lineage Falsification

**Status:** current substantive finding
**Date:** 2026-09-03

## Purpose

External sources were used to challenge the project split, not to select a taxonomy by authority.

## Lineage 1 — Human factors / ergonomics

ILO HFE and SEIPS work-system traditions repeatedly model Work as emerging from interactions among people, tasks, tools/technology, organization and physical/external environment.

What this supports:
- Work is shaped by multiple interacting conditions;
- technical, physical, organizational/social and broader external factors can all be load-bearing;
- isolated component lists are insufficient.

What it does not support:
- copying `person/task/tool/organization/environment` as children of Work Conditions, because several are owned by project Work/Actors rather than Environment;
- any single universal child taxonomy.

Falsifier effect:
- technical-only and physical-only reductions fail;
- interaction/coupling becomes more important than type taxonomy.

## Lineage 2 — Occupational work context

O*NET defines Work Context as physical and social factors influencing the nature of work, then groups interpersonal relationships, physical work conditions and structural job characteristics such as pace/scheduling.

Eurofound job-quality research uses another set: physical environment, work intensity, working time, social environment, skills/development, prospects and earnings.

What this supports:
- broad context matters;
- time/social/physical factors can be material.

What this falsifies:
- the assumption that external expert traditions converge on one stable decomposition.

Why:
- the taxonomies intentionally mix Work design, social relations, physical environment and employment conditions because they serve occupational/job-quality purposes.

Project consequence:
> type/category recurrence alone is insufficient for child promotion.

## Lineage 3 — Job Demands–Resources

Demerouti et al. explicitly analyze working conditions as job demands and job resources and show different associations with exhaustion/disengagement across several occupational groups.

This is the strongest external rival to the project's neutral `enabling–constraining` language.

Why the rival does not win as universal project hierarchy:
- JD-R protects worker health/motivation outcomes, not general Work feasibility across human/nonhuman/project/system cases;
- demands/resources can both be physical/social/organizational rather than independent types;
- later challenge/hindrance evidence shows demand valence is not uniformly negative;
- one variable can move from resource to constraint as its current state changes.

Project consequence:
> enabling versus constraining remains a useful directional relation, not a stable child split.

## Lineage 4 — Occupational safety / hazards

OSHA distinguishes chemical, biological, physical, ergonomic, work-organization/process-flow and other hazard families.

What this supports:
- hazards and exposures are genuine first-order Work-relevant conditions;
- physical setting is not the only condition source.

Why it does not define Work Conditions children:
- it is a hazard-recognition taxonomy, not a general feasibility/support taxonomy;
- a hazard can arise from tools, facilities, work organization, materials or people;
- enabling conditions are outside its main classification purpose.

Project consequence:
> hazard is a high-value condition role/property, not a universal sibling of resource/access/etc.

## Lineage 5 — Systems engineering / operational support

NASA systems-engineering material distinguishes physical environment and support environment and describes support through planning, maintenance, repair, replacement, spares, facilities, personnel, procedures and systems.

What this supports:
- Work/system execution can depend on a broad support infrastructure;
- support is not reducible to software tools;
- support capability can include people, facilities, procedures and technical systems.

Why its physical-versus-support split does not become the project hierarchy:
- the distinction is useful in a system description/ConOps context but is not invariant across HFE, O*NET, JD-R or TWE;
- support systems can themselves create ambient physical/technical/social conditions;
- boundary/reference choices can switch a support system into the focal Domain Reality.

Project consequence:
> `support infrastructure versus ambient conditions` is a useful lens, not a universal child split.

## Lineage 6 — Technical Work Environment prior corpus

The TWE corpus independently found:
- capability/service/resource differs from component inventory;
- exists/reachable/bound/permitted/available/sufficient differ;
- readiness can depend on a dependency closure;
- partial degradation/contention can matter without tool crash;
- interfaces and constraints change effective support surface.

Generalizable contribution:
- support availability/sufficiency/access/dependency distinctions are real.

Non-generalizable conclusion to avoid:
- technical mechanisms define the universal Work Conditions node.

Project consequence:
> TWE remains a deep technical specialization/profile; its strongest anti-collapse distinctions lift upward as dimensions.

## Lineage 7 — Affordance / ecological actionability

Affordance traditions characterize action possibilities through organism/agent–environment relations.

Project use:
- environment-side conditions contribute to what is possible;
- full actionability cannot be owned by Work Conditions alone because Actor ability and task/purpose matter.

Project consequence:
> Work Conditions carries environment-side relata; `can this Actor do this Work now?` remains a cross-ground relation/judgment.

## Cross-lineage verdict

Strong convergence:
- realized conditions materially shape Work;
- several condition families can interact;
- positive support and negative constraints both matter;
- current availability/access/capacity can matter more than nominal existence;
- physical, technical, organizational/social and institutional/external conditions can all participate.

Weak/nonexistent convergence:
- one universal child taxonomy;
- one stable technical/physical/social/institutional split;
- enablers and constraints as exclusive object families;
- support systems versus ambient context as a universal binary.

## Structural implication

External evidence strengthens the node while weakening all tested immediate-child decompositions.

Current strongest candidate remains:

```text
Work Conditions     ← one universal research node
```

with explicit internal problem regions and optional domain specializations, pending cross-domain holdouts and final omission audit.
