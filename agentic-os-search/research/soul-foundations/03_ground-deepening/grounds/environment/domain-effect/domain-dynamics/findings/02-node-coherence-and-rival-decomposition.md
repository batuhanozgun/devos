# Domain Dynamics Finding 02 — Node Coherence and Rival Decomposition

**Status:** active-pass finding  
**Stage:** D1 / D3 result

## Node survival

A dedicated Dynamics problem remains justified beneath Domain Reality.

State asks what realized Structure/Composition and Condition/Status obtain at selected scope/time. Dynamics asks what actually unfolds across time: occurrence, change, persistence, interaction, sequence, rate, delay, feedback, disturbance, trajectory and regime behavior.

Current State alone does not universally determine future unfolding. Markov traditions make history-independence conditional on a sufficiently defined current state; hysteretic/path-dependent systems provide counterexamples to silently assuming that sufficiency. Dynamics therefore cannot be reduced to 'two State snapshots'.

## Label result

The strongest current label is:

> **Domain Dynamics**

rather than `Domain Dynamics / Processes`.

Reason: `process` is an important occurrence mode but not a neutral synonym for all dynamics. Events/boundaries, transitions, trajectories, persistence, regime behavior and hybrid flow/jump behavior can be load-bearing. Keeping `/ Processes` in the node label would privilege one ontology/model vocabulary before child testing.

## Rivals tested

### A — one `Domain Dynamics` node with no immediate children

Strengths:
- covers continuous, discrete, stochastic, hybrid, social and institutional change without forcing one formalism;
- allows occurrence modes and temporal properties to remain explicit without pretending they are independent subtrees;
- minimizes hierarchy while preserving a dedicated Dynamics question.

Current verdict: **strongest**.

### B — `Processes / Flows / Interactions` + `Events / Transitions`

Attractive because ongoing and localized/discontinuous occurrences expose different practical failures.

Weakness:
- hybrid-systems traditions deliberately unify flows and jumps under one dynamics;
- process/event ontologies disagree about whether events are processes, completed occurrences, boundaries or alternative aspectual descriptions;
- the same real phenomenon can be modeled as continuous at one scale and event-like at another;
- abruptness and duration can depend on reference scale.

Verdict: retain as explicit **occurrence modes**, not universal immediate children.

### C — `Interactions / Mechanisms` + `Trajectories / Evolution`

Weakness:
- trajectory is the realized temporal path/pattern of dynamics, not a peer source of dynamics;
- 'mechanism' language easily leaks into causal explanation, while the Dynamics node must remain valid without proving causal attribution.

Verdict: do not promote.

### D — `Endogenous` + `Exogenous / Disturbance`

Weakness:
- endogenous/exogenous status changes with system boundary and focal reference;
- the same concrete process can be internal dynamics at one boundary and disturbance/Work Condition at another.

Verdict: source/boundary dimension, not child.

### E — `Continuous` + `Discrete`

Weakness:
- hybrid dynamical systems explicitly combine both;
- continuous/discrete classification is often a modeling resolution/formalism choice rather than a reality partition.

Verdict: modeling/occurrence-mode dimension, not child.

### F — `Dynamics` + `History / Path Dependence`

Weakness:
- history dependence is a property of how current/future evolution depends on prior path;
- some apparent history dependence disappears when State is made sufficient, while genuine hysteresis remains possible;
- this distinction therefore cuts across processes, transitions and trajectories rather than forming a stable peer branch.

Verdict: cross-cutting Dynamics property / reopen trigger, not child.

### G — `Dynamics` + `Stability / Regimes`

Weakness:
- equilibrium, limit cycle, attractor, stability, regime and regime shift characterize patterns/properties of trajectories or sets under dynamics;
- they do not form a separate first-order family parallel to the dynamics that exhibit them.

Verdict: cross-cutting pattern/property dimension.

### H — State-transition reduction

Weakness:
- a transition relation or equation usually describes possible/modelled evolution, not the realized unfolding itself;
- ongoing processes, timing, rates, feedback, delays and partial observability can be lost in a bare transition relation;
- model/simulation must remain distinct from actual Dynamics.

Verdict: useful representation, insufficient reduction of the research problem.

## Immediate-child result

No candidate split currently clears the child-promotion threshold across ontology/model lineages and holdout domains.

**Current strongest bounded result: `Domain Dynamics` is a leaf at this foundation-research level.**

Leaf status is reopenable if a later domain exposes a recurrent first-order Dynamics problem that cannot be handled without forced fit as an occurrence mode, temporal property, boundary relation or representation choice.
