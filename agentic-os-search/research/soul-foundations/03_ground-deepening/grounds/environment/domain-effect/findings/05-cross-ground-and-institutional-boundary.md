# Domain Reality Finding 05 — Cross-Ground and Institutional Boundary

**Status:** current active-node finding  
**Question:** how can Domain Reality remain broad enough for social/digital/institutional cases without swallowing Information, Work or Actors?

## Central rule

> **Work target ≠ automatically Domain Reality.**

Domain Reality applies when a **realized environment/domain role** is materially at issue. A Work item can target something whose first-order semantics remain in another ground.

## Information target

Examples:
- editing a specification;
- revising a Record;
- restructuring documentation;
- changing source text as a persistent representation.

The fact that Work targets the artifact does not make its meaning/provenance/version semantics Environment-owned.

The artifact or platform may also have an environmental/domain role in a different question, but that does not transfer Information semantics into Domain Reality.

## Actor target

Examples:
- training a person;
- changing organizational authority;
- evaluating competence;
- assigning responsibility.

Actor identity/capability/authority remains Actors semantics. Domain Reality may capture realized external consequences or conditions created by Actors, not the full Actor target merely because Work concerns it.

## Work target

Examples:
- redesigning a workflow;
- correcting a process/commitment structure;
- changing coordination rules.

Work lifecycle/commitment/execution semantics remain Work. Produced consequences can become Domain Reality conditions for another analysis.

## Digital domain case

Digital systems do not invalidate Domain Reality.

For software/product/operations Work, distinguish when material:
- source/configuration/specification as Information/representation;
- actual running/deployed system configuration and externally consequential behavior as Domain Reality;
- development/CI/runtime resources as Work Conditions when they support the focal Work.

The same concrete repository/service/system can participate in several roles.

## Social/institutional reality

Some social or institutional facts can depend constitutively on collective attitudes, rules, status assignments or representational practices.

Therefore reject the overly strong rule:

> `Domain Reality = reality that exists independently of all representation`

Stronger project rule:

> **actual domain condition is not identical to a particular observation, model, assertion or Record of that condition, even where the kind of condition is socially or institutionally constituted.**

Examples:
- a permit is legally in force;
- a market is open;
- an office has a recognized institutional status;
- a jurisdiction currently applies.

The authoritative texts/claims/evidence and authority-bearing Actors remain separate semantics.

## Optionality result

Not every Work episode requires a material Domain Reality target.

A purely representational or administrative Work item may primarily involve Information/Work/Actors, while Environment contributes only execution conditions.

This does not weaken Domain Reality as an Environment child; it prevents it from becoming a mandatory `target of Work` bucket.

## Failure surfaces

- Environment steals Information semantics because an artifact is the Work target;
- Environment steals Actor semantics because a person/organization is being changed;
- Environment steals Work semantics because a process/workflow is being redesigned;
- digital representation is mistaken for actual system behavior;
- socially constituted reality is falsely excluded as `not real`;
- a particular Record is treated as identical to institutional reality.

## Result

Domain Reality is a **role-relative realized-reality problem region**, not a universal superclass of Work targets.
