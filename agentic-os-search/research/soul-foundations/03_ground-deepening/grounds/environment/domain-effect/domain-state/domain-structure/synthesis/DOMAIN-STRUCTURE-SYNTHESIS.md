```text
Domain Structure
```

The branches are analytical problem regions, not object classes, graph primitives, schema sections or implementation modules.

The core distinction is:

> **Which constituents actually participate in / make up the selected whole?**
>
> versus
>
> **How are those relevant constituents actually structurally organized?**

At the broader Domain-State level, bundling constituents and relations into `Structure / Composition` was economically useful to separate Structure from Condition. At the deeper Structure-specific resolution, that bundle no longer survives as a leaf: the two questions repeatedly fail independently and trigger different reconstruction/recovery work.

---

# 1. Why the node becomes `Domain Structure`

`Composition` is not wrong, but it is narrower than the full node.

A domain may have:
- the correct constituent/member set;
- the correct part-whole nesting;
- but the wrong connection, routing, dependency, arrangement or interface relation.

That is clearly a structural failure without a composition/membership failure.

Likewise, an expected constituent can be missing while all known relations among remaining constituents are correct.

Therefore:

> `Structure` is the broad node; `Composition / Membership` is one of its immediate regions.

`Organization`, `Topology`, `Membership`, `Composition` and `Configuration` were tested as parent replacements. None covers the whole problem as cleanly without importing narrower or ambiguous disciplinary meanings.

---

# 2. Immediate Child A — Constituent Composition / Membership

Primary question:

> **Which materially relevant constituents actually participate in or make up the selected whole/scope, including materially relevant part–whole and nested membership?**

Includes when material:
- constituent presence/absence;
- structural participation/membership;
- part–whole composition;
- subsystem/subassembly/component nesting;
- aggregation/decomposition;
- membership in a selected network/group/domain whole even where classical physical `part-of` is not the right metaphysical relation.

## Failure surfaces

- missing/extra/wrong constituent;
- wrong participant set;
- wrong part-whole decomposition;
- wrong nesting/aggregation;
- correct topology over an incomplete or incorrect constituent set;
- inventory/BOM/API/model mistaken for realized membership.

## Why this is not just a graph-node child

Promotion is based on a real reasoning failure: the wrong things may be treated as participating in the selected structure. The structure can be represented as a graph, table, diagram, inventory, physical inspection or not stored at all.

SEP Mereology is useful only as a counterframe showing that parthood/decomposition/composition form a substantive problem family. Manufacturing assembly/BOM, Kubernetes runtime membership and supply-network participation independently show that the real-world constituent-set question recurs outside philosophical mereology.

---

# 3. Immediate Child B — Relational Organization / Topology

Primary question:

> **How are the relevant constituents actually structurally related, connected, arranged, contained or interfaced at the selected scope/time?**

Includes when material:
- realized connections and dependencies;
- network topology;
- adjacency/routing structure;
- realized internal/external interfaces;
- placement/arrangement/geometry;
- spatial containment/contact/overlap when the protected question is arrangement;
- organizational/market/supply relations;
- standing structural couplings when they describe extant linkage rather than interaction dynamics.

## Failure surfaces

- correct constituent set, wrong connections/dependencies;
- wrong topology/routing;
- wrong interface/interconnection;
- wrong structural placement/containment;
- correct parts assembled/connected incorrectly;
- architecture/topology model mistaken for realized organization.

## Why this is not just a graph-edge child

Promotion is based on independently recurring reconstruction work: knowing the right constituents does not establish how they are actually organized.

SEBoK and NIST systems sources place relations/connections inside the broad concept of structure. NIST SP 800-160v1r1 is especially discriminating: it says a general hierarchical representation is not suitable for all systems and that non-hierarchical systems such as networks need models that reflect relations among constituent elements. Supply-chain, market, ecological and network traditions independently stress relational organization.

`Topology` is retained only as an explanatory companion label. In some disciplines topology itself includes nodes plus links, so the project boundary is defined by the primary semantic phrase `Relational Organization`, not by importing one formal definition of topology.

# 4. Why the two children qualify now

The split passes the project child-promotion test at the deeper Structure resolution.

## Distinct first-order questions

- Composition / Membership → **what actually belongs/participates, and what is part of what?**
- Relational Organization / Topology → **how are those relevant constituents structurally organized?**

## Independently variable failures

Examples:
- correct services, wrong dependency topology;
- wrong service/member set, correct known dependencies among remaining members;
- correct machine parts, wrong assembly connection;
- wrong subassembly installed, otherwise correct interface pattern;
- correct suppliers/venues, wrong routing/interconnection;
- correct species set, changed interaction-network structure.

## Different recovery behavior

Composition failure asks:
- what should count as actual participant/part at this scope?
- which expected constituent is absent/extra/wrong?
- what part-whole/nesting relation actually obtains?

Relational failure asks:
- which realized connection/dependency/adjacency/interface obtains?
- how is the constituent set arranged/connected?
- where does the assumed topology differ from reality?

That difference is strong enough to justify one additional level of hierarchy.

# 5. Why this does not contradict the Domain State pass

The parent pass asked whether `Domain State` should distinguish structural facts from current condition/status facts. At that resolution, `constituents + organizing relations` jointly formed one structural region and a three-way `Constituents + Relations + Properties` split was correctly rejected as too primitive/model-like.

This pass asks a different question one level deeper:

> once `Structure` is selected as the active node, does it itself contain stable first-order problem regions?

New result:
- yes, at this deeper resolution;
- promotion is not because graphs have nodes and edges;
- it is because constituent-set/part-whole failures and organization/topology failures recur independently and require different recovery questions.

Therefore the parent result is **refined, not overturned**.

# 6. Third-child candidates tested and rejected

## Spatial Arrangement / Containment

Important but not universal. Spatial/topological relation families fit `Relational Organization` when structurally material. `Part-of` or nested membership fits `Composition` when constitution of the whole is at issue.

OGC GeoSPARQL's rich RCC8/topological relation families show that spatial organization deserves explicit treatment without establishing a universal spatial child.

## Hierarchy / Nesting versus Network / Topology

These are structural forms, not co-equal universal children. NIST explicitly notes that not all systems are hierarchical; nested elements may themselves be systems while networks require non-hierarchical relation modeling.

## Interfaces / Boundaries / Couplings

Realized interface/interconnection → Relational Organization.

But:
- interaction/exchange through interface → Dynamics;
- health/capacity/availability → Condition;
- interface specification/architecture → Information/representation;
- selected analytical boundary → cross-cutting scope/reference.

A universal interface child would collapse roles.

## Structural Integrity / Unity

No independent region survives after composition, relational organization and cross-cutting identity/boundary criteria are explicit.

---

# 7. Cross-cutting dimensions retained

## Identity / Continuity

Snapshot membership needs identity. But persistence through replacement/change also governs Condition bearers and Dynamics. Current SEP Identity Over Time material independently confirms that persistence through change is a distinct through-time problem.

Retain as cross-cutting prerequisite/reopen trigger.

## Scope / Boundary / Reference

The selected whole determines which participants and external relations matter. This indexes both children and is not itself a third child.

## Granularity / Scale / Level

Nested and multi-scale systems recurse through both children. Scale selects analytical resolution; it does not define an independent universal structural region.

## Constraints / Invariants

Constraints can restrict membership, relations, conditions and transitions. The rule/invariant is not generally identical to the currently realized structure that satisfies or violates it.

## Actual versus represented

Applies to both children and remains a hard boundary:

```text
actual constituent membership
≠ inventory/BOM/Service selector/Record by identity

actual relational organization
≠ architecture/topology diagram/cache/model by identity
```

## Time index / currentness

A structural claim is anchored to scope/time. The most recently recorded structure is not automatically the current realized structure.

# 8. Structure / Condition boundary after deeper research

The parent boundary survives.

Use the semantic question:

### Structure
- is the constituent actually part of / participating in the selected structure?
- how is it structurally related/arranged?

### Condition
- given that bearer/whole, what current property, health, availability, mode, value or standing holds?

Therefore:

```text
component absent from selected realized structure
≠
component structurally present but unavailable/degraded
```

Some relational facts can be Condition if they represent current standing rather than structural organization. Relation type alone does not decide ownership.

---

# 9. Structure / Dynamics boundary

Keep:

```text
current structural relation
≠ interaction/process through that relation
≠ transition that creates/removes/rewires that relation
```

Examples:
- `A connected to B` → Structure;
- packet/material/resource exchange A→B over interval → Dynamics;
- connection created/removed → Dynamics event with before/after Structure snapshots.

This distinction is especially necessary in supply, market, ecological and software-network cases where `link`, `flow`, `interaction` and `topology` are frequently bundled in domain vocabulary.

---

# 10. Actual Structure versus architecture/inventory/model

One of the strongest boundary findings survives and gains new concrete support.

NASA configuration-management guidance requires audits that confirm actual work-product configuration matches documented configuration. Such audits are necessary precisely because the two can diverge.

Kubernetes likewise illustrates that Service/EndpointSlice control representations can track/abstract a changing runtime backend set.

Therefore:

```text
actual Domain Structure
≠ architecture/design structure
≠ configuration baseline
≠ BOM/inventory
≠ topology/network diagram
≠ ontology/graph representation
≠ API/control-plane object
≠ digital twin / simulated structure
```

Representations can be excellent and operationally essential without becoming the represented reality by identity.

---

# 11. Social / institutional boundary

Institutional structure is a genuine holdout, not a reason to create a type-specific child.

The current SEP Social Institutions entry treats roles as constitutive elements of institutions and discusses structured organizations/systems of organizations. Thus realized institutional organization can exercise both structural children.

But avoid swallowing Actors:
- Actor identity/capability/authority/responsibility remain Actors semantics when those are the protected questions;
- a role relation can simultaneously participate in Domain Structure when the organization itself is the focal realized domain.

Rules, charters, org charts and records remain Information representations/constitutive artifacts according to role and cannot be equated automatically with the realized institutional structure.
