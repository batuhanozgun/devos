# Relational Organization Finding 03 — Spatial, Interface, Hierarchy and Pattern Rivals

**Status:** bounded finding  
**Date:** 2026-09-04

## Question

Do spatial/geometric arrangement, interfaces/boundaries or hierarchy/layering form stable immediate children distinct from other relational organization?

## Result

**No. All three survive as materially important relation/organization forms, but none earns a universal immediate-child branch.**

## Spatial / geometric organization

Spatial organization can be structurally decisive:
- relative position and orientation in a mechanical assembly;
- within/contains/touches/overlaps/crosses relations in geographic systems;
- zone/region placement influencing network routing;
- physical adjacency/separation in infrastructure or experimental apparatus.

But the evidence does not support a universal split:

```text
Relational Organization
    ├─ Connectivity / Dependency
    └─ Spatial / Geometric Organization
```

Reasons:
- mechanical assembly practice treats relative placement, joint and constraint relationships together;
- networking traditions often include both placement and interconnection under physical topology;
- spatial-topology standards provide a rich relation family specific to spatial objects rather than a project-wide recovery branch;
- many software/institutional relation problems have no material geometry at all.

Therefore spatial/geometric organization remains a **relation family / domain profile** inside Relational Organization when structurally material.

Intrinsic geometry of a single constituent is not automatically Relational Organization; this node protects relative arrangement/organization. A realized geometric quantity may instead become a Condition characteristic depending on the protected question.

## Interfaces / boundaries / couplings

Interfaces are structurally important because they specify extant relation points/pathways between elements or systems.

NASA engineering material spans mechanical, fluid, thermal, electrical, data, logical and human interfaces. This plurality weakens rather than strengthens a universal interface child: interface is a family of typed relations crossing many implementation domains.

Boundary rule:

```text
extant interface / structural connection
→ Relational Organization

interaction, exchange or flow through it
→ Domain Dynamics when unfolding/process is the protected question

latency, capacity, strength, health or quality of the interface
→ can be Domain Condition when current value/standing is the protected question

interface requirement/specification/diagram
→ Information / representation unless actual realization is established separately
```

## Hierarchy / layering / order

NIST explicitly notes that some systems are hierarchical and others are not. Hierarchy therefore cannot be a universal sibling of network organization.

Hierarchy, ordering, layering and matrix-like organization are **forms/patterns of relations**.

Institutional hierarchy creates an additional cross-ground boundary: a reporting relation may be structurally relevant to Domain Structure, while authority, obligation, delegation or competence semantics may require Actors analysis. Relation presence alone does not settle semantic ownership.

## Derived structural patterns

Path structure, redundancy, bottlenecks, centralization, clustering and reachability can materially describe organization.

They do not form a separate universal child because:
- they are often derived from the underlying relation structure;
- the same measure has different meaning across domains;
- if a numeric value/standing is the protected question, it may instead be a realized Condition characteristic;
- the structural pattern itself remains recoverable within Relational Organization.

## Falsifier impact

- F4 connectivity/dependency versus spatial/geometric split → **rejected**.
- F6 interface child → **rejected**.
- F7 hierarchy/ordering child → **rejected**.
- F18 derived-pattern child → **rejected**.
