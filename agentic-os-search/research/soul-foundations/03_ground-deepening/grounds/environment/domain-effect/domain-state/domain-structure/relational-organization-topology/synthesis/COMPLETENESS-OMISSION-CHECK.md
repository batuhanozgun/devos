# Relational Organization — Completeness / Omission / Rival Audit

**Status:** bounded same-model adversarial audit  
**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Verification class:** NON-INDEPENDENT

## Audit rule

This file tests the frozen F1–F21 rivals from `../RELATIONAL-ORGANIZATION-TOPOLOGY-RESEARCH-PLAN.md`. It is a same-model adversarial review and must not be interpreted as independent validation.

## F1 — node incoherence

**Result: REJECTED.**

Across materially different domains the same recoverable question recurs: how the selected constituents are structurally organized. Relation semantics vary but do not destroy node coherence.

## F2 — `Topology` label overbreadth

**Result: SUPPORTED.**

Networking, GIS and mathematical/network-science traditions use `topology` at materially different semantic scopes. Some networking usage includes nodes/placement and even logical flow. Drop the companion label.

Bounded label: **Relational Organization**.

## F3 — `Relational Organization` label narrowness

**Result: REJECTED.**

Relative placement, orientation and containment can be treated as structural relations to other constituents or a reference frame when arrangement is the protected question. Intrinsic constituent geometry is not automatically absorbed.

## F4 — connectivity/dependency versus spatial/geometric split

**Result: REJECTED.**

Spatial organization is rich but domain-selective. Mechanical assembly, networking and systems traditions commonly integrate relative placement and other relationship forms. No universal two-child recovery split survives.

## F5 — connection versus dependency split

**Result: REJECTED.**

Connection and dependency are semantically distinct relation types, but both fit the same organization-reconstruction problem. AWS runtime dependency practice demonstrates typed dependency edges inside a common relationship graph.

## F6 — interface child

**Result: REJECTED.**

Interfaces span mechanical, fluid, thermal, electrical, data, logical and human forms. Extant interfaces are typed structural relations; interaction through them, condition of them and specifications of them belong to neighbouring analytical questions as appropriate.

## F7 — hierarchy/ordering child

**Result: REJECTED.**

Hierarchy is an organization form, not universal. NIST explicitly notes that many systems are non-hierarchical. Layering/order similarly behaves as structural pattern/semantics.

## F8 — merge into Constituent Composition

**Result: REJECTED.**

Correct constituents can coexist with wrong relation structure. Recovery reconstructs organization rather than membership. This independent failure surface survives all major holdouts.

## F9 — containment ambiguity

**Result: CONTAINED; NO PARENT FAILURE.**

`part of` and `spatially inside` are not universal equivalents. Constitution belongs to Constituent Composition when membership is protected; spatial containment belongs to Relational Organization when arrangement is protected.

## F10 — Condition leakage

**Result: CONTAINED.**

Relation existence/pattern and current relation characteristics are distinguishable:
- dependency relation → Structure;
- latency/strength/distance/health/metric value → may be Condition when the value/standing itself is protected.

No new organization child is required.

## F11 — Dynamics leakage

**Result: CONTAINED.**

Extant relation/interface is distinct from flow/interaction/event through it and from rewiring/change history. Current relation structure remains state-side; change/process belongs to Dynamics when protected.

## F12 — Actor leakage

**Result: CONTAINED WITH ROLE-SWITCHING RULE.**

Reporting/hierarchy may be structurally relevant, but authority, obligation, permission, delegation and competence semantics require Actor-side analysis. Edge appearance in an org chart is insufficient to collapse the distinction.

## F13 — representation collapse

**Result: REJECTED.**

Graph, map, topology diagram, routing/configuration table, dependency map, org chart, GIS assertion or digital twin is not actual organization by identity. It can represent/evidence it.

## F14 — normative relation leakage

**Result: REJECTED.**

Required, allowed, intended or potential relation is not actual realized relation. Ecology's potential-vs-realized link distinction provides a useful external challenge.

## F15 — scope/reference child

**Result: REJECTED.**

Internal/external and cross-level relation selection depends on the chosen boundary/reference frame and recurs across structure questions. It remains cross-cutting.

## F16 — temporal/identity child

**Result: REJECTED.**

Rewiring/change is Dynamics; relation persistence and whole identity through reorganization remain temporal/identity cross-cuts. Current organization does not require a separate temporal child.

## F17 — relation-form taxonomy trap

**Result: REJECTED AS CHILD-GENERATING BASIS.**

Directionality, arity, weighted/unweighted, signed/unsigned and typed-relation classifications materially affect semantics but are not by themselves distinct first-order project recovery regions.

Higher-order-network evidence strengthens the guardrail that the node cannot assume pairwise graph edges.

## F18 — derived-pattern child

**Result: REJECTED.**

Reachability, bottleneck, redundancy, centrality, clustering, density and hierarchy can summarize/derive organization. The structural pattern remains recoverable here; a numeric metric/value may be Condition when that value is the protected question.

No universal `global topology metrics` child is needed.

## F19 — cross-domain breakage

**Result: REJECTED.**

The node survives physical assemblies, deployed software, systems-of-systems, spatial systems, ecology, supply/market networks, institutions, scientific setups and hybrid systems.

Different relation semantics do not create forced fit at the higher protected-question level.

## F20 — Domain Structure parent reopen

**Result: REJECTED.**

No third co-equal Structure problem emerges and the accepted Composition-versus-Relational-Organization split remains independently recoverable.

## F21 — bounded leaf rival

**Result: SUPPORTED.**

Late search yields relation subtypes, formal representation alternatives, cross-cutting dimensions, derived metrics and neighbouring-ground boundary cases rather than a stable immediate child split.

`Relational Organization` therefore closes as a bounded leaf at the current foundation-research depth.
