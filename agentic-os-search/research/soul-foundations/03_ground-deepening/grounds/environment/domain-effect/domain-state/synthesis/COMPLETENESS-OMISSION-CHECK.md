# 1. Frozen falsifier results

## F1 — `State / Configuration` is an arbitrary bundle

**Partly confirmed and corrected.**

The inherited parent label was too ambiguous because `configuration` can mean structural arrangement, settings or a documented baseline. The node remains coherent after shortening/reframing to:

> **Domain State** = realized domain condition/situation at selected scope/time.

The internal bundle is then made explicit through Structure/Composition and Condition/Status.

## F2 — Structure / Composition must be promoted

**Supported.**

Repeated evidence and holdouts show a distinct first-order question around:
- what constituents are present;
- how they are composed/organized/connected.

Wrong topology/composition can coexist with correct condition readings and requires different recovery reasoning.

## F3 — Identity / Continuity must be promoted

**Not supported as State-local child.**

Identity is load-bearing but:
- underlies both Structure and Condition bearer/reference questions;
- crosses State into Dynamics through persistence/change;
- has domain- and purpose-relative criteria.

Keep explicit and cross-cutting.

## F4 — Properties/Status and Relations/Topology require separate children

**Supported only at the broader Structure-vs-Condition level.**

A three-way primitive split (`Constituents`, `Relations`, `Properties`) over-decomposes the project problem:
- constituents + organizing relations jointly constitute the structural question;
- properties/status constitute the condition question;
- not every relation is structural (e.g. some current institutional standings are condition/status relations).

Thus relation arity/type is not the child rule.

## F5 — Constraints / Invariants form a third child

**Not supported.**

Constraints/invariants generally limit admissible states and/or transitions. They are predicates/limits over State/Dynamics rather than a current-state region by identity.

A currently binding constraint can produce a current status/condition fact, but the rule/constraint family remains cross-cutting and may have represented forms in Information.

## F6 — State granularity/sufficiency destroys stable child decomposition

**Rejected as a reason to dissolve the node.**

State sufficiency is strongly purpose/model relative, but this concerns which realized facts a later representation must select. It does not erase the recurring reality-side distinction between organization/composition and current condition/status.

Granularity remains cross-cutting.

## F7 — `State` is too model-centric and must be replaced

**Weakened but not forced.**

`State`, `situation`, `condition` and `state of affairs` all carry disciplinary baggage.

`Domain State` remains the strongest concise working label if explicitly defined as architecture-neutral realized condition/situation and kept distinct from one formal state vector or status Record.

## F8 — institutional/social state breaks actual-vs-representation semantics

**Over-strong independence formulation rejected; core boundary survives.**

Institutional reality can depend on collective rules, roles and representational practices. The safe project claim is:

> actual institutional/domain standing ≠ any particular observation/model/assertion/Record of it.

This allows socially constituted reality without collapsing it into Information.

## F9 — a materially different domain exposes a missing first-order State family

**Not found in current holdouts.**

Tested:
- deployed software / SOUL-product work;
- manufacturing/physical asset;
- ecology;
- social/institutional;
- financial/market;
- scientific specimen/system;
- hybrid physical/digital/digital-twin target;
- identity-through-replacement;
- snapshot-insufficient/history-dependent cases.

Late cases mostly mapped to Structure, Condition or cross-cutting dimensions.

## F10 — Domain State is a bounded leaf

**Rejected for current purpose.**

A leaf loses a recurrent, decision-relevant difference:
- `wrong things/organization` versus
- `wrong condition/status`.

These produce different failure analysis and later reasoning, so the two-child split is materially productive.

# 2. Fresh challenge set against ritualized validation

To avoid simply repeating the prior Domain-Reality challenge set, the completion review added structurally different cases.

## A — domain with almost no meaningful internal structure

A narrow scalar/state variable domain may mainly exercise Condition/Status.

Result:
- Structure child may be non-applicable/minimal;
- hierarchy does not require every child to be populated in every case.

No new child.

## B — domain dominated by topology

A network/connectivity problem may mainly exercise Structure/Composition while current component conditions are secondary.

Result:
- Condition child may be minimal;
- again no universal mandatory-field interpretation.

## C — relational status that is not structural

Example pattern: a current legal/institutional relation or standing.

Result:
- demonstrates that `relation = Structure` is false;
- Condition/Status can itself be relational.

The split is semantic/problem-role based, not graph arity.

## D — material/phase or configuration change with stable composition

Result:
- a domain can retain constituent membership while structural arrangement or condition changes;
- reinforces that structure and condition are independently variable analytical questions.

## E — component replacement with continuity claim

Result:
- Identity/Continuity becomes essential to compare states;
- still spans Structure and Dynamics and does not become a State-only third child.

## F — history-dependent future under identical selected snapshot

Result:
- exposes representation insufficiency or Dynamics/path-dependence;
- does not justify a `History` State child.

## G — constraint violation

Result:
- the fact `currently violating constraint X` is Condition/Status;
- constraint X itself remains an admissibility rule/physical relation potentially spanning State and Dynamics.

No third child.

- this is adversarial self-review, not independent verification;

- deeper Structure or Condition research may expose a sibling leak and reopen this node;
- later Dynamics, Information, Work or Actors research may expose boundary problems.

# 8. Stop judgment

## Domain State node

**BOUNDEDLY SUFFICIENT FOR CURRENT IMMEDIATE-CHILD PURPOSE; READY FOR USER EVALUATION.**

## Why broad State search should stop now

Information gain trajectory:
- inherited recovery exposed Structure/Identity/Constraint reopen risks;
- external systems/configuration/condition traditions produced a material two-child split;
- reality/representation research removed several false child candidates;
- identity, constraints and granularity were preserved explicitly but demoted from child status;
- cross-domain holdouts did not force a third region;
- fresh challenge cases mostly refined semantic overlap/non-applicability rather than child count.

Further broad search without a new falsifier is now more likely to refine terminology or domain profiles than to alter the immediate hierarchy.

## Reopen if

- deeper Structure research cannot contain membership/composition/topology without forced fit;
- deeper Condition research exposes a missing co-equal State region;
- identity or constraints repeatedly require dedicated first-order handling that cannot remain cross-cutting;
- a materially different domain exposes a recurrent State-local problem outside both children;
- later Dynamics/Information/Work/Actors research reveals boundary leakage;
- independent review offers a materially stronger decomposition.
