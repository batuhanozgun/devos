# 1. Frozen falsifier results

## F1 — inherited `Structure / Composition` is an arbitrary bundle

**Partly confirmed and corrected.**

`Composition` was too narrow as a co-primary synonym for the whole node. The node survives after refinement to `Domain Structure` and then splits one level deeper.

## F2 — Constituents/Membership and Relations/Topology require separate immediate children

**Supported at the deeper Structure level.**

The constituent-set/part-whole question and relational-organization question can fail independently and produce different reconstruction steps across multiple domains.

## F3 — Spatial Arrangement / Containment requires its own child

**Not supported.**

Spatial/topological relations are rich and sometimes irreducible to classical parthood, but they remain a relation/arrangement specialization. When constitution of the whole is at issue, nested part-of belongs to Composition; when placement/contact/within/overlap is at issue, it belongs to Relational Organization.

## F4 — Hierarchy/Nesting and Network/Topology require separate children

**Not supported.**

NIST explicitly presents hierarchy as one representation and notes non-hierarchical networks. Nested systems and networks are structural forms that recurse through the two current children.

## F5 — Interfaces/Boundaries/Couplings form a missing child

**Not supported.**

Realized interface/interconnection fits Relational Organization. Boundary can also be analytical scope; exchange is Dynamics; availability/capacity can be Condition; interface specification is Information/architecture. A universal child would collapse roles.

## F6 — Presence/absence destroys Structure/Condition boundary

**Rejected with semantic distinction.**

`not structurally part/participating` is Composition. `structurally present but unhealthy/inactive/unavailable` is Condition. Addition/removal occurrence belongs to Dynamics with before/after Structure snapshots.

## F7 — Identity/Continuity must be a Structure-local child

**Not supported.**

Identity is necessary for snapshot membership but persistence across replacement/change crosses both Structure children, Condition bearers and Dynamics.

## F8 — Constraints/Invariants form a third child

**Not supported.**

Constraints restrict admissible membership, relations, conditions and transitions. They are not generally identical to the current realized structure.

## F9 — Multi-scale/Nested structure requires a Scale child

**Not supported.**

Scale/level selects reference and recurses through composition and relation questions. No independent first-order recovery region survives.

## F10 — physical/software/biological/social types are universal children

**Rejected.**

Hybrid CPS and cross-domain holdouts show heterogeneous types inside the same structure while reproducing the same two analytical questions.

## F11 — actual Structure cannot be separated from architecture/inventory/model/Record

**Rejected.**

NASA configuration audits explicitly compare actual work-product configuration with documented configuration, and Kubernetes abstractions can represent a changing runtime member set. Operational practice depends on possible divergence.

## F12 — institutional structure collapses into Actors/Information

**Rejected with guardrail.**

Realized institutional organization can be Domain Structure while Actor authority/capability and represented rules/charts retain their own semantic ownership.

## F13 — materially different domain breaks the node or split

**Not found.**

Software, physical assembly, infrastructure, ecology/biology, institutions, markets/supply networks, scientific setups and hybrid CPS all fit without forced third-child creation.

## F14 — deeper Structure exposes missing co-equal Domain State region

**Not triggered.**

No evidence from this pass forces a third Domain State child or collapse of Structure/Condition.

## F15 — Domain Structure is a bounded leaf

**Rejected.**

The two-child split materially changes diagnosis/recovery and survives cross-domain challenge.

# 2. Fresh challenge set against ritualized validation

The audit added challenges that were not enough merely to repeat the parent Domain State examples.

## A — right members, wrong relations

Correct deployed components but wrong dependency/connection/placement.

Result: requires Relational Organization independently of Composition.

## B — wrong members, coherent relation map

A missing or extra constituent with internally coherent known links.

Result: requires Composition independently of Relational Organization.

## C — topology definition that includes nodes and links

Network-topology traditions often define topology as arrangement of both nodes and links.

Result:
- warns against using formal `Topology` as the child boundary;
- does **not** collapse the project split because the semantic recovery questions still separate `what participates` from `how participants are related`;
- primary label remains `Relational Organization`, with `Topology` only explanatory.

## D — part-whole relation is itself a relation

Mereology formally treats parthood as a relation.

Result:
- confirms that the project split cannot be based on relation arity;
- semantic ownership places part-whole under Composition because it answers constitution/membership of the whole;
- other structural relations answer organization among relevant constituents.

## E — mereotopology can unify part-whole and connectedness

Formal work can combine mereology and topology into one theory.

Result:
- shows ontology/formal unification is possible;
- does not remove the independently useful project failure split;
- reinforces that this research tree is not a claim about primitive ontology.

## F — one domain with nearly no internal organization

A narrowly scoped object may have trivial membership and little relational structure.

Result: both children can be minimal/non-material. The hierarchy does not require mandatory population.

## G — highly networked domain with fuzzy membership

Supply, market or ecological networks may have changing/fuzzy participation and many relations.

Result:
- scope/currentness/identity become critical;
- still no third child; uncertainty/evidence belongs later to Information.

## H — spatial containment differs from structural parthood

A thing can be located within another region without being a constituent part of the selected whole.

Result: validates semantic separation of Composition and spatial Relational Organization rather than a spatial third child.

## I — role/authority relation in institution

An organizational relation can be structurally constitutive and also carry Actor authority semantics.

Result: role switching/cross-ground ownership is necessary, but no separate social Structure child is justified.

## J — connection whose activity is zero

Two nodes remain structurally connected while no flow occurs.

Result: strengthens Structure/Dynamics separation.
