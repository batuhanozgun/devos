# Domain Structure Finding 02 — Constituent Composition / Membership

**Status:** bounded child-promotion finding  
**Date:** 2026-09-03

## Question

Does the realized constituent/part-whole problem deserve a distinct immediate child under Domain Structure rather than remaining inseparable from topology/relations?

## Result

**Yes. `Constituent Composition / Membership` passes the immediate-child test.**

Primary question:

> **Which materially relevant constituents actually participate in or make up the selected whole/scope, including materially relevant part–whole and nested membership?**

## What belongs here when material

- constituent/member presence and absence;
- participation in the selected structural whole/network;
- part–whole composition;
- subsystem/subassembly/component nesting;
- aggregation/decomposition relevant to the realized whole;
- scope-bound membership where the selected domain is a group/network rather than a material whole.

The branch is not restricted to classical physical parthood. A market, service, institution or supply network can have participating constituents without every participant being a metaphysical `part` in the same sense as a machine component.

## Distinct failure surface

Examples:
- all known links among deployed services are correct, but one required service is missing;
- a machine's interfaces are correct among installed parts, but the wrong subassembly is installed;
- a supply network has correct known routes, but an actual supplier is omitted from the selected network;
- an institution's relation map is internally consistent but the realized organizational unit/member set is wrong;
- a specimen/setup is arranged correctly for the components present, but the wrong specimen/component is present.

Recovery begins by reconstructing **what actually belongs/participates**, not by first measuring condition or analyzing link topology.

## External discrimination

- SEP Mereology treats parthood, decomposition and composition as a substantive problem family in its own right.
- Manufacturing/BOM traditions recursively represent product/subassembly/component composition.
- Kubernetes Services expose a live example in which the backend Pod set can change over time independently of the access abstraction.
- Supply-chain network work separately identifies participating organizations as nodes and flows/links among them as edges.

These traditions differ substantially; their agreement is only used to show that constituent-set/composition questions recur and can fail independently of relational organization.

## Why this is not merely a graph `node` primitive

Promotion is not based on the fact that models have nodes.

The protected reasoning question is whether the selected real-world whole/network is constituted by the right participants/parts at the relevant scope/time. The representation could be a graph, table, inventory, physical inspection, narrative or no stored model at all.

## Presence / absence boundary with Condition

Use the semantic role, not vocabulary alone:

- `component X is not part of / does not participate in the selected realized structure` → Composition / Membership;
- `component X remains part of the structure but is unavailable, unhealthy, inactive or degraded` → Condition / Status;
- `component X was removed/added` as an occurrence/transition → Dynamics, while the before/after membership snapshots belong here.

Ambiguous domains may require explicit scope and identity criteria.

## Identity warning

Membership presupposes a way to identify the relevant constituent at the snapshot. But identity/continuity across replacement is not promoted here because it also governs Condition bearers and through-time Dynamics.

- **F6 presence/absence destroys Structure/Condition boundary:** rejected with the membership-versus-current-condition distinction.
- **F7 identity must be Structure-local child:** rejected; identity remains cross-cutting.
