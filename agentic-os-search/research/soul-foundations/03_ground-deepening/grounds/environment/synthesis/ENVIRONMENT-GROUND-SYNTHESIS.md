## Executive conclusion

The deeper parent pass **supports Environment / Operating Reality as a coherent top-level research ground**, but materially refines the lower hierarchy inherited from `02_adequacy`.

The strongest decomposition at closure of this parent pass was:

```text
Environment / Operating Reality                 [working parent label]
    ├─ Domain / Effect Reality
    │    └─ actual reality Work is about, must accommodate, navigate,
    │       understand, maintain, transform, or judge outcomes against
    │
    └─ Work Conditions / Enabling–Constraining Environment
         └─ realized conditions, capabilities, resources, interfaces,
            hazards and constraints that materially shape execution
             └─ Technical Work Environment
                 = existing deep technical specialization
```

The branch labels were provisional. The structural result was stronger than the terminology.

This is a **research/problem hierarchy**, not a taxonomy of objects, architecture modules or schema classes.

---

# 1. What survived from the adequacy hypothesis

## One broad parent survives

The evidence does not require restoring Operating World and Technical Work Environment as peer top-level grounds.

They share a productive higher grammar around:
- selected reference/system-of-interest/focal Work;
- boundary and load-bearing participation;
- actual realized state rather than only descriptions;
- independently changing conditions/dynamics;
- coupling/interfaces/resources;
- constraints and disturbances;
- incomplete observability;
- effects on feasibility/risk/outcomes;
- nesting and role switching.

Systems-engineering definitions are compatible with a broad environment notion spanning use/support/development/lifecycle surroundings while preserving operational and enabling-system distinctions.

## Lower separation remains mandatory

The parent must not erase the difference between:
- **what reality the Work is about or affects**; and
- **under what actual conditions the Work can be carried out**.

The same concrete system/condition can occupy either or both roles depending on focal Work.

---

# 2. Material correction — `Work-Support Environment` was too narrow

The substantive correction produced by this pass is semantic, not merely terminological.

`support` can be read as a positive enabling resource/tool/service relation. Cross-lineage evidence and counterexamples show that execution is also shaped by conditions that:
- constrain;
- degrade;
- interfere;
- create hazards;
- limit capacity;
- impose physical/social/institutional operating conditions;
without naturally being called `support`.

Examples:
- heat/noise/layout;
- adverse weather;
- resource scarcity/contention;
- inaccessible location;
- service outage;
- regulatory prohibition in its realized-condition role.

The existing TWE research already included constraints, isolation, degradation, unavailability and capacity, so the previous *content* was broader than the branch name.

Current stronger branch concept from this pass:

> **Work Conditions / Enabling–Constraining Environment**

Technical Work Environment remains a deep specialization of this branch.

---

# 3. Parent definition

The strongest bounded formulation from this pass is:

> **Environment / Operating Reality is the boundary- and reference-relative ground of realized conditions and dynamics that matter to focal Work because they constitute the reality/effect space the Work must understand, accommodate or change, and/or because they materially enable, constrain, degrade or otherwise shape execution.**

Mandatory qualifications:
- not everything outside;
- not everything relevant;
- not all context;
- not an intrinsic object family;
- not the representation/model of conditions;
- not ownership of Actor, Work or Information semantics merely because those things contribute to a condition.

Environmental participation requires a defensible load-bearing coupling to target/effect or execution-condition semantics.

---

# 4. Role A — Domain / Effect Reality

Primary question at parent depth:

> **What actual reality is the Work about, trying to understand/change/maintain/navigate/accommodate, or using as the ground of real-world success and effects?**

Includes when material:
- domain entities/processes;
- actual state;
- natural/engineered/social/institutional dynamics in their domain role;
- exogenous changes/disturbances;
- Work-produced changes;
- side effects/partial effects;
- state against which outcome must ultimately be judged.

Examples:
- patient physiology;
- product/system behavior;
- specimen/phenomenon;
- traffic/terrain;
- damaged infrastructure;
- market/business state.

### Critical rule

> **Work closure/status ≠ actual domain effect.**

A task can complete while the intended environmental result fails, remains partial or changes later.

Later dedicated research refined this branch name and structure; follow the notice at the top for current routing.

---

# 5. Role B — Work Conditions / Enabling–Constraining Environment

Primary question:

> **What realized conditions/capabilities/resources/interfaces materially shape whether and how the focal Work can be performed?**

Includes when material:
- technical work-support systems;
- physical workspace and environmental conditions;
- equipment/material/service availability;
- capacity/scarcity/contention;
- location/access/route conditions;
- communication/logistics infrastructure;
- hazards/interference;
- institutional/regulatory conditions in their realized feasibility role;
- persons/organizations in a support-condition role, without absorbing their Actor semantics.

### Technical Work Environment

The existing TWE corpus remains a strong deep specialization covering:
- support closure;
- configuration/resolution/live state;
- capabilities/resources/interfaces/constraints;
- sharedness/isolation;
- identity/equivalence;
- lifecycle;
- readiness/availability/capacity;
- observability/control;
- reproducibility/portability/dynamic resolution.

These specialized technical semantics should not all be mechanically generalized to every Environment case.

# 6. No third first-order Environment branch currently required at parent depth

The pass explicitly tested:
- physical environment;
- social environment;
- institutional/legal environment;
- economic/resource environment;
- ecological/natural environment;
- ambient/background conditions;
- hazards/risk;
- infrastructure/tools;
- other agents/systems;
- temporal/spatial conditions;
- specified operating envelope;
- unknown/uncertain environment.

Mapping rule at this depth:
- matters because Work is about/affects/accommodates it → Domain/Effect role;
- matters because it shapes execution → Work Conditions;
- both roles may apply;
- neither → not load-bearing Environment merely by existence.

No tested case required a third sibling region.

This is bounded evidence, not universal proof.

---

# 7. Environment is actual-condition side, not representation side

Across both prior project corpora and external holdouts, preserve:

```text
actual Environment condition
    ≠ specified operating envelope/profile
    ≠ observation/measurement
    ≠ inferred/modelled/believed state
    ≠ persistent Record
```

ISO 34503's ODD concept provides a clear domain example: a system can be specified to operate within defined conditions while the actual current conditions may or may not satisfy them.

Environment can exist/change while poorly observed, wrongly modelled or incompletely recorded.

Therefore:

> **realization is primary; representation is evidence/model about realization.**

The representation side is intentionally deferred to the Information ground/composition phase.

---

# 8. Dynamics are first-order, not optional metadata

Environment is not a context snapshot.

Load-bearing dynamics can include:
- exogenous transitions;
- disturbances/shocks;
- resource/capacity variation;
- service degradation/recovery;
- movement/spatial change;
- other-system action;
- institutional/regulatory change;
- Work-caused effects;
- delayed/path-dependent consequences.

The same Work description can produce different feasibility/outcomes under different realized Environment trajectories.

History may matter causally without making Environment itself a Record/history store.

---

# 9. Actionability is relational

Environment contributes condition-side facts to feasibility/action possibility, but does not own the full relation.

Keep distinct:
- Actor capability/competence;
- authority/permission;
- Work requirement/purpose;
- Environment condition/resource/interface;
- situated actionability.

Affordance traditions independently support action possibilities as relational between environmental structure and actor capacities.

Thus:

> **Actor capability ≠ situated actionability.**

This remains a deferred cross-ground composition problem after Environment captures its own condition-side semantics.

---

# 11. Boundary, nesting and role switching

Environment classification is reference-relative.

Keep separate:
- concrete identity;
- Environment participation;
- Environment role.

A CI cluster, hospital device, facility, person or external service can move between target/domain, execution-condition and internal-system roles as focal Work/boundary changes.

This is not an exception; it is a core Environment property.

Systems-engineering sources reinforce nested systems and observer-/analysis-dependent boundaries.

---

# 12. Boundary-leak rule

The broad parent remains useful only under:

> **environmental participation ≠ semantic ownership**

Examples:
- a person can be a current support condition while identity/authority remain Actor semantics;
- a regulation can constrain execution while its text/interpretation remain Information and its issuer/enforcement semantics involve Actors/control;
- another Work's output can become an Environment condition while the producing activity remains Work;
- a database/manual can support Work while meaning/provenance remain Information.

This allows multi-ground participation without turning Environment into an `everything else` bucket.

# 13. Rival decompositions

## Five peers / separate Domain + support top-level grounds
Useful anti-collapse view but weaker level alignment; repeats shared higher grammar and creates unnecessary peer specificity.

## One undifferentiated Environment
Rejected: loses target/effect versus execution-condition failures.

## Three Environment branches with `ambient/exogenous`
Not currently justified. Apparently ambient cases map by why they are load-bearing.

## `Context`
Rejected as parent label because context is too broad/relevance/representation-oriented and can absorb Work, Actors and Information.

## `System Context`
Strong analytical method, but usually broader/system-centric rather than the exact Environment semantic ground.

## relation-only/no Environment ground
Possible later representation, but insufficient as research decomposition because it does not force explicit investigation of realized state, dynamics, hidden conditions and actual effects.

Current candidate at parent closure survives best.

# 15. SOUL / Development OS implication — research only

The Environment ground gives later reasoning a disciplined way to ask two complementary questions for any focal Work:

1. **Domain:** what actual reality is this Work about and what realized state/change matters?
2. **Conditions of execution:** under what actual conditions/resources/interfaces/constraints can it be carried out?

Then separately, in other grounds/later composition:
- what is represented/known about those conditions? → Information;
- what is the activity/lifecycle? → Work;
- who/what participates with capability/authority/responsibility? → Actors.

This is not yet a SOUL module design.

For Development OS, the distinction naturally separates:
- actual product/system/repository behavior as Domain Reality;
- GitHub/CI/runtimes/credentials/workspaces/etc. as technical execution conditions when used as support.

For general-purpose SOUL, the same grammar survives physical, clinical, scientific, institutional and hybrid cases.
