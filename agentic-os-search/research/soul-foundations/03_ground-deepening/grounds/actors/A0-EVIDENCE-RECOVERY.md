## 4. Accepted Information boundary recovered

Information owns mediated representations and their formation/transition.

Actors therefore must not silently own informational artifacts such as:

- identity records;
- role assignments represented in files/directories;
- policy statements;
- authorization tokens/claims;
- delegation records;
- logs/evidence of action;
- reputation/trust scores;
- assertions that someone is responsible/accountable.

Critical audit rule:

```text
representation says Actor standing = X
!= actual Actor standing is X
```

An informational artifact may be evidence for identity/authority/participation without being identical to the Actor-side relation it represents.

## 5. Accepted Environment boundary recovered

Environment owns actual realized domain/runtime conditions and Work Conditions.

Actors must therefore distinguish standing/intrinsic/relational capability from situated actionability.

Examples:

- a bearer may be competent but the required tool/resource/interface is unavailable;
- an actor may be authorized but technically blocked;
- a software agent may have code-level capability but sandbox/network/credential constraints prevent action;
- a role may permit an operation but current environment conditions make it impossible or unsafe.

Critical audit rule:

```text
Actor capability/authority
!= actual Environment capability/resource/access condition
!= resulting situated actionability
```

## 6. Accepted Work boundary recovered

Work owns the organization/evolution of pursuits, including candidate/admission/execution/coordination/evaluation/closure/recovery.

Actors must therefore distinguish:

- participation/assignment from Work identity;
- Work admission from Actor obligation/authorization;
- Work requiredness from bearer duty;
- acceptance status from authority to accept;
- continuing Work from continuing Actor responsibility.

Critical audit rule:

```text
Work selected/admitted/required
!= Actor promised/obligated/authorized
```

## 8. Live audit hypotheses entering A1/A2

These are hypotheses to attack, not findings.

### H1 — Actors may steal Information state

Identity/role/authority/delegation may be inferred from records/tokens/context rather than preserving actual standing vs representation.

### H2 — Actors may steal Environment actionability

`Capability` may conflate bearer competence with actual resource/tool/network/credential availability.

### H3 — Actors may steal Work semantics

Participation, assignment, commitment and obligation may collapse Work admission/requiredness into Actor standing.

### H4 — Effective/transitive authority may be missing

A bearer with limited direct authority may obtain effects through another reachable bearer/tool with stronger authority. It is unclear whether existing relational/combinatorial authority + delegation/path semantics fully cover this.

### H5 — Epistemic authority/trust/expertise may be missing

Being believed, trusted or treated as an expert may not reduce cleanly to competence, authority, attribution or Information standing.

### H6 — Affected/beneficiary/rights-holder positions may require a broader ground

Semantically relevant parties need not act, decide or hold authority. This may pressure the `Actors` label toward a broader `Parties/Agency/Standing` region.

### H7 — Continuing/stewardship responsibility may be missing

Standing responsibility may persist without a current episode and may not reduce to Work requiredness or generic obligation.

### H8 — Non-human operational discretion may exceed current treatment

Modern agents may autonomously choose tools, delegate, spawn subagents and adapt plans; this may require more than `operational participation vs normative attribution`.

### H9 — Current multi-anchor map may be a grab-bag

The many separations may be useful but fail to form one coherent protected Actors problem region.

### H10 — A stable child decomposition may exist

Possible splits such as `Bearer/Standing`, `Participation/Agency`, `Authority/Representation`, `Attribution/Responsibility` may preserve enough separation to justify immediate children—or may falsely partition cross-cutting relations.
