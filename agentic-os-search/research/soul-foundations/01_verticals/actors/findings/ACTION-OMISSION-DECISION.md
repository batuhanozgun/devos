# Actors Finding — Action, Decision, Refraining and Omission

**Status:** current topic finding  
**Scope:** Actors-local corrective to event-only analysis

## Current finding

An `action event` is not a sufficient universal locus for the Actors problem region.

Decisions, commitments, intentional refraining, and responsibility for failures to act can be semantically important even where no positive action event is the right object of analysis.

This finding materially revises the initial non-Actor-centred alternative frame.

## Why this matters

### 1. Observed action ≠ full agency surface

Action theory recognizes cases in which agency is expressed by refraining or intentional omission. Responsibility theory likewise includes cases where the relevant question is why a bearer did not perform an available or expected action.

If a model records only positive events and their performers, it can make a duty breach or deliberate non-action invisible.

### 2. Omission depends on a counterfactual/normative background

A `failure to act` is not just an empty event slot. It normally depends on some combination of:

- an available or possible action;
- an expectation, duty, plan, intention or opportunity;
- relevant control/capability;
- a time/context in which action could or should have occurred.

The Actors region therefore interacts with possibility, obligation and context rather than only event occurrence.

### 3. Performer ≠ decider

Even in positive-action episodes, the performer may differ from the person/system that selected, approved, requested or committed to the course of action. Decision participation must not be inferred from execution alone.

### 4. Non-action does not erase attribution questions

Where responsibility for omission is meaningful, the same distinctions still matter: who had capacity, who had authority, who had a duty, who knew, who could control the outcome, and who is answerable.

## Decision-critical implication

The literal `event + participant` alternative is rejected as too narrow.

The stronger non-Actor-centred frame is now:

> **situation/episode + possible/expected conduct + typed participation/decision/omission relations**

Persistent bearers, standing roles/authority and attribution remain separate anchors because an episode/situation alone cannot explain continuity or standing obligations.

This is not a proposed SOUL schema. It is a falsification result: later synthesis must not require a positive action event in order to represent every meaningful agency/responsibility case.

## Evidence used

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Action

Inspected view:
- current/recent web entry, including discussion of intentional omissions and refrainings as manifestations of agency.

Source:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/action/

Supports:
- agency need not be exhausted by positive action events.

Use limitation:
- philosophical theories of action are contested; the evidence is used only to establish that event-only analysis has a serious counterexample class.

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Moral Responsibility and Alternative Possibilities

Inspected view:
- web entry section on responsibility for omissions.

Source:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/alternative-possibilities/

Supports:
- responsibility can attach to what a person did not do, with capability/alternative possibilities relevant to the analysis.

Use limitation:
- no particular philosophical principle about alternative possibilities is adopted as a SOUL rule.

## Residual uncertainty

- The ontology of omissions is philosophically contested and not required to be solved by this foundation research.
- The later synthesis must decide how much explicit structure is necessary to represent expected/possible conduct without prematurely modelling Work or normative governance inside Actors.
