# Actors Finding — Responsibility, Attribution and Accountability

**Status:** current topic finding  
**Scope:** Actors-local semantic distinctions; not a complete theory of responsibility

## Current finding

`Responsibility` is not a single first-order property. Different sources use it for materially different relations: causal contribution, duty/obligation, attributability, moral responsibility, institutional responsibility and accountability.

`Accountability` is especially poorly represented as an intrinsic Actor attribute; a strong public-administration formulation treats it as an actor–forum relation with information, questioning/judgment and possible consequences.

## Distinctions to preserve

### 1. Causal responsibility ≠ moral responsibility

The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy explicitly distinguishes causing an outcome from being morally responsible for it. The capacities and relations needed for moral responsibility are not identical to causal powers.

A system or person can therefore be causally involved without automatically being blameworthy or answerable in the moral sense.

### 2. Responsibility-as-obligation ≠ responsibility-for-past-action

Ordinary language also uses `responsibility` to mean an obligation or duty: what someone is supposed to do. That forward-looking sense differs from retrospective attribution for an outcome.

### 3. Attributability ≠ accountability

An action or outcome may properly be attributed to a bearer without yet establishing every condition for blame, sanction or formal answerability.

The evidence supports keeping provenance/causal/legal attribution separate from stronger normative responsibility judgments.

### 4. Accountability is relational and procedural

The Bovens public-accountability tradition defines an accountability relation around an actor and a forum: the actor has an obligation to explain and justify conduct; the forum can question and judge; consequences may follow.

This means `is accountable` is incomplete without at least the relevant forum, subject matter/standard and process or consequence context.

### 5. Control and epistemic position can matter

Moral-responsibility literature commonly separates a control/freedom condition from an epistemic/knowledge condition. This research does not import those philosophical criteria as a universal SOUL rule, but they demonstrate that responsibility can depend on what a participant could control and know, not merely on role or causal connection.

### 6. Collective responsibility does not eliminate individual responsibility

Collective-responsibility literature treats genuinely collective responsibility as distinct from simply summing individual blame. At the same time, collective responsibility need not imply that all members are individually blameless or equally responsible.

The later synthesis must therefore avoid a zero-sum choice between group and individual responsibility.

## Decision-critical implication

The initial assumption `responsibility = accountability = Actor property` is rejected.

A later synthesis must preserve the possibility of separately representing or reasoning about:

- causal contribution;
- provenance/attribution;
- duty or obligation;
- responsibility for an action/outcome under a norm;
- answerability to a forum;
- judgment/consequence or sanction context;
- control/knowledge conditions where the relevant responsibility regime requires them;
- individual and collective responsibility without forced mutual exclusion.

## Evidence used

### Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — Moral Responsibility

Inspected view:
- current/recent web entry, substantive revision 3 June 2024.
- sections distinguishing moral responsibility from obligation and causal responsibility.

Source:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/

Supports:
- responsibility is semantically plural;
- causal responsibility does not entail moral responsibility.

Use limitation:
- philosophical moral responsibility is not treated as a universal institutional rule.

View-fidelity limitation:
- the complete 2007 publisher text was not inspected in this pass. Only the actor–forum formulation and mechanism/virtue distinction visible in later scholarly/publication surfaces are used as evidence.

## Residual uncertainty

- Which responsibility regimes SOUL will eventually need—legal, operational, governance, moral, contractual, safety, etc.—is a later use-context decision.
- `Accountability` varies across institutional settings; the actor–forum model establishes relational structure, not one universal enforcement mechanism.
