# Actors — Remaining Actors-Local Questions

**Status:** current residual surface  
**Scope:** unresolved questions that remain inside the Actors problem region after the bounded foundation research.

These are not evidence that the vertical must remain open indefinitely. Reopen substantive research only if one becomes decision-critical for the foundation purpose or later evidence materially falsifies the current synthesis.

## OQ1 — Bearer continuity rules

What makes a person, organization, office, software system or other bearer `the same` across time for the specific semantic purpose at hand?

Current result:
- persistent continuity matters;
- identity is not sufficient to determine role/authority/participation;
- organization/system continuity rules are domain-sensitive.

Why left open:
- resolving mergers, succession, account migration, software replacement, legal identity and social identity would require domain-specific modelling beyond the current foundation need.

## OQ2 — Role vs office/position

Should `role`, `office`, `position`, `appointment` and `membership` be separate first-order constructs in later synthesis?

Current result:
- occupant identity must be separable from standing institutional position;
- role activation can be contextual.

Why left open:
- the current evidence establishes non-collapse but not a universal vocabulary across organizations, law and access control.

## OQ3 — Competence decomposition

How much structure is needed to distinguish skill, qualification, certification, experience, demonstrated performance and resource availability?

Current result:
- competence/capability must not collapse into permission/authority/legal capacity.

Why left open:
- detailed competence taxonomies are domain-specific and did not alter the first-order Actors findings during omission probes.

## OQ4 — Permission vs authority/power

In which future use contexts must `permission`, `authority`, `power`, `mandate` and `entitlement` be separate?

Current result:
- they cannot safely be assumed identical;
- authorization can be policy-, context- and relation-dependent.

Why left open:
- exact distinctions vary across security, law, governance and organizational practice.

## OQ5 — Shared vs corporate agency thresholds

What minimum integration makes several participants a shared-action unit, and what minimum organization makes a collective a corporate/group agent?

Current result:
- mere aggregation, shared/joint agency and corporate/group agency are distinct;
- organizational decision procedures can license group-level attribution.

Why left open:
- the threshold is contested in the underlying literature and depends on the semantic question.

## OQ6 — Representation of omissions/non-action

How should a later model represent an expected/available action that did not occur without confusing the absence of an event with a positive event?

Current result:
- event-only analysis is insufficient;
- meaningful omission depends on context, possibility/capability and often a norm/expectation.

Why left open:
- a detailed representation risks prematurely importing Work or normative-governance structure into Actors.

## OQ7 — Non-human agency boundary

When, if ever, should a non-human system be treated as an enduring agent rather than only an operational participant, tool, provenance agent or component of a joint system?

Current result:
- there is no single context-free Actor status that preserves operational, provenance, legal and moral semantics;
- operational action does not entail legal/moral personhood.

Why left open:
- AI personhood, moral agency and legal personality are contested questions unnecessary to the current bounded foundation result.

## OQ8 — Responsibility-regime selection

Which responsibility regimes will SOUL later need to distinguish explicitly: causal, operational, contractual, legal, governance, safety, moral, fiduciary, or others?

Current result:
- causal contribution, duty, attribution and accountability are not interchangeable.

Why left open:
- the required regimes depend on later authorized use contexts and architecture decisions.

## OQ9 — Affected and beneficiary positions

Are `affected party`, `beneficiary`, `recipient`, `rights holder` or similar positions part of the eventual Actors model, another foundation domain, or merely contextual relations?

Current result:
- event/relationship analysis can require parties who are not themselves performers or decision-makers;
- this further weakens `Actor = active doer` as a universal definition.

Why left open:
- determining ownership of these positions belongs to later cross-domain synthesis; they did not produce a new independent Actors-local semantic family during the current research.
