# Source Ledger — Records Management and Archival Science

**Research role:** establish competing definitions of `record`, the importance of context/relationships/evidence, and lifecycle/continuum tensions without assuming a software storage model.

## 1. ISO 15489-1:2016 — Records management concepts and principles

Source:
- https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html
- public ISO introduction deck: https://www.iso.org/home.isoDocumentsDownload.do?t=ieHkWblRDvFnWT3XDviIIV9SHZnpbYrw3W_44ExKX-8EeHQbvZkHab3bO9tesz_2

Observed:
- ISO frames record creation, capture and management as applicable in any environment and over time, rather than as a particular database/file technology.
- Records are framed as authoritative evidence with authenticity, reliability, integrity and usability characteristics.
- The ISO introduction says records consist of content plus metadata describing context, content, structure and management.
- Records processes include creation, capture, classification/indexing, access control, storage, use/reuse, migration/conversion and disposition.
- Appraisal determines what records should be made/kept and how they should be managed; keeping everything is not assumed.

Research implication:
- `record management` is materially broader than `storage management`.
- Content alone is an insufficient model for managed records in this tradition.

Caveat:
- ISO 15489 is a records-management standard. Its business/evidential framing should not silently become a universal ontology for every kind of persistent information.

## 2. Society of American Archivists Dictionary — record

Source:
- https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/record.html

Observed:
- The dictionary aggregates materially different definitions rather than presenting one uncontested essence.
- Definitions include information/data stored on a medium for memory/accountability; data whose content, context and structure are tied together; representations resulting from business events; and Yeo's `persistent representations of activities` formulation.
- A synthesis cited by the dictionary associates records with an action/transaction/event and at least enough metadata to provide contextual/evidential information.
- The dictionary explicitly warns that the generic term `record` can cause confusion because particular legal, technical and archival record types have different attributes.
- It also records the observation that records in information systems need not exist as discrete physical objects.

Research implication:
- `record` is not safely reducible to `file`, `row`, `document`, or `stored information`.
- Recordness may partly be a contextual/functional status rather than a material type. This remains a hypothesis, not a closed conclusion.

## 3. Society of American Archivists Dictionary — archival bond

Source:
- https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/archival-bond.html

Observed:
- The archival bond is the interrelationship between a record and other records arising from the same activity.
- Archival theory treats these relationships as inherent to records generated/accumulated through activity, not merely as later search links.
- Direct item access without contextual metadata can obscure creation/use context.

Research implication:
- Relationships and aggregation can carry meaning that item content alone does not.
- A system that preserves isolated items but loses their relevant relational context may preserve bytes while losing record meaning/evidence.

## 4. Society of American Archivists Dictionary — provenance / original context

Sources:
- https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/provenance.html
- https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/original-order.html

Observed:
- Archival provenance links the significance of records to their origin and context of creation.
- Original-order practice historically preserves creator-established arrangement where that arrangement carries evidential/contextual meaning.

Research implication:
- Organisation for preserving context may be different from organisation for later access/search. A subject taxonomy or search index should not automatically replace origin/context structure.

## 5. Yeo-derived `persistent representation` line

Sources:
- SAA record entry above, citing Geoffrey Yeo.
- secondary exploration: https://journal.kci.go.kr/ksas/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002436001

Observed:
- One influential line characterises records as persistent representations of activities/occurrents, made by participants, observers or authorised proxies.
- Secondary analysis of record definitions distinguishes evidence-oriented, information-oriented and activity-representation approaches.

Research implication:
- Persistence is important but persistence alone does not distinguish records from all other durable objects; representation-of-something and contextual relation to occurrence/activity are candidate differentiators.

Caveat:
- This is one theoretical framing among several. It is useful precisely because it competes with `record = stored information` and `record = evidence` framings.

## 6. Records lifecycle and records continuum

Source:
- James Lappin, `Rival records management models in an era of partial automation`, Archival Science: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10502-020-09354-9

Observed:
- The literature compares rival records-management models rather than treating a single lifecycle model as settled.
- Records-continuum theory describes overlapping dimensions around creation, capture, organisation and pluralisation rather than a purely linear end-of-life sequence.
- The continuum model also contains distinct axes for recordkeeping/evidence, transactional purpose/activity, and institutional/actor identity.

Research implication:
- `creation → maintenance/use → disposition` is an operationally useful lifecycle model, but should not be assumed to be the only correct conceptual model for digital records.
- The independent appearance of activity and actor/identity dimensions is relevant to later SOUL Foundations synthesis, but is explicitly deferred out of this vertical.

## Bounded source-level conclusion

Across these sources, the strongest common ground is not a single exact definition. It is that a managed record is more than undifferentiated stored content: persistence/recording, contextual anchoring, relationships, metadata, and later evidential/use value recur. The exact boundary between `information`, `document`, `record`, and other categories remains contested and must stay open in synthesis.
