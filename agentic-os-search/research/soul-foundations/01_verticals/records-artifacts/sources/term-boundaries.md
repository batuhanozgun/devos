# Source Ledger — Term Boundaries and Counter-Frames

**Research role:** challenge convenient equations among `record`, `artifact`, `document`, `information`, and `recordness`; preserve competing theoretical frames rather than selecting one by terminology.

## 1. NIST CSRC — artifact

Source:
- https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/artifact

Observed:
- NIST's glossary carries multiple source-dependent definitions of `artifact`.
- These include `a piece of evidence` and work products produced/used during a project to capture and convey information, with examples such as models and source code.
- NIST explicitly tells readers to consult the source context for each definition.

Research implication:
- `artifact` is a broad contextual term and cannot safely be equated with archival `record`.
- In technical/project discourse an artifact can be a produced work product; in assurance discourse it can be evidence.

## 2. NIST software-supply-chain terminology — artifacts as evidence of practice

Source:
- https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-order-14028-improving-nations-cybersecurity/software-supply-chain-security-guidance-10

Observed:
- NIST describes artifacts as evidence supporting conformity claims.
- Examples include threat models, log entries, source code, scan reports, test results, telemetry and risk-mitigation decisions.
- Higher-level artifacts can summarise lower-level artifacts.

Research implication:
- `artifact` can include both primary work products and derived summaries.
- Artifact status does not itself answer whether an object is authoritative, complete, current, persistent, or managed as a record.

## 3. SAA Dictionary — recordness

Source:
- https://dictionary.archivists.org/entry/recordness.html

Observed:
- `recordness` is defined as the quality/state of having record characteristics.
- The entry explicitly says the definition changes with purpose, law and context.
- Cited commentary both argues that structural/contextual data are crucial to recordness and criticises essentialist attempts to fix one timeless meaning of recordness.
- The notes distinguish fixity after archival capture from the possibility that a database/webpage changes during active use.

Research implication:
- There is direct archival-theory support for treating `recordness` as potentially contextual/status-like rather than a simple immutable material type.
- There is also explicit disagreement: some approaches seek defining record characteristics, others warn against essentialism.

This is a load-bearing counter-frame for the next research pass.

## 4. Record DNA / recent Archival Science reconceptualisation

Source:
- https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10502-023-09414-w

Observed:
- The paper reviews Yeo's move away from records as information containers toward records as persistent representations of activities/other occurrents.
- It treats the concept of a record as an active research problem in digital environments rather than a settled file/document category.

Research implication:
- A storage-container ontology is not the only contemporary archival frame.

## 5. `Recordness` as emergent property — counter-model

Source:
- Archivaria article, `Toward a Third Order Archival Interface`: https://archivaria.ca/index.php/archivaria/article/download/13721/15117/17447

Observed:
- The article proposes speaking of `recordness` as an emergent property of an information system rather than treating records only as discrete objects.
- It describes records/recordness through persistent representation of interactions and networks of relationships.

Research implication:
- This is a strong counterexample to any premature `Record` object-class assumption.
- It does not prove the emergent-property model is correct; it demonstrates that the category-vs-role/status question is genuinely contested and decision-critical.

## 6. Seol 2019 — three families of record definition

Source:
- https://journal.kci.go.kr/ksas/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002436001
- DOI 10.20923/kjas.2019.59.005

Observed:
- The study groups record definitions into three broad perspectives: evidence, information, and representation of activities.
- It analyses digital-record implications through fixity, materiality, activity/context and trustworthiness.

Research implication:
- The current research should compare definition families, not pick the first institutional standard as universal truth.

## Bounded conclusion

The strongest result of this ledger is negative but important:

> There is not yet enough evidence to treat `Record` as one universally fixed intrinsic object type, and there is clear theoretical evidence that recordness can depend on context, purpose, relationships and managed status.

This directly prioritises the next internal question: `type vs role/status vs family of domain-specific record types`.
