# Artifact — Counter-Frame Source Note

**Pass:** object boundaries adversarial check  
**Accessed:** 2026-09-01  
**Role:** counter-evidence against an overbroad conclusion that `artifact` lacks coherent ontological uses. This is a source note, not a primary source.

## Why this note was added

The object-boundaries pass found strong cross-domain polysemy in the unqualified word `artifact`. Before finalising a discard verdict for Records, this note asks the opposite question:

> Are there serious traditions in which `artifact` or `information artifact` is a coherent, intentionally bounded ontological concept?

The answer is **yes**. That does not make `artifact` a good Records super-category, but it narrows the verdict.

---

## C1 — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Artifact

URL: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/artifact/

### Direct observations

- A standard philosophical characterization treats artifacts as objects intentionally made to accomplish a purpose.
- The literature distinguishes artifact kinds, functions, material/abstract artifacts, and questions of identity.
- This is a coherent ontology problem, not merely loose terminology.

### Relevance

Counter-evidence to `artifact is intrinsically meaningless or undefinable`.

### Boundary

The philosophical category is much broader than persistent information and therefore does not by itself justify `artifact` as a Records-domain supertype.

## C2 — Information Artifact Ontology (IAO) uses in biomedical ontologies

Representative open-access sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7868854.1/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10510162/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8972912/

### Direct observations

- IAO provides ontology terms for data/information entities and is reused by multiple biomedical ontologies.
- A central class is `information content entity`, commonly defined as a generically dependent continuant that is about something.
- Implementations distinguish informational content from the material artifact/entity that bears or concretizes it.
- Documents, data elements, identifiers, measurement data and similar entities can be modelled under this information ontology.

### Relevance

Shows that a **qualified information-artifact / information-content ontology** can have disciplined semantics and explicit content/bearer separation.

### Boundary

This evidence does not establish that the unqualified word `artifact` should be the root category of the present Records research. In fact, the content/bearer distinction reinforces the need to specify which meaning of artifact is intended.

---

## C3 — Artifact ontology in philosophy of technology / computer science

Representative sources:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/technology/
- https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computer-science/

### Direct observations

- Technical artifacts are human-made systems/objects designed for functions.
- Their ontology can depend jointly on functional and structural properties.
- Computational systems are treated as technical artifacts in this tradition.

### Relevance

Again, `artifact` can be coherent when the qualifying ontology is fixed.

### Boundary

This is a **function/design** ontology. Importing it as the Records super-category would pull production purpose and Work semantics into a vertical whose current scope is persistent information.

---

# Adversarial result

The stronger claim:

> `artifact` is too polysemous to have any useful ontology

is **rejected**.

The narrower claim survives:

> **Unqualified `artifact` is too cross-domain and semantically unstable to serve as the foundational super-category of the Records vertical without first fixing a qualifying ontology.**

A qualified concept such as `information artifact` or `information content entity` remains a legitimate candidate for the broader persistent-information frame. It must be compared against alternatives such as `information resource`, `information object`, `record resource`, and similar concepts rather than inherited by name.

## Consequence for the current pass

- Do not use `artifact` as an unqualified foundational primitive.
- Do not conclude that artifact ontologies are invalid.
- Keep `information artifact` as an explicit counter-candidate in the unresolved broader-domain question.
- Do not rename `records-artifacts/` solely from this pass; the neutral broader category remains unresolved.
