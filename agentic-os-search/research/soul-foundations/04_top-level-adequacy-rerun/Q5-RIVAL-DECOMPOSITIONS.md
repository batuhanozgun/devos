# Top-Level Adequacy Rerun — Q5 Rival Top-Level Decompositions

**Status:** COMPLETE  
**Date:** 2026-09-05

## 1. Question

Is there a materially stronger top-level research decomposition than:

```text
Environment / Operating Reality
Information / Representation
Work
Actors / Agency–Participation–Authority

+ typed relations / control / transitions
```

The comparison is not about preferred vocabulary. A rival wins only if it preserves the load-bearing distinctions with less forced overlap/residuality and remains useful across materially different domains.

## 2. Comparison criteria

Each rival is evaluated on:

1. **referent/representation separation**;
2. **actual reality vs pursuit/action separation**;
3. **pursuit/Work vs Actor participation/authority separation**;
4. **standing/authority vs technical capability/actionability separation**;
5. **goal/value/governance handling without residual buckets**;
6. **level alignment across peers**;
7. **cross-domain stability**;
8. **ability to remain architecture-neutral**;
9. **research-routing usefulness**;
10. **relation burden** — whether the model merely hides necessary typed relations inside broad labels.

## 3. Baseline — current four-ground frame

```text
Environment / Operating Reality
Information / Representation
Work
Actors / Agency–Participation–Authority
```

### Strengths

- preserves actual reality vs representation;
- preserves pursuit semantics vs participation/agency;
- separates Actor standing from Environment actionability;
- has survived recursive ground deepening;
- does not require equal child shape;
- generalizes across software, emergency management, measurement, healthcare and governance-heavy cases;
- leaves relation/control as explicit later composition rather than hiding it.

### Weaknesses

- `Environment` and `Actors` remain overloaded everyday words;
- relation/control fabric is not yet researched systematically;
- the ground set alone is not a complete Foundation model;
- the exact final terminology remains provisional.

These weaknesses are real but do not yet amount to structural falsifiers.

## 4. Rival A — State / Process / Agent / Representation

```text
State
Process
Agent
Representation
```

### Attraction

Very compact. Resembles familiar ontology/system-analysis distinctions.

### Failure

It classifies by **formal mode** rather than protected problem semantics.

`State` immediately mixes:

- Environment state;
- Work state;
- Actor standing/state;
- Informational state.

`Process` similarly mixes:

- Domain Dynamics;
- Informational Dynamics;
- Work execution/evolution;
- Actor action/participation.

The exact distinctions our research repeatedly found necessary become lower exceptions inside generic State/Process buckets.

`Agent` roughly captures part of Actors but not non-performing rights holders/forums/standing relations cleanly.

### Verdict

**WEAKER.**

Useful as an ontological axis, not a stronger top-level research-ground map.

## 5. Rival B — World / Intent / Action / Knowledge / Agency

```text
World
Intent
Action
Knowledge
Agency
```

### Attraction

Makes purpose/intent explicit and looks natural for agentic reasoning.

### Failure

`World` under-specifies Work-support conditions versus target/domain reality.

`Knowledge` overclaims epistemic standing and under-covers false/conflicting/transient representations that still belong in Information.

`Action` collapses:

- deliberate Work;
- Actor action;
- Environment dynamics/effects;
- Information transformations.

`Intent` is not one stable locus:

- represented intent/specification → Information;
- pursuit direction/commitment → Work;
- preference/authority/commitment bearer → Actors;
- desired realized condition → Environment criterion/target relation.

### Verdict

**WEAKER.**

It promotes a cross-ground direction relation (`Intent`) while collapsing Work into a generic action category.

## 6. Rival C — Environment / Information / Activity / Parties / Norms

```text
Environment
Information
Activity
Parties
Norms
```

### Attraction

Directly addresses two pressures on the current map:

- `Parties` includes non-performing stakeholders better than `Actors`;
- `Norms` makes governance/authority explicit.

### Failure — Activity

`Activity` is broader and less precise than Work. It can denote:

- uncontrolled Environment processes;
- informational transformations;
- one Actor action event;
- deliberate pursuit/coordination.

The Work research showed why purpose, admission, dependency, coordination, continuing Work and closure cannot be recovered from generic activity occurrence.

### Failure — Parties

`Parties` improves one naming issue but loses agency/participation/authority emphasis. A party/bearer can have capability, role, delegation, omission and accountability semantics that are central to the current Actors ground.

A rename may be possible, but a separate Parties ground is not justified.

### Failure — Norms

`Norms` becomes a heterogeneous bucket for:

- Actor obligations/authority;
- policy texts/configuration in Information;
- governance/control Work;
- realized legal/institutional constraints in Environment;
- enforcement/transition relations.

### Verdict

**WEAKER AS A STRUCTURE.**

It identifies legitimate naming/control pressures but resolves them by reifying a cross-ground Norms bucket and weakening Work semantics.

## 7. Rival D — Resources / Work / Actors / Artifacts / Governance

```text
Resources
Work
Actors
Artifacts
Governance
```

### Attraction

Looks operationally useful for Development OS and software delivery.

### Failure

`Resources` is much narrower than actual Domain Reality and turns Environment into a support-only frame.

`Artifacts` is narrower than Information; observations, transient context, hypotheses, signals, models and non-persistent representations do not cleanly fit.

`Governance` repeats the cross-ground norm/control problem from Rival C.

It is strongly software/project-shaped and fails general-purpose SOUL domains such as patient physiology, market conditions or scientific phenomena.

### Verdict

**REJECTED for general Foundation use.**

Could be a task-specific operating profile, not the top-level research frame.

## 8. Rival E — Relations / Control as a fifth peer

```text
Environment
Information
Work
Actors
Relations / Control
```

### Attraction

Deepening produced extensive deferred-relation ledgers, and Q3 showed relation/control is mandatory.

### Failure

`Relations / Control` has no stable first-order semantic population independent of its endpoints.

It would mix:

- causation;
- provenance;
- observation;
- Work dependency;
- Actor delegation/authority;
- access;
- verification/support;
- target/effect;
- accountability;
- temporal transitions.

The category is meta-structural: it is about **how the grounds compose**.

### Verdict

**REJECTED AS PEER; PROMOTED IN IMPORTANCE AS NEXT RESEARCH PHASE.**

This is the strongest post-deepening correction to the old adequacy picture: relation/control is not a ground, but it is no longer merely a small appendix. It is the next major Foundation problem after ground adequacy.

## 9. Rival F — Goal / Value as a fifth peer

```text
Environment
Information
Work
Actors
Goal / Value
```

### Attraction

Purpose and outcome are load-bearing for general-purpose SOUL.

### Failure

The candidate repeatedly decomposes into:

- Actor preferences/authority/obligations;
- Work direction/selection/stopping;
- Information criteria/specifications/evidence;
- Environment target/realized standing.

A fifth Goal ground would either duplicate these facts or become a container for a typed cross-ground directedness relation.

### Verdict

**REJECTED AS PEER.**

Goal/outcome/value must receive explicit treatment during composition, not disappearance.

## 10. Rival G — Pure relation/event network / no stable grounds

### Attraction

Avoids reifying categories and can represent multi-role facts flexibly.

### Failure

Without stable problem grounds, research retrieval and semantic discrimination become harder:

- actual state vs representation becomes just edge convention;
- Work vs Actor participation must be reconstructed repeatedly;
- authority vs technical capability lacks protected loci;
- the same relation name can drift across incompatible endpoint meanings.

A relation-only model may later be useful as a representation technique, but it does not replace the need for stable semantic anchors in Foundation research.

### Verdict

**REJECTED as a research-ground replacement.**

## 11. Rival H — Domain / Representation / Pursuit / Agency

```text
Domain / Situated Reality
Representation
Pursuit
Agency / Standing
```

### Attraction

This is almost isomorphic to the current four and potentially uses less overloaded language.

### Analysis

- `Pursuit` captures much of Work's protected semantics but may under-signal maintenance/support/continuing Work in ordinary use;
- `Agency / Standing` improves the non-performing-party issue but may under-signal responsibility/accountability and institutional roles;
- `Domain / Situated Reality` risks under-signaling Work-support conditions outside the target domain;
- `Representation` alone can under-signal signals/data/access and informational dynamics.

### Verdict

**SEMANTICALLY CLOSE, NOT STRUCTURALLY STRONGER.**

This rival is evidence that the main remaining uncertainty is partly naming, not ground count or boundary structure.

## 12. Naming review of the current four

### Environment / Operating Reality

Retain the paired label for now.

`Environment` alone is overloaded; `Operating Reality` helps emphasize actual realized conditions. No tested alternative improves both Domain Reality and Work Conditions coverage without new ambiguity.

### Information / Representation

Retain.

The pair avoids treating all information as true knowledge and preserves mediation/representation semantics.

### Work

Retain.

The research definition is broader than task execution and already covers investigation, decision, governance, maintenance and continuing/supporting Work when organized as pursuits.

### Actors / Agency–Participation–Authority

Retain for now with the explicit warning:

> `Actors` is an umbrella research label, not `active doers only` and not one universal Actor primitive.

Possible future refinement toward `Agency / Participation / Standing` should be reconsidered after relation/control research, when the role of authority, affected parties and standing relations is better composed.

Premature renaming now risks optimizing the label before the cross-ground semantics are explicit.

## 13. Cross-domain rival check

The current frame and rivals were compared against the Q4 holdouts.

### Scientific measurement

Current frame cleanly distinguishes measurand/reality, measurement result, measurement process and operator/roles. State/Process or Action-based rivals re-mix these distinctions.

### Emergency management

Current frame distinguishes incident/resource reality, situational information, objective-directed Work and command/delegation. Goal/Norm peers duplicate relations already distributed across these anchors.

### Patient safety

Current frame preserves actual harm, incident reports, improvement/care Work and clinicians/patients/families. `Parties` is a useful Actors naming pressure but not a separate peer.

### AI risk governance

Current frame can treat NIST's Govern/Map/Measure/Manage as Work/control patterns over Actors, Information and Environment rather than importing a function taxonomy as ground structure.

## 14. Q5 verdict

> **No tested rival top-level decomposition explains the protected post-deepening distinctions with less semantic loss or better level alignment than the current four-ground frame plus explicit typed relation/control/transition fabric.**

The strongest rivals either:

- classify by formal ontology axes (State/Process) and collapse semantic loci;
- promote cross-ground relations such as Intent/Norm/Goal into peer buckets;
- use software/project-specific categories;
- or are near-isomorphic renamings rather than stronger structures.

The current four therefore remain the strongest bounded research-ground map found in this rerun.

## 15. Remaining uncertainty after Q5

The substantive uncertainty has shifted.

It is no longer primarily:

> Are the four top-level grounds wrong?

It is increasingly:

> What typed relations, derived standings, controls and transitions are necessary to compose these four without collapsing their local semantics?

That is a later stage, not permission to begin it automatically.
