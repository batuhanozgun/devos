## 3. Late challenge LC1 — Do not export SDD's worker contract to every delegation route

S39, `dispatching-parallel-agents/SKILL.md`, describes independent domains, focused self-contained assignments, expected summaries, conflict checks and integrated tests. Its frontmatter has broader independent-task wording; most worked examples concern separate failures. The complete inspected file does not contain SDD's express worker/reviewer no-subagent rule. SDD does contain that rule. This is a source-grounded scope difference, not a new empirical failure or proof that all nested delegation is wrong.

Public issue #2202 reports a research fan-out in which one worker allegedly delegated again and returned a promise instead of a report. The author acknowledges that the use was research rather than the skill's usual failing-test examples. The report suggests terminal-role wording and a receipt check.

Important qualifications:

- No full run/transcript, authenticated token accounting, exact loaded commit or independently checked host lifetime was recovered.
- The issue metadata reports one comment, but both the comments tool and direct comments endpoint returned empty collections. The discussion is therefore not claimed as completely recovered. No maintainer agreement is inferred.
- A claim that a child has no later output channel is host-specific; the issue cannot establish it universally.
- Successful payload delivery and adequacy of the delivered work remain separate. Keyword scanning for promises is only a proposed heuristic, not a validated acceptance mechanism.

**Effect on CORE:** retain route-specific role and return responsibilities. Do not summarize Superpowers as universally forbidding recursive agents, and do not equate a returned status message with the promised deliverable. This refines the existing actor/handoff explanation without adding a new subsystem or adopting the proposed fix. The conclusion rests on direct S39/S30 comparison; the report supplies a qualified challenge, not independent causation evidence.

## 4. Late challenge LC2 — Release shorthand can change the apparent guarantee

The first 120 lines of `RELEASE-NOTES.md` were read at the primary pin. This is a deliberately PARTIAL historical read, including the 6.3.0 section and older entries. Its reported experiments, platform claims and performance figures were not newly authenticated.

Two comparisons matter:

1. The 6.3.0 summary describes continued controller execution with only destructive/irreversible operations stopping for a human. Full SDD defines four stop classes, also including security-sensitive actions, normally permission-requiring outside-worktree effects and a plan with no non-guessing route. CORE keeps the full contract, not the shortened release sentence.
2. The release heading broadly describes protection of untracked work during removal. The actual finishing rule is conditioned on Git refusing removal and forbids unprompted force-removal. The original U05 ignored-scratch counterexample remains relevant; no new experiment was run and no old result was erased by the release narrative.

The older entries also describe removal and later restoration of platform support. They are historical author statements, not current vendor availability evidence. The inspected code, original findings and direct main identity remain the basis of the model.

**Effect on CORE:** preserve the object, trigger, scope and exceptions of a guarantee during compression. This is precisely why a source summary or release label cannot replace the underlying contract.
