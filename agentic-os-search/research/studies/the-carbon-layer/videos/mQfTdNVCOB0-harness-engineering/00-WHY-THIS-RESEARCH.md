# Why this research — from harness composition to qualified change

**Date:** 2026-09-12. **Standing:** research rationale and hypotheses, not SOUL architecture.

## The question supplied by the sources

Batu's proposal is that this video and paper may contribute to the bridge between the research library and SOUL. The proposal is worth testing, but is not itself evidence that the bridge has been built.

The trigger sources approach different objects. The video explains the responsibilities around an agent and ends with a feedback-driven evolution loop. Meta-Harness describes an outer procedure that changes task-specific harness code while keeping a base model fixed. This creates a tractable research question: **what must be known, observable, measurable and protected for an operating system around an agent to improve rather than merely change?** See [source map](notes/01-SOURCE-NATIVE-MAP.md) and exact sources in [the ledger](SOURCE-LEDGER.md).

## Why not append another generic harness summary to the older package?

The existing [context–memory–harness package](../../../context-memory-harness-engineering/STATE.md) already distinguishes current working context, persistence/memory lifecycle and runtime/control. Its [cross-layer synthesis](../../../context-memory-harness-engineering/04-CROSS-LAYER-SYNTHESIS.md), especially §§10–17 and 20–21, already asks about authority, source quality, task-conditioned configuration and instruction-versus-enforcement. Those are not new discoveries of this study.

The new object is narrower in one direction and deeper in another: reconstruct the supplied sources, then investigate **how these responsibilities are composed and how an evidenced change travels through proposal, evaluation, selection and possible acceptance**. A fixed conceptual stack, a list of agent features and an optimiser's winning score are three different things.

The package is hybrid because source-specific critique and a bounded mechanism inquiry are both material. It must retain the video and paper's native accounts even when later analysis uses a different decomposition.

## Hypotheses to test, not promises

**H1:** Relevant prior research can become useful input to harness formation/change when its applicability, source status and failure boundaries survive the transfer. Alternative: much of the library may be background understanding rather than executable input.

**H2:** Queryable diagnostic history can support better candidate generation than precompressed feedback in some tasks. Alternatives include proposer strength, extra inference/analysis budget, task-specific priors, baseline quality and selection noise.

**H3:** Harness composition is a plausible operational pressure-test surface for all four Foundation grounds. This does not make a harness equivalent to SOUL or make the video's ten components Foundation nodes.

**H4:** A separate acceptance boundary is necessary when an optimiser may alter a working system. Whether a particular source implements that boundary, and what form it should take for SOUL, are open questions rather than assumptions of implementation.

## Scope that must not disappear

Study both normal operation and change: context/tool/state/control interactions; human and machine roles; authority and effect; failure and recovery; feedback production and interpretation; cost and transfer; component removal as well as addition. A model-capability problem, a wrong objective or a faulty source must remain possible explanations.

Do not turn the research into an exhaustive survey of every agent framework. Admit neighbouring sources only when they resolve a named question or challenge a material claim. Do not require all existing studies to finish before making local progress.

## Meaning of a useful result

The eventual result should tell us what can be reused, for which work and evidence conditions, what does not transfer, what remains unverified, and which new questions SOUL/Development OS must answer. A well-supported rejection or limitation is as useful as a candidate mechanism. The stopping criteria live in [METHOD](METHOD.md); the current next action lives in [STATE](STATE.md).
