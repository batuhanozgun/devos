## 1. The source pair asks two connected questions

The video describes the responsibilities that let an agent carry out work and then proposes changing that arrangement in response to experience. The separate Meta-Harness paper investigates a particular outer search over executable programs around a fixed task-solving model. The relationship is at the video's evolution loop; the paper is neither the video's transcript nor validation of every video responsibility. [Note01](01-SOURCE-NATIVE-MAP.md); [Note12](12-VERIFICATION-OBSERVABILITY-AND-QUALIFIED-EVOLUTION.md); P01 §§1–3.

The paper's native mechanism is proposal, interface validation, task evaluation and retained code/results/traces, with selective filesystem inspection informing subsequent proposals. Its algorithm returns a frontier of candidates. A controlled rollout into an operating organization is a further question, not something the algorithm's returned score settles. Our acceptance/materialization extension below is explicitly analytical. [P01 §3; Notes03–07]

Our combined question is therefore:

**How can a working arrangement produce useful work now, preserve experience that supports a later change, and make the changed arrangement usable without losing the conditions that justified it?**

This is broader than optimizing the contents of a prompt, but narrower than a complete theory of SOUL. It also allows the conclusion that no harness change is warranted.

## 3. Three kinds of change that should not share one success label

Our analytical distinction is about **what is changing**, not a requirement for separate databases or actors.

| Changed object | Example | What a successful update would not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Current work state | Record a completed check, preserve an unresolved question or resume a task | A reusable method has improved. |
| Reusable knowledge or procedure | Save a lesson, example-selection criterion or revised skill | The lesson is applicable, correctly loaded or beneficial on future work. |
| Operating policy or executable harness | Change retrieval, preparation, tool handling or control flow | The candidate is accepted, installed or actually used by the intended consumer. |

An inner memory may accumulate examples while its governing policy remains unchanged. Conversely, the policy can change while the underlying model weights remain fixed. A new file in either case does not prove improved reasoning. [Notes03, 08–12; P01 §§2–3]

Meta-Harness is itself a harness around its proposer in the broad terminology of the paper. That does not show that the evaluated system recursively rewrites its own optimizer, model, evaluator and authorization rules. The study used a particular strong proposer; its discussion leaves broader proposer dependence open. A system capable of proposing a change is not thereby entitled to make every surrounding change. [P01 §3/§5]

## 4. Two loops, joined by evidence and a further acceptance boundary

**Analytical synthesis; not a verbatim architecture from either source:**

```text
An authorized work objective and its constraints
  → select relevant information, methods and usable capabilities
  → act, observe, coordinate and preserve progress
  → judge the result against the actual work
  → retain useful evidence, uncertainty and unfinished responsibilities
                         ↓ when a change is worth investigating
Recover relevant experience and candidate explanations
  → propose a bounded change, including simpler/no-change alternatives
  → evaluate the identified candidate under stated conditions
  → compare and select, or reject/defer
                         ↓ not supplied by selection alone
Resolve acceptance authority and intended scope
  → materialize the accepted change for the actual consumer
  → check what that consumer now receives and can do
  → observe subsequent work, regressions and changed conditions
```

The operating and improvement loops need not run at different clock speeds, in separate products or with separate models. A human may perform diagnosis or acceptance. Some low-risk changes can be preauthorized; some operations need an explicit human decision. The useful distinction is responsibility and evidence, not actor count.

A work failure is not an automatic command to modify the harness. The source may be wrong, the objective inappropriate, the model insufficient, the environment unavailable, or the evaluator defective. Those explanations can require different remedies. Investigation should also consider the cost of making and maintaining the remedy. [Notes02, 12–15]

Selection and acceptance answer different questions even when one process performs both. Selection compares candidates under the experiment's criteria. Acceptance additionally concerns the intended use, constraints, authority and consequences. Materialization then asks whether the accepted artifact actually became the behavior consumed by a worker. GSTACK and HERMES supply bounded cases for these latter transitions, developed in Note17.

## 5. What must survive each handoff?

A useful result must preserve the qualifications needed by its next consumer. These can be short references rather than a single enormous record. Our synthesis identifies six recurring handoffs:

**Source to working context.** Preserve which material, version and scope are relevant, which constraints are binding, and what was omitted or remains uncertain. A faithful quotation is not automatically an authoritative instruction. A valid source can still be inapplicable to the new task. [Note08]

**Request to actual effect.** Preserve the intended target, admitted operation and executor, and distinguish acknowledgment from observation of the postcondition. A timed-out call can leave an uncertain effect rather than a known failure. Recovery should resolve that uncertainty where duplication matters. [Notes09–10]

**Worker to integrating owner.** Preserve the assignment, examined artifact, coverage and unfinished duty. Two accurate reports about different revisions need not support one verdict about the latest patch. A handoff of conversation control does not define every business responsibility. [Notes11–12]

**Outcome to evaluation.** Preserve the checked object, criterion, population, missing outcomes and relevant environment. Different score definitions must not be merged just because their labels or dataset names match. An internal completion message and an external verifier are distinct observations. [Notes13–15]

**Evaluation to improvement decision.** Preserve the comparator, search exposure, resource accounting and adverse results. A better selected candidate is not a repeated demonstration of the optimizer's superiority. A positive average does not identify all users or work types that benefit. [Notes13–15]

**Accepted change to subsequent use.** Preserve the intended scope and actual materialization, consumption and readiness status. A source update or successful skill load is not a measured improvement in future work. A rollback of files also need not reverse every external effect or relationship. [Note17]

These are questions that the evidence must answer when relevant, not a universal schema or a mandate to insert six approval services. For a small task, a revision identifier, exact test result and concise handoff may be sufficient. For consequential external actions, stronger receipts and reconciliation may be necessary.

## 6. The three experiments constrain different parts of the explanation

The earlier audits are not pooled into one improvement percentage or counted as three independent replications.

| Experimental family | Positive contribution retained | Limit that changes reuse |
|---|---|---|
| Classification | A selected context/memory procedure improves the reported mean; the local information-interface comparison favors richer diagnostic access; additional datasets test some transfer. | Task regressions, comparator-dependent context savings and unresolved historical adapters/metrics remain. The selected output, search outcomes and causal explanation are separate. |
| Math retrieval | A query-conditioned policy improves over no retrieval, including models not used during search. | Its comparison with a strong dense baseline is near-tied on the four-model transfer subset; corpus eligibility and actual grading remain load-bearing. |
| Coding | An automatically discovered preparation step is added to a mature inherited agent. | Historical run scopes cannot be stitched together; repeated use of the same benchmark does not establish new-task transfer or isolate the snapshot's causal effect. |

For exact numbers, use Notes13–15 and their original arithmetic inputs rather than this table. U04 performs no new numerical or performance experiment.

Together the cases support a **candidate research direction**: improve how a fixed solver is situated, informed or coordinated rather than assuming model replacement is the only option. They do not establish a universal improvement algorithm, one mandatory representation, or a monotonic learning guarantee. Better models, simpler methods and no change remain legitimate alternatives. Correcting the paper's overcompressed descriptions of MCE, GEPA and ACE in U03-A remains in force; this synthesis does not revive those caricatures.

## 7. A change can also change what the system is able to notice

This is a cross-unit inference, not a new observed incident. A policy that changes selection, logging, context or evaluation can alter both the work and its visible evidence. Higher observed quality can therefore mean better work, a changed observation population, a narrower criterion, or a different resource allocation. The alternatives must be separated before attribution.

For example, a context-saving policy might keep fewer records of failed attempts. A review based only on retained results could look better without any increase in actual task success. Conversely, additional instrumentation might reveal more failures even while the underlying system improves. These are analytical examples, not claims that the paper performed such manipulation. The prior source audits supply narrower concrete population and metric-scope cases. [Notes04, 06, 13–15]

The corrective principle is not to freeze every evaluator forever. An evaluator can be wrong and need revision. But changing the question or the scoring rule creates a new comparison boundary; it should not silently turn the original result into a stronger confirmation. Avoid an infinite chain of validators by identifying the actual claim, protected effect and proportional external or independently constructed check.

Traceability helps investigate causes; it is not causal proof. Likewise, a checker's source code makes its measurement inspectable, not universally correct. The path-tracing task illustrates this: output similarity and execution constraints establish selected observable properties, not every internal algorithmic property described in comments. [Note15]

## 8. One coherent work and learning example

**Synthetic analytical scenario; no workflow was executed.** A worker is authorized to repair a repository test without deploying. The owner supplies the relevant revision, failure and no-deployment constraint. The worker receives or retrieves applicable instructions and a procedure; it explores only the permitted environment and records what it actually observed.

The worker proposes patch C1. A reviewer examines that same revision and returns findings with scope and uncertainty. A test runner checks C1 under the intended conditions. The owner can accept the repair while keeping release authority separate. If interrupted, a later actor can recover both the checked artifact and the remaining work instead of relying on a bare completed label. This connects all ten responsibilities without requiring ten components.

**Adverse branch.** The reviewer checked C0, the patch is C1, and a summary drops that distinction. A green report then supports the wrong artifact. More reviewers using the same deficient handoff need not repair the problem. Recover the actual revisions, preserve the unresolved verdict and obtain the missing check or escalate. Do not infer that a new skill is the only possible fix.

**Improvement branch.** Repeated review of such events may support a candidate method that binds checks to the artifact being accepted. A simpler candidate might only improve the handoff format. Evaluation should include the motivating mismatch and legitimate cases that must remain allowed. Candidate selection retains both detected failures and overhead; neither the incident's recurrence nor one passing replay alone establishes the method's general benefit.

**Delivery branch.** Suppose the chosen rule is stored in a skill. It can still fail to help if the installed rendering is old, the worker receives only an index entry, required tooling is missing or the rule is inapplicable. Check the actual recipient and subsequent work, not merely the updated source file. GSTACK's materialization distinction and HERMES's view/use semantics make this branch concrete without demonstrating the synthetic scenario. [Note17]

**Changed-conditions branch.** If a later tool already binds checks correctly, the added rule may become redundant. If the task now needs a different type of evidence, the old rule can become misleading. Retiring or narrowing a lesson can therefore be improvement too. Reverting the procedure would not undo an already performed external action.

## 9. Bounded U04 conclusion

A compact expression of this synthesis is:

**An operating arrangement enables work; an improvement loop proposes changes from experience; qualified reuse requires the right evidence and authority to survive into what a later worker actually receives and does.**

The new value is the connected account of work, experience, method change, comparison and actual use, with the sources' mismatches intact. It is not a claim that more context, more agents, more rules or more tests always improve outcomes. Nor is every research finding expected to become code or a prompt.
