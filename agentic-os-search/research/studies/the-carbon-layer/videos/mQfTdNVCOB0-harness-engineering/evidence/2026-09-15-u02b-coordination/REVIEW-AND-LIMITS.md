## Reverse challenges actually applied

**Source fidelity:** the full new subtitle text was checked against selected frames, not assumed to be verified speech. The older table discrepancy remains. The final slide's categorical slogan was compared with the qualified subtitle passage and opening context. The companion article's grouping was not substituted for the video structure.

**Control scope:** a post-effect or asynchronous check may observe without preventing; a normal-stop rule does not cover every exit. This is documented scope plus analytical implication, not a demonstrated exploit or conformance test.

**Delegation:** manager/tool and handoff patterns have different conversation-control semantics. Narrow context can omit decisive constraints; broad history can bring noise or stale authority. Neither pattern proves business-work ownership or current permission.

**Procedure:** a shared skill can reduce inconsistent practice while also producing correlated error. A format validator and worker agreement are not independent outcome verification. A simpler single worker or script remains a legitimate alternative.

**Evidence:** tests on the wrong revision and a grader that rejects a valid alternative challenge superficial pass/fail counts. Missing trace data is not automatic proof that an event did not occur. Privacy and observation coverage must be considered together.

**Evolution:** the motivating failure can be explained by model, source, objective, delivery, execution or evaluator problems. A candidate repair can overfit the incident or create regressions. Acceptance and deployment are not granted by a score or a source's compounding metaphor.

**Novelty:** the author already distinguishes main-agent integration, procedure versus proof and observation versus diagnosis. The unit's contribution is grounding and interaction conditions, not claiming those cautions as new discoveries.
